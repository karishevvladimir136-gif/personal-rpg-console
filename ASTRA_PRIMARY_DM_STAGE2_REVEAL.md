## Owner blind preference

The owner preferred **Y (B)** overall, narrowly, approximately **55/45** over X (C). The stated reasons were stronger longitudinal NPC autonomy, more persistent attention to Orin's priorities and fatigue, a more character-specific response to the chosen-one claim, slightly richer relational development in the quiet and callback scenes, and realistic escalation without automatic heroic authority.

The owner also found **X (C)** strong: its prose was materially tighter, several responses were especially concise, and its reciprocal quiet-scene response was particularly natural. This is the owner's qualitative blind judgment, not a score derived from the model ledgers.

## B versus C in the recorded evidence

In retained Stage 1 cells, **B and C each committed 10/10**. Both preserved the map-promise callback, let Orin refuse dangerous solo work, rejected automatic chosen-one authority, and kept the wrong-culprit accusation unproven. B's provider latency was **184.945 s** over 15 requests; C's was **212.453 s** over 13 requests. These different tool choices and responses do not establish a general speed or quality ranking.

In Stage 2, **B (Y) and C (X) each committed 12/14 turns** and used 19 model requests and five server tools. B's measured provider latency was **358.07 s** with estimated spend **US $1.2830**; C's was **403.36 s** and **US $1.4011**. C's committed narration totaled **816 words** versus B's **973**—about **16% fewer**—which supports the owner's tighter-prose observation without scoring prose quality by length.

The blind preference supports **B's Console Memory/state layer as the more appealing long-form DM experience in this specific run**. The extra Mega DM/Cognitive/Character Model packet in C did not show a clear incremental benefit here, despite C's concise writing and strong quiet scene. This is a narrow qualitative result, not proof that C's guidance is generally worse or unnecessary. The Stage 2 contract recorded **zero Character Model updates** in either arm, so evolving Character Models were not evaluated.

## Limits that remain after reveal

**Neither arm left the Copper Lantern in validated structured state.** No public HP, resource, item or location change committed during Stage 2. Mechanical travel, resource pressure, HP/combat continuity and sustained physical consequences were therefore **not adequately tested**, even though the fixed later inputs referred to the mine and magistrate. The run provides stronger evidence about conversational continuity, NPC refusal, rumor boundaries, relational callbacks and chosen-one resistance than about the whole-gameplay state loop.

Four Stage 2 finals failed validation: **B/Y turn 2**, **C/X turn 4**, and **both arms' turn 9**. Their narrated proposals in the blind review are **uncommitted evidence**, not gameplay events, Memory or successful state changes. Both arms continued each fixed next input from their own last validated state. No failed proposal was treated as a committed consequence for this comparison.

The owner's preference and this reveal do **not** authorize a production runtime change, prompt change, migration, owner gameplay change or automatic B/C selection. Any production decision remains separate.
