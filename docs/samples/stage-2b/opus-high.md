## 01-miller / talk

**Player:** I walk down the lane to the village mill and ask the miller whether he has any paid work.

**DM** (first word 7.3 s, turn 30.4 s, 1 request, $0.119):

> The lane drops off the wayside square between hedges of hawthorn. You hear the mill before you see it: a slow, uneven *thunk… thunk* where there ought to be a steady churn. The building sits low against the stream, stone below and weathered planks above. The wheel turns, but lazily, and the water in the race is a hand's width lower than the green line on the boards shows it should be.
>
> The miller is out on the sluice walk with his sleeves rolled, scowling down at the water as if it owes him money. He's broad and greying, his forearms dusted white to the elbow. When you ask about work he looks you over: the travel-worn boots, and the compass if it shows.
>
> "Work. Aye, there's work, if you can walk and you've got eyes." He spits into the race. "Name's Aldo Fenn. Three days now the stream's been dropping. No rain to blame either way. Something upstream's choking it, a fallen tree or some fool damming it for his own fields. I can't leave the stones half-turning with orders waiting, and my boy's down with a fever."
>
> He jerks his chin north, where the stream winds up into alder scrub and rough pasture.
>
> "Follow the water. Find what's stopping it. Come back and tell me true, and don't go starting fights with anyone on my account. Eight silver for the finding, and a loaf for the road now." He hesitates, then adds more quietly, "If it's a person doing it, I'd want to know who before I go shouting."
>
> He's already reaching for the bread on the windowsill.

>

## 03-reward-once / offer

**Player:** I ask the work organiser for paid work. He mentions the miller needs flour hauled — I agree to do it for five coins.

**DM** (first word 6.8 s, turn 25.9 s, 1 request, $0.060):

> The organiser is a broad, unhurried man with chalk dust on his sleeves. He turns from the board and taps a slip of paper pinned near the bottom, the corners curled with damp.
>
> "Garrow Fenn, down at the brook mill. His boy's laid up with a turned ankle, so there's flour sitting in sacks that should've gone up to the waystation storehouse two days ago." He looks you over: your boots, your shoulders, the compass at your belt. "Eight sacks. A handcart's at the mill, but the track climbs and it's rutted. It'll take you most of an afternoon."
>
> You name your price, five coins, and he takes a moment over it, rubbing his thumb along his jaw.
>
> "Five's fair. Garrow pays on delivery, not before. He's careful with coin, so don't take it personally." He pulls the slip off the board and hands it to you. *Fenn — 8 sacks flour — mill to waystation store — 5c on receipt*, it reads in neat block letters. "Show him that so he knows I sent you. Name's Edric Hollis, if anyone asks who vouched for you."
>
> He points past the well, where a footpath drops between hawthorn hedges toward the sound of running water. "Follow the brook downhill. You'll hear the wheel before you see it."

>
> "If you're hoping for bread, you're a day late and so is everyone else," she says, not unkindly. "Name's Odile Varn. I don't hand out loaves. I hand out *work*, and some of it pays in loaves." She taps a fresh notice pinned over the older ones. "Today, all of it does."
>
> The paper is short: *Baker's cart from Ashford Mill: overdue since yesterday noon. Seek driver Tamsin Crale on the moor road. Reward on return.*
>
> "Tamsin's done that run for eleven years. Never been late once." Odile's mouth goes thin. "The moor road forks three times past the standing stone, and the fog's been sitting on it since the day before last. Half the lads I'd send can't tell north from their own boots." Her glance drops to the brass compass at Rowan's belt and stays there a beat. "Six silver and two loaves off the first batch, if you bring her back, or the cart, or word of either. The bread's what the village eats, so you'd be feeding more than yourself."
>
> She waits. She doesn't plead.
