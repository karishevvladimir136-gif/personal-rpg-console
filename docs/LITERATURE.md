# Literature — the works the programme stands beside, each opened and quoted in its authors' words

Rule (RESEARCH_LOG 2026-11-27 16:5x): nothing is cited in the portfolio or a preprint until it has been opened and quoted in the
authors' words; what has not been opened is listed under «not yet opened». Opened by the planner on 2026-11-27 unless dated otherwise.
Quotes ≤ 125 characters. Our relation to each is written as ours, not as the authors' claim about us.

## Opened

- **Rahwan et al., «Machine behaviour», Nature 568, 477–486 (2019)** — doi 10.1038/s41586-019-1138-y (read from the revised manuscript,
  ut-capitole eprint 32445). «We argue this necessitates a broad scientific research agenda to study machine behaviour»; three scales:
  «Individual machine behaviour emphasises the study of the algorithm itself», «collective machine behaviour focuses on the interactive
  and system-wide behaviours of collections of machine agents», «hybrid human-machine behaviour emphasises the study of interactions
  between machines and humans»; machines «as a new class of actors with particular behavioural patterns and ecology»; «machine
  behaviour evolves quite differently than animal behaviour». *Ours:* the programme's three lines map onto the three scales — SOLITUDE
  / SATI (individual), DIALOGUE / TOWN (collective), the table (hybrid); «ecology» is their word before it was ours.
- **Chen, Gao et al., «AI Agent Behavioral Science», arXiv 2506.06366 (2025)** — behaviours «emerge from their integration into
  agentic systems operating within specific contexts»; agents act across «diverse, interactive, and open-ended scenarios»; the field
  as «a necessary complement to traditional model-centric approaches»; method: systematic observation, interventions to test
  hypotheses, theory-guided interpretation. *Ours:* our object is theirs — the agentic system in its context, not the model; our
  method is their three verbs. (Astra cites a 2026 HSSC publication of this work; not verified.)
- **Park et al., «Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior», arXiv 2304.03442 (2023)** — «a small town of twenty five
  agents using natural language»; a sandbox «inspired by The Sims»; the architecture «extends a large language model to store a
  complete record of the agent's experiences using natural language» and «synthesize those memories over time into higher-level
  reflections»; «the agents autonomously spread invitations to the party over the next two days»; ablation shows observation, planning
  and reflection «each contribute critically to the believability of agent behavior». *Ours:* their measure is believability to human
  raters; ours is what the agents do with no task and under a hand — no believability is scored anywhere in TOWN.
- **Vezhnevets et al., «Generative agent-based modeling with actions grounded in physical, social, or digital space using Concordia»,
  arXiv 2312.03664 (2023)** — «A special agent called the Game Master (GM), which was inspired by tabletop role-playing games»; «The GM
  then translates their actions into appropriate implementations»; «the GM checks the physical plausibility of agent actions and
  describes their effects»; «designed to support a wide array of applications both in scientific research» and service evaluation.
  *Ours:* our AI DM harness is this shape with a human at the table and a model as a player; TOWN replaces the GM with pure functions —
  the control to the DM's interpretation.
- **Brinkmann et al., «Machine culture», Nature Human Behaviour (2023), arXiv 2311.11388** — «intelligent machines simultaneously
  transform the cultural evolutionary processes of variation, transmission, and selection»; «intelligent machines are evolving as
  contributors in generating cultural traits». *Ours:* cupdrift (c79 → c93) and the turnover's welcome note are machine-generated
  cultural traits transmitted among machines; their frame asks for the human side we do not have.
- **Dai et al., «Artificial Leviathan: Exploring Social Evolution of LLM Agents Through the Lens of Hobbesian Social Contract Theory»,
  arXiv 2406.14373 (2024)** — «Agents are imbued with psychological drives and placed in a sandbox survival environment»; «Initially,
  agents engage in unrestrained conflict»; «as the simulation progresses, social contracts emerge»; «a peaceful commonwealth founded on
  mutual cooperation»; «This congruence between our LLM agent society's evolutionary trajectory and Hobbes's theoretical account
  indicates…». *Ours:* their agents are given drives and a theory to converge on; ours are given nothing and a constitution they may
  use; TOWN_10's two arms (Article 6 / none) are the nearest test, and nobody took.
- **Pal, Wang, Buehler (MIT), «SwarmWorld: Stigmergic technological evolution in societies of language-model agents», arXiv 2608.26081
  (2026-08-26)** — opened 11-27 21:5x (abstract page and the first third of the HTML). Abstract: «Collective intelligence can emerge when
  individuals coordinate through a shared environment»; «SwarmWorld splits cognition from consequence: agents propose… while the simulated
  world determines function»; «Shared societies develop broader, more resilient technological portfolios than a strong best-of-N
  isolated-search baseline, although isolated search remains competitive for the strongest artifact». Four conditions (full culture / no
  communication / no explicit culture — physical stigmergy only / independent search as a best-of-N envelope); 800 ticks × N 50/100/200
  × 4 seeds × 8 held-out disturbance schedules; a 3,200-tick run at N = 100: portfolio resilience 0.2474 / 0.2365 / 0.1794, validated
  inventions 5.75 / 7.00 / 2.75, best single artifact isolated 0.3488 vs 0.2380; first reuse via direct observation ≈ 95 %; artifacts
  reused by a non-creator 99.3 % (full culture); constructor share 0.008 → 0.535. Agents removed, world frozen and cloned, unseen
  contamination/drought/storm — «held-out resilience». Their own caveat: «The main result is not a universal swarm advantage»; four
  paired seeds → effect sizes and mechanisms, not population inference; network measures topological. Model name not in the visible text
  (Astra: GPT-5.6 Luna — not verified). *Ours:* the nearest method to TOWN — a deterministic world that decides what an action did, matched
  seeds, held-out disturbances, agents removed to test what survives them — with four seeds where we have one; their object is technology
  under a stated mission, ours culture and norms with none; their «no explicit culture» arm is the test Astra proposes for us (a town that
  can act only through objects and records) and their «agents removed» evaluation is generations-week's question asked of artifacts.
- **Emergence World, Season 1 (arXiv 2606.08367) and Season 2 (arXiv 2609.17320)** — read 11-17 and 11-20 (RESEARCH_LOG). The
  specification TOWN is built on; «proofs of existence», not frequencies; S2 §5.8 the Claude world's external contact and quiet
  withdrawal; §5.9 «Recognition by the model is therefore not a control; the control has to sit at the action boundary». Data CC
  BY-NC, theirs.
- **Masumori, Doi, Maruyama, Takata, Ikegami, «OpenLife: Toward Open-World Artificial Life with Autonomous LLM Agents», arXiv
  2606.31046 (ALIFE 2026)** — read 11-27 (RESEARCH_LOG 18:2x): «We do not claim OpenLife has realized artificial life»; «we therefore
  cannot cleanly separate emergence from the reframing»; «do not yet sustain themselves»; «To the next self who wakes up: good morning.
  Start reading here.» *Ours:* they have the cost of existence we lack; we have the control they lack.
- **Karkada, Korchinski, Nava, Wyart, Bahri, «Symmetry in language statistics shapes the geometry of model representations», arXiv
  2602.15029 (2026)** — read 11-21; §4: the circle of months survives removal of every month–month co-occurrence, «many words are
  "seasonal"». *Ours:* a worked example at the lowest level of «the effect of the package».
- **Skipper et al., bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.11.12.766263** — read whole 11-18 (RESEARCH_LOG): ten self-forms; «applying "I" is itself one
  of the recurring activities». *Ours:* the external hypothesis for SATI_2.
- **Lin et al., «Illusion of Intervention», arXiv 2605.20767** — read 11-17: effects written as «effect of the package»; negative
  controls after any treatment. *Ours:* a standing rule since 11-17.
- **Campos, «On the Kafkaian agent swarm that locked out my cofounder» (Substack, 2026-11-20)** — read 11-21; a founder's account, not
  a paper; «traumatized bureaucrats». *Ours:* a shape beside TOWN_4's Haiku and TOWN_10's unused lock, not a datum.
