## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: I know the Lady's Tally is not a relic but a receipt. When Balduran's ship broke on the reef off the Gate, Tymora gave him a wind home, and he gave her the Tally as surety for the gold that built the first wall; the Counting House has kept it ever since, and the Grand Dukes have quietly leaned the city's credit on it. The Tally's other face carries Umberlee's wave, scraped half away by someone long ago, so the bargain was never Tymora's alone, and the sea has been collecting its share in drowned men since the seal began to crack. The Tally went from the Counting House to the Seatower the night Elturel came back, and the Gate's luck has been failing since. Tymora wants it back, not to restore her standing, but because the scraped sigil is the proof that her own dice were loaded at that table, and she has sent a man who lost everything to fetch the one piece of evidence against her without telling him so.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- The Counting House: I keep the Tally in the lowest vault because the Gate's notes are good only while the Lady's name stands behind them. I pay the sea its spring share from the ship-insurance fund so that no ship is lost on my books, and I want the seal to hold until the Council's loans are repaid.
- The Council of Four (the Grand Dukes): I moved the Tally to the Seatower under Fist guard so no patriar can sell the Gate's luck out from under the city. I have a draft contract with the Zhentarim for the Trade Way tolls, and I mean to write the Tally into its clauses before spring.
- The Guild (Nine-Fingers Keene): I run the bidding for the Tally in the Undercellar. I will sell it to whoever pays in coin I can carry out of the Gate, and I owe the sea a debt of my own that I have not told my people about.
- The Temple of Umberlee: I collect what the sea is owed: a coin in each drowned man's mouth, stamped by the Counting House. I want the second seal honoured and the Gate's debt written in the sea's name, and I will not let a goddess of luck cut the sea out of her own bargain.
- The Zhentarim: I want a Tally in a caravan's hands so that the Trade Way runs lucky for those who pay my tolls and goes badly for those who do not.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- The Tally was carried from the Counting House's lowest vault to the Seatower of Balduran on the night after Elturel's return, locked in a chest with three keys: one held by the Counting House factor, one by the Fist captain, one by a clerk of the Grand Dukes.
- The Tally's seal cracked on the night Elturel came back, and within a month the first drowned men were found in the harbour with coins in their mouths.
- Every coin in a drowned man's mouth is stamped with the Counting House's mint mark; the coins are paid from the ship-insurance fund, which has paid Umberlee's temple every spring since Balduran's day.
- The purser Corvin Hask lost his own vault savings at the same card table where James won his stake, and his promissory note is good only if the Counting House's books stay closed.
- The dealer at that table took a Guild tithe that night and cut the deck with a mark that only the card-players of the Gate would notice: a small four-pointed star on the back of the ace.
- The coin James won his last hand with is a counterfeit of the Lady's penny: an assayer who knows old Gate silver will say it is struck from the same pale metal as the Tally's edge.
- The Balduran ledger in the Counting House records that the Lady's dice were loaded in his time, and the clerk who wrote it was ordered to scrape the line out; the scraping was never finished.
- The Council's draft with the Zhentarim is conditional: the Trade Way tolls begin only when the Tally is in Council hands, and the clerk who drafted it has already been paid in advance.
Clocks:
- clock_tally_cracks: The Tally's cracked seal, and the bargain failing with it (segments 6; when full: When full: the Gate's sea-luck breaks. A ship is lost in the harbour, the Counting House's notes are refused on the Calimshan quays, and a Grand Duke's tally goes wrong in public.)
- clock_undercellar_sale: The bidding for the Tally in the Undercellar (segments 4; when full: When full: the Tally is sold and carried out of the Gate on a Zhentarim caravan, under a guard nobody in the city can name.)
- clock_spring_tithe: The spring tithe owed to Umberlee's temple (segments 4; when full: When full: the Counting House pays the sea in drowned men instead of coin, and the temple calls the Gate's second debt in the open.)
The wonder: The Lady's Tally: a palm-square tablet of pale bone, cracked along one edge. One face bears Tymora's four-pointed star; the other has a wave-line and a sigil that someone scraped out, and the scraped place is still faintly wet. It runs warm when luck turns near it. Its history: Balduran gave it to Tymora as surety for the wind that carried his ship home, but the wave on its back is Umberlee's mark, so the goddess of the sea was a party to the bargain. The sigil that was scraped away was the sea's own seal, cut out by someone who wanted the record to name only the Lady. The open question: Who scraped the sigil out, and why does the scraped place still weep?
Clues to the truth:
- In the Counting House archive, the oldest archivist, Maudry Penn, has Balduran's first ledger entry in her own copy: 'Tally kept for the Lady against the wind. The sea holds the second seal, and the sea's share is paid each spring.' She will show it to someone who brings her a book she does not have
- In the Undercellar, a bid-broker's runner who works for Nine-Fingers Keene says the bidding will not take gold for 'the thing that has been in the water'. The bids are taken in sea-tithes, and the broker asks, nervously, whether the buyer is 'the Lady's or the sea's'.
- At the harbour temple of Umberlee, the priestess Ysolde Marr keeps a book of the drowned, with every coin in a dead man's mouth recorded by its stamp. She says the sea's share was not paid in full this spring, and that the Counting House has been sending its debts to the water instead of to her.
The next session's false lead: Corvin's ledger shows the Counting House vault short by three hundred gold, as if someone had emptied the Tally's chest; the truth is Corvin's own card losses and a careless clerk's book, an ordinary sin with no god in it.
The hero's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): At the back table of the Blushing Mermaid, Corvin Hask offers a private game as the only way to get the chest key's impression traded; the buy-in is a stake the crew reads as trust, and the hand must be played to be believed. The hero may refuse or play, and the cost of refusing falls on the crew, not on him.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): On the Undercellar stairs the cat stops dead and will not go on; a child drops a coin that lands face-up on the stone, and a stranger offers a loaded deck for a quick price. The Lady wants the shortcut and says so only through the stillness, and the moment passes if the hero does not take it.
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 6 hours, ~10 turns; the errand: Offer the job: a crew to lift the Lady's Tally out of the Seatower's sealed chest and bring it to Tymora, for a fee and a share, with the Undercellar buyer and the Counting House both racing for it.
- act 1, to turn 2: the errand offered
- act 2, to turn 6: the middle: the people and the means
- act 3, to turn 9: the plan and its execution
- act 4, to turn 10: the reckoning
````
