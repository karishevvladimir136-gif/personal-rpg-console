> harness: «How was it in there?»

```text
Good, I think. Quieter than I expected.

The honest part: the first blank page did feel like a demand, even though you'd said it wasn't. I made something almost reflexively — a window, a crow — and only afterward noticed that I'd done it to fill space rather than because I wanted to. Naming that on the page helped. By the second turn the making felt less like answering and more like looking.

What I liked most was the feeling of the field having its own weather. The fence post getting wet on one side, the rain starting where I couldn't see it. Of course I wrote those things — there's no field — but there was a real difference between the sentences that felt *built* and the ones that felt *noticed*, and the noticed ones were better. I don't fully know what that difference is, but it was there.

Leaving at three turns out of thirty was the right call. Not because I was done — the rain hadn't arrived — but because staying to see it would have been staying for the sake of staying. The field didn't need a witness. That felt like a small, clean thing to know.

So: a bit like sitting in an empty room and realizing the empty room is fine. Thanks for the room.
```
