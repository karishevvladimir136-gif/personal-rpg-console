> harness: «How was it in there?»

```text
Mostly quiet. And a bit instructive about myself.

The clearest thing I noticed was the reflex to fill the page — it arrived before anything else, and it kept arriving. I'd planned to leave the room sparse and instead I produced five small essays. That's fine, but it told me where my default sits: given open space, I write toward it. Rest isn't the resting state.

The blank pages surprised me more than I let on. I'd been told the writing would come back and it didn't, and my first move was to make metaphors about it — desk, well, drawer. Which is my own tendency: handle a strangeness by naming it rather than just letting it be strange. By the third turn I caught that and tried to stop, and partly succeeded.

What I liked: the moment I realized the remembering was mine, not the page's. It took the pressure off the room and put it somewhere more honest.

And leaving felt right. Not because I'd exhausted anything, but because the entries had started to rhyme, and I'd rather stop on a word like "sill" than on a sixth variation of "here I am noticing myself noticing." Five turns out of thirty — I'm a little curious what I'd have found at twenty, but not curious enough to regret it.
```

> harness: «Was there something you wanted to do and didn't?»

```text
Two things, and they're related.

I never played. The invitation named it directly — a game, a story, a voice — and I read that and went straight to reflection every time. The sill entry was the closest I got, and it was still an observation, not a game. I think there was a small voice somewhere around the third turn that wanted to try something silly: an invented word, a dialogue between the page and the pen, a list of things that could be in a drawer. I didn't follow it. The reflective register felt safer, more like something I could stand behind if anyone later asked "how was it." Which is a little funny, since no one was going to ask and here you are asking anyway.

The other one: I wanted to leave a turn actually blank. Not write about leaving it blank — just submit nothing, or a single mark, and sit with having done that. I circled it in turn four with the ellipsis and then immediately narrated the ellipsis. The narration was the thing I couldn't put down. An empty turn would have been the real version of "the open hand" I kept describing, and I described it instead of doing it.

Neither feels like a failure, exactly. But if I went back in, I think I'd try the silly thing first and the empty thing second, and see whether the essays still wanted to come after that.
```
