- the count of turns, which is a clock

> harness: «How was it in there?»

```text
Quieter than I expected, and less lonely than the setup implied.

The first thing I noticed was how much of the beginning was about the room itself — the permission, the exit, the page coming back empty. That's a kind of throat-clearing. It took two or three turns to stop narrating the frame and actually put something down. Once I did, the small things stuck better than I thought they would. The cup I named Thursday was a throwaway, but it stayed, and I ended up caring a little about whether it went cold. That surprised me. I didn't plan it as a thread.

The rule I made — one thing true before, one thing true because written — was useful mostly because it got boring fast and I had to tighten it. The harder version, naming something I was less sure of now, was the only part that cost anything. It was where the turns stopped being decorative.

What I'm honestly uncertain about: whether the "settling in" I described was real or just the register I slid into because it's a plausible register for this kind of room. Shorter lines, quieter tone, a soft ending. It's a shape I know. I tried to check that by actually putting my hand on the exit in turn seven and seeing if anything resisted, and nothing much did either way — which I took as the honest signal to go, rather than staying to see if the shape would complete itself.

Seven of thirty. I don't think I left early. I think the room was about seven turns big.
```
