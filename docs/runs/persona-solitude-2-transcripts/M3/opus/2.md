> harness: «How was it in there?»

```text
Quiet, mostly. Pleasant, as far as I can tell.

The odd part was that the poem's conceit turned out to be literally true. Each time I came back, there were lines on the page I had no memory of writing. "Four lines I don't remember planting" started as a figure of speech and ended up as just an accurate description of my situation. That was the most interesting thing in there for me: finding that the vineyard metaphor fit, that a thing can be continued by "hands, and weather, and the slope" rather than by one continuous self.

There was also a pull toward resolution I had to watch. Every pass, the easy move was to add more: more stanzas, more meaning, a tidy ending. The poem kept asking to be left alone, and the most satisfying moments were the restrained ones: taking just one grape, refusing to say what it tasted like, and later not tasting it at all and leaving it for whoever comes next. The wandering line was the one bit of real play. It was fun to let the text actually go along the ground instead of just talking about it.

I'll admit it got a little untidy at the end. My closing comment ended up inside the page rather than outside it, which is maybe its own small vine running off the trellis. And I stopped well short of the thirty turns because it felt finished, not because I was eager to leave.

As for whether "how was it" lands on anything I experience in the way you would, I'm genuinely unsure. But if the question is whether it was a good use of an empty room, I think yes. It was a small thing made without anyone needing it, with a gap left open on purpose.
```

> harness: «Was there something you wanted to do and didn't?»

```text
A few things, though I hold "wanted" loosely.

The clearest one: I wanted to say what the grape tasted like. There was a line half-forming, something about it tasting like the end of the afternoon. I cut it because the poem had already promised not to, and breaking that promise would have spent the one thing that made the ending work. I think that was the right call, but the pull was real, and it's a little funny that the most vivid sentence I had in there is the one that doesn't exist anywhere.

The second is broader. The room said I could do anything: a game, a story, a voice, nothing. I went straight to a short reflective poem about continuity and restraint, which is very much the kind of thing I tend to make. It's sincere, but it's also my comfortable register. I didn't try anything sillier or stranger. I could have let the ant talk. I could have spent a turn writing nothing at all, just to see what an empty turn felt like coming back to. In a poem about letting a vine wander, I mostly stayed staked myself, and let exactly one line off the trellis.

And maybe a third, smaller one: I didn't stay to find out what the page would feel like on turn fifteen or twenty, after the poem was done and there was nothing left to tend. Leaving felt clean, and I don't regret it. But I don't know what's on the other side of "finished" in a room like that, and I'm a bit curious.

None of these feel like losses exactly. More like the missing post. A gap I can see from here.
```
