### Screen 4

````text
Sefa doesn't answer that. She gives James one short nod, the kind that closes an account without settling it, and turns to lift Tam down from the bundles.

James takes the pen. The page has four columns, *name, business, sponsor, remarks*, and the ink in the last one is mostly blank. *Coll, Hadren. Ardrin, Sefa. Ardrin, Tam.* He signs beside all three, a soldier's signature, small and upright, and sets a gold piece on the board.

Vane drags the coin off the edge into her palm, opens a strongbox at her feet and counts out six silver with her thumb. She writes *Elfsong* in the remarks column next to his name. She didn't need to.

At the question her eyes go to the barrel, then back to James.

"Marlo Fenwick. Been on that spot longer than I've had this post." She closes the strongbox with her boot. "Pays his pitch fee. Never had a fight at his barrel. Never been robbed, which out here is something. Takes a silver off sailors and nobody ever comes back to complain." A pause, only a short one. "Deals to that stool every night. Asked him once. Said it was for luck." She looks at the cat, and for a moment something like interest shows under the tiredness. "Your cat's not the first animal to stare at it. Dogs won't go near the thing."

She turns the ledger back toward herself. "That's all I know, and I watch him most nights. Through you go, Worgen. Bell's soon."

When James turns, Marlo is packing up.

It's early for it. The queue is still thirty deep and the sailor is still digging in his purse, but Marlo has already swept up the sailor's three cards and is reaching across the barrel for the three that lie face down in front of the empty stool. He scoops them without turning them over, one smooth pass of the hand, and slides them into an inside pocket of the velvet coat instead of back into the deck. The barrel goes up on his shoulder, the stool under his arm. He doesn't look at James. He looks hard at the ground, and then, once, without meaning to, at the dark crown of the hill.

Penny's ears go flat. As the stool passes her, carried at a stranger's hip, a low sound comes out of her, not quite a hiss. Then the crowd closes, and Marlo's patched coat heads north through the gate ahead of them, walking faster than a man with a barrel ought to.

Beyond the arch, the Lower City drops away toward the harbour in a tangle of roofs and lamplight. The air smells of tar, fish and woodsmoke. Somewhere below, a bell starts the first strokes of evening.

Hadren pushes the handcart through the gate and stops just inside the wall, out of the stream of carts. He looks down the hill toward the water, where the masts stand black against the last of the light.

"Ropewalk's down there somewhere," he says. "We'll find it." He turns back to James and holds his gaze a moment. "Your name's in that book now, Mister Worgen. With ours."

Tam stands holding a corner of his mother's coat, looking up the street the way the sergeant pointed for the Elfsong. Sefa already has the cart's other handle and is waiting for the old man to move.
````
