````text
"Tymora's house. You going there to pray, or to collect?"
````

````text
Marsh's thumb stops. She looks down at her own glove as if it belonged to somebody else, then back up at you. Her face doesn't change, but the line behind her has gone quiet, and two Fist privates by the bench are listening without seeming to.

It's the way you said it: no pity in it, no sly angle, just a soldier offering a hand to someone else in a uniform. Something in Marsh decides. She turns half away from the privates, so her shoulder blocks their view, and works two fingers into the cuff of her gauntlet.

"Debts. Right." She holds it out low, between your bodies, flat on the palm of her glove. "Then tell me what this is."

It's a copper, worn smooth around the rim and darkened from the water. Every dockside copper in the Gate is meant to carry Umberlee's wave on one face. This one carries an antler instead: one branched tine, struck sharp, as if the die were new. The other face has been rubbed nearly blank by a thumb that has gone over it for weeks.

Penny gets up and sniffs once at the glove. Her ears go flat.

"Came out of a dead man's mouth," Marsh says. "Pulled him off the Grey Harbor steps last month. Water in the lungs, no marks, purse still on his belt. Coin under the tongue. Fist calls it an offering to the sea and closes the file." Her jaw works. "Third one this season. Same coin every time. Nobody's asking why the sea's taking payment with an antler on it."

She closes her hand before you can take it and tucks the copper back into the cuff. Her thumb goes straight back to it.

"You want to be useful. Fine." She looks past you at the line of newcomers, then back at you. "You're off a boat. Nobody here knows your face yet. That's worth something." A beat. "Don't make me sorry I showed you that."

Behind you, at the crate, the dealer in the green coat has stopped shuffling. He's looking at the two of you over his cards with mild, friendly interest, the way a man watches a dice game he hasn't bet on yet.

 Looking back, your words were what tipped it. You talked a wary Fist sergeant into trusting a stranger off a boat with the thing she guards in her glove, and she showed you the antler copper because you asked plainly and she believed you.
````

**Player** (note):

````text
The strongest part was Marsh. Her worn gauntlet, the unconscious movement of her thumb, and her decision to show me the coin made her feel like a person rather than a quest dispenser. My choice to offer help plainly mattered. That’s a good opening for James: an old soldier recognising something troubling another soldier.

The harbour also worked well. The empty stool at the card table put James’s weakness in front of him immediately without forcing him to indulge it. Penny noticing the glove was a nice, understated use of the familiar. I had several things I could pursue, but a clear person to talk to first.

What broke was mostly mechanical and presentational:
- The Persuasion roll used +4 instead of +7. You caught and acknowledged it, which helped.
- The records contradict themselves: Penny has 2 HP in her familiar statistics and 8 HP in the party list. Healing Light’s spending limit also differs from the campaign sheet.
- The full character sheet after almost every exchange overwhelms a fairly short scene. Changes and current essentials would be much easier to read.
- The roll required an extra “Onward” after revealing the result, then repeated the setup before continuing. That took the momentum out of Marsh deciding whether to trust me.

The final explanation that my plain speaking earned her trust was unnecessary—the scene had already shown that convincingly.

What was missing was time to follow through. We finished just as the first real lead appeared, so I can’t yet judge investigation, danger, or the promised crew-and-heist campaign. I don’t need Tymora’s whole assignment immediately, but I’d like an early indication that James’s particular summons matters alongside the harbour murders.

Overall, I wanted to keep playing. Keep the specific NPC behaviour and room for player initiative; trim the repeated records and roll ceremony.
````
