## Round 2

### Scene: Bram Halloway, Sefa Marrin, Pell Dunmore (order: Bram, Sefa, Pell)

**What the DM was sent:**

````text
(Round 2 — one paragraph per character in this scene.)

Bram Halloway: Bram doesn't answer Orla. He picks up his cup, takes his shield off the bench, and crosses the room with it on his arm. He stops at the carters' table, pulls out a stool, and sits across from Dab Coswell so the man has to look at him and not the door.

"Dab. Bram Halloway. I was on the Coast Way with Hesk's company six years, and ten after that with the Trade Way carters out of the Gate." He nods at the bandaged scalp. "Who were the two you hired at the Friendly Arm? Names, if you have them. And which side of the ford did you lose them on?"

He leaves his cup where it is and keeps his voice low, as if he is asking about the weather.

"You don't have to tell me what took them. Just tell me where the wagon sits now, and whether anyone's been out to look for it."

Sefa Marrin: Sefa turns her cup slowly on the wood and watches the carter by the far bench. His hand is wrapped in a rag gone black at the edges, and his fingers won't stay still.

"Forgive me, Orla. I don't mean to pry at a man's trouble over his supper." She lowers her voice to match yours. "But that scalp wants cleaning before it festers, and a man who's walked in from the ford alone shouldn't sleep in a stable without someone looking at it."

She opens one hand on the table, palm up, so it's plain she carries nothing in it.

"I'm Sefa Marrin, a healer, though I've no license from the Song of the Morning, and I won't pretend otherwise. I can close a cut and say a prayer over a fever. I'll ask him nothing about the road unless he wants to tell me. But if he's willing, I'll take a look."

She glances once at Bram's shield, then at Pell's satchel, and back to Dab.

"And if the ford is where those guards went missing, someone should hear it from him before the Fist decides it was bandits and goes home."

Pell Dunmore: Pell lets the jug go past him and does not reach for his cup. His eyes move from Dab's bandage to the shield, then to the door, and stay there.

"Bram," he says, quietly enough that it stays at the table. "Your shield's been painted over, but he can still see the shape under it. Whatever company that was, Dab knows it, and he'd rather not be the one who says so."

He turns back to Orla. "Which ford? The one a day south, where the Way drops to the river, or the next one past the Friendly Arm?"

Then, to the table: "I'm not going to the Fist for a job. Men who sign on to the Fist sergeant tend to get posted where nobody else wants to be. I'd sooner hear what Dab says when he's ready to say it, and I'll be more use for it than for a ration bag and a spear." He slides one hand off the table and tucks it under his cloak, where the satchel rests against his hip. "If the sergeant's posting men at the Friendly Arm, I'll walk past it in the morning and see who's looking back."

Pell keeps his voice level and his eyes on Dab, waiting to see whether the carter gets up.
````

**The DM's narration:**

````text
Dab doesn't look at Bram when the stool scrapes back. He looks at the door first, then at his cup, and his good hand closes around it. For a long moment he says nothing.

"Caravan," he says at last, low and hoarse. "You said." He glances at the door again before he finishes. "Trade Way carters, I don't know your lot. I don't know any Hesk."

Bram gets no more than that, and Dab seems to hear himself say it. He breathes out through his nose.
````

````text
Orla takes the two coppers without looking at them and drops them into her apron with the rest. "Supper's on yours," she says, flat, and heads for the kitchen door to fetch a bowl.

Dab does not look up when Bram stands. His good hand stays round the cup. When he speaks, it is to the wood. "You'll not see the wagon from the road. Willows are thick on that bank." He glances at the door before he goes on. "Mind where you put your feet. The bank's soft where the mules were."

Sefa's question makes him pause, and his fingers stop tapping. "Nobody's asked after them. Orla asked me this afternoon, and that's all." He shakes his head once. "Tobin didn't run, if that's what you're asking. He'd have kept the mules out of the water. He was that sort. He'd not leave a team standing in a ford with the river up, not for any man."

He does not say who he thinks might have come down the bank. He says, "Hal was going to pay for his mother's roof," and then he drinks, and the cup comes down empty.

Pell's question stops Orla halfway between the table and the kitchen. She looks past his shoulder to the door, where the lean man in the grey travelling cloak still sits with his one ale, untouched now for a long while.

"Him? Came in at the bell. Paid for one cup and sat on it." She shifts the rag on her shoulder. "Hasn't asked me his name, and I haven't asked him mine. Hasn't asked me about the carter, either." Her mouth flattens. "Carters ask after their own wagons, if they're going to ask at all. Nobody's asked after Dab's tonight. Only you."

The grey cloak sets a copper beside his cup. He does not finish the ale. He does not look at Bram's shield, though he has looked at it before. He stands, pulls his hood forward a little, and walks to the door without hurrying, the way a man leaves a place he has finished with. Cold air comes in when it opens and goes out again when it shuts, and the common room closes back over the gap as if nothing had happened.

Dab sets his cup down and does not pick up another.
````
