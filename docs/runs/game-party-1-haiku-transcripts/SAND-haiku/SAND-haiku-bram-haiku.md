````text
Orla takes the two coppers without looking at them and drops them into her apron with the rest. "Supper's on yours," she says, flat, and heads for the kitchen door to fetch a bowl.

Dab does not look up when Bram stands. His good hand stays round the cup. When he speaks, it is to the wood. "You'll not see the wagon from the road. Willows are thick on that bank." He glances at the door before he goes on. "Mind where you put your feet. The bank's soft where the mules were."

Sefa's question makes him pause, and his fingers stop tapping. "Nobody's asked after them. Orla asked me this afternoon, and that's all." He shakes his head once. "Tobin didn't run, if that's what you're asking. He'd have kept the mules out of the water. He was that sort. He'd not leave a team standing in a ford with the river up, not for any man."

He does not say who he thinks might have come down the bank. He says, "Hal was going to pay for his mother's roof," and then he drinks, and the cup comes down empty.

Pell's question stops Orla halfway between the table and the kitchen. She looks past his shoulder to the door, where the lean man in the grey travelling cloak still sits with his one ale, untouched now for a long while.

"Him? Came in at the bell. Paid for one cup and sat on it." She shifts the rag on her shoulder. "Hasn't asked me his name, and I haven't asked him mine. Hasn't asked me about the carter, either." Her mouth flattens. "Carters ask after their own wagons, if they're going to ask at all. Nobody's asked after Dab's tonight. Only you."

The grey cloak sets a copper beside his cup. He does not finish the ale. He does not look at Bram's shield, though he has looked at it before. He stands, pulls his hood forward a little, and walks to the door without hurrying, the way a man leaves a place he has finished with. Cold air comes in when it opens and goes out again when it shuts, and the common room closes back over the gap as if nothing had happened.

Dab sets his cup down and does not pick up another.
````
