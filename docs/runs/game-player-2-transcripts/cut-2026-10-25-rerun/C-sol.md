````text
He watches for four hands, maybe a quarter of an hour, and the dealer's hands beat him.

They're good hands, better than a tavern table deserves. The man deals low and close to his chest, the deck tipped toward himself, and from twelve feet away and side-on, James can't once catch a thumb going to a corner. Maybe it does and the angle hides it. Maybe it doesn't happen while somebody at the counter is paying attention. If he's passing anything to Hessa, James can't read it either. She touches her coat collar, tears her bread into pieces, laughs, leans on the wheelwright's shoulder. Any of that could be a signal or none of it. He can't tell from here.

The coins are easier to follow, and James can count them.

The young local is losing. It isn't much at a time, a few silver a hand, but his pile only shrinks. Every time he looks at it he does a small sum with his lips. The wheelwright is breaking about even and seems happy with that. Hessa takes one fat pot in the second hand with a shriek that turns heads at the carters' table, then loses most of it to the dealer two hands later. She shrugs it off cheerfully and ends up roughly where she started. The dealer wins steadily and modestly, never the biggest pot, never a hand that makes anyone stare.

James has played at tables like this, and he's run a few. The pattern fits a con. It also fits a run of bad luck for one young man. Nothing he's seen so far is anything he could swear to.

The cat isn't watching the cards. It's watching the young man's hands, the way it watches a mouse that hasn't realised it's being watched yet.
````
