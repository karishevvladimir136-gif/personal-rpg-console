## The opening

> The cart track bends one last time, and Whitestone opens up before you: a white wound in the hillside, terraces of chalky stone glaring in the late-morning sun, dust drifting across a yard of spoil heaps and sheds. Pell Dunmore, Bram Halloway and Sefa Marrin have covered half a day of road since before dawn, and somewhere behind them, half a day back, Maela Thorn's shop waits for a sealed case that must reach it before sunset.
> 
> But the quarry does not sound like a quarry. No ring of picks, no shouting from the faces, no creak from the lifting frame. Hammers and wedges lie scattered near the lower cut as if their owners simply set them down and walked away. A stone cart stands half-loaded, its traces empty, no mule in sight. Only the chimney of the low slate-roofed house shows life — a thin grey thread of smoke.
> 
> In the doorway of that house stands a heavy-set man in a dust-whitened leather apron, a ring of keys at his belt. He has already seen you. He does not wave and he does not come forward; he just stands there, arms folded, waiting to see what you do next.

**Pell Dunmore** (action):

> Pell slows a step behind the others and lets his eyes do the walking: the scattered tools, the empty traces on the cart, the sheds, the upper terraces, any spot where a man with a bow could lie flat. > 
> Quietly, out of the side of his mouth, to Bram and Sefa:

> 
> He keeps his hands loose and well away from his shortsword and lifts one in an easy greeting toward the man in the doorway. He stays a pace back and off to Bram's side, where he can see past the man into the house.

> "Morning! We're sent by Maela Thorn from Beregost, come for her case. Quiet day for it, eh? Where's your crew got to?"

**Bram Halloway** (action):

> “Quiet for a working quarry,” I murmur to Pell and Sefa.
>

> 
> “Morning. Bram Halloway. Maela Thorn sent us for a sealed case of dye-salts. Are you the warden?” I glance back across the silent yard. “Everyone all right here?”

> Sefa slows as they come to the edge of the yard, eyes moving over the scattered tools, the half-loaded cart, the still lifting frame. > 
> She glances past him, openly, at the table and the papers, then back. "You've kept her case safe a week. You stayed when your lads walked. I see no reason not to help you get what you're owed—but the box needs to be on the road before long, or none of us are paid, and your lads go another week hungry either way. May I come in and look at what's on that table?"

**Pell Dunmore** (action):

> Pell lets his hand drop and steps up level with Bram. He keeps his voice easy, one working man to another.
> 
> "Fair question. Honest answer is we don't know. We're hired legs, Master Hale. Thorn pays us on delivery, by sunset, or not at all. Nobody showed us her ledger." He nods past him at the iron-strapped box on the shelf, then looks back at Hale's face. "But I know the shape of this. Somebody up in the Gate is getting paid for your stone and your salts, and the coin's stopping in his purse instead of rolling down the hill. Three weeks is long enough that it's not a late courier."
> 
> He jerks his chin toward the lower cut, at the three hammers standing on their heads on the block.
> 
> "Your lads left those for somebody to read. A strike mark? Or a message for whoever comes up from the Gate?"
> 
> Then he gets practical:
> 
> "Who's the owner? Who's supposed to send your pay, and who carries it? Give us names. Write it on that slate if you like, with what you're owed. Thorn's a merchant. She'll know who she settled with, and she'll want to know her money went astray, because a quarry with no crew sells her no salts next season. We put your note in her hand alongside the case, today. That's a better road to your coin than keeping the box on a shelf. Keep it there and nobody's paid, you included."
> 
> He keeps his eyes on Hale while he says it. Is the man after his wages, or after someone in particular?
