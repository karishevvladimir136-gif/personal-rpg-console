Bram Halloway

Claude Haiku 5.5 (the dry run's player)
player r5
Pell spoke to Osric of how gold moves in the Gate, of agents and clerks, and asked whether anyone from the house had come out in three weeks. He offered to carry the warden's word to Maela Thorn or to hear his price, his hand drifting toward his belt. Sefa read the letter twice, named Edda Pyle and Vandarren's seal, and said it names no buyer, cart or owed wages. She asked Osric quietly about the sweetness on the wind. Osric answers Pell first, flat, without looking away from the shed corner: nobody from the Gate or the house, nobody up the track in three weeks but the carter, and the rain once. He is still speaking when the flat ground under Pell's boot gives. A skin of white dust over a crust of rotten board and chalk folds with a sound like a dropped plank, and Pell drops out of sight. You see the whole of it from the tailboard: the ragged hole between you and the sheds, the rope coiled at your belt, and an old beam at the lip where the workings have collapsed, half in the dust and within reach of two pairs of hands. Osric stops at the rim, looking down at Pell, then at the broken edge under his own boots. He looks at the hole a moment longer before answering Sefa, his voice not raised. The sheds held stone, lime for the dye-men when the Gate asked, and the white crust off the low walls when the Gate paid for it. The sweetness comes up from the lower cut, not the sheds. To you, about the tally, he says it is written, every wage every week, in his own book in the house: Ansel Pruitt, thirty-one gold, four weeks owing. He keeps his eyes on the hole. Ask in Beregost by the smithies; Ansel drinks where the carters drink. Pell's voice comes up out of the working, tight with cold. He is in, his leg is under a block, the water is to his ribs and cold. Then, lower, he tells you not to come down the lip. Where you are: in the yard near the cart, by the hole's edge · Time: Day 1 · morning
wrote
Bram doesn't answer Osric. He drops to one knee at the cart, well back from the rim, and reads the lip with his eyes before he trusts it. The rotten boards at the edge tilt toward the hole, and the beam is pinned by its own weight, not by anything sound beneath it. "Pell, hold still and keep your breath easy. I'm coming for you with a rope, not a plank." He tells Sefa, low and fast, "Sefa, keep Osric in front of you and stay on the dust. If he moves toward the sheds, shout." He uncoils the hempen rope from his belt and ties a bowline round the cart's rear axle, then runs the line to the tailboard and checks the knot with a hard tug, both hands and his full weight. It holds. He doesn't touch the beam. He sets the rope's far end down the slope, away from the rim, lets it play out a few feet, and watches the dust settle over the broken boards. "Pell, can you reach a line if I throw it, or is your arm pinned?" Then he looks up at Osric, flat and steady. "Warden, you know this pit better than I do. Which beam is holding the face above him, and which one's only sitting on it? Tell me straight, and I'll go down on the good one. You're going to help me get him out of there, because whoever's pocket that gold went into, Pell didn't put his hand in it."

Sefa does not go to the rim. She drops to one knee on the firm dust well back from the broken edge and calls down into the hole, low and steady. "Pell, don't move your leg yet. Can you feel whether the block is pinning you, or only lying on it? Tell me what your hands are touching." To Bram, without looking round: "Rope to the beam, not to me. Tie it off on something that won't move, and keep your weight behind it. If Pell's pinned, I want to be able to pull him sideways, not straight up." Then to Osric, sharp and clear: "Master Hale, you said the sweetness comes up from the lower cut. How do we reach the lower cut without the lip? Is there a ramp, a ladder, a way round by the spoil?" She takes the stoppered vial from her belt-pouch? No. She has none. Instead she draws the prayer beads through her fingers once, sets her other hand flat on the chalk at the edge of the dust, and holds it there, eyes on the black water in the hole. "Pell, I'm going to keep my hands on the ground and my head clear. If that water starts to rise, tell me before you tell anyone else."

Osric answers Pell first, flat and without looking away from the shed corner. "Nobody from the Gate. Nobody from the house. Osric is the first to move toward it, and he stops at the rim, looking down at Pell, and then at the broken edge under his own boots.
