She looks at the open hands, then at the purse Sefa hasn't touched, and says nothing yet. Pell comes up the yard from the staves at the same easy walk and stops just outside arm's reach. He has his purse out and open, so the coin shows. Wenna's eyes go to it and stay there a moment. Bram doesn't wait for her answer. He stands still a moment with his jaw set, then counts three gold and four silver out of his own purse into his palm and holds it out to her. He gives her Vell's folded order with the other hand. She takes the coin first. She weighs it in her hand, counts it with her thumb, and only then takes the paper. She looks at the red wax and the key between the letters, and hands the order back to him unopened. "That's it paid." She drops the coins into her apron pocket and turns to Pell. "Put that away. I don't get paid twice for one room." She goes back into the kitchen. When she comes out she has a little rent book with a greasy cover and a stub of pencil. She writes slowly, licking the pencil, tears the leaf out and gives it to Bram: *Recd of Bram Halloway three gold four silver rent owing Jassan Ferr middle room paid in full. W. Pike.* The letters are big and careful, the hand of someone who learned to write late. Bram nods at the gallery, and she goes up ahead of them with one hand on the rail. The stair creaks under Bram's chain mail. At the top she stops by the middle door with her back to it, the key on its cord in her fist. Now that she's been paid, the answers come. "Big through the shoulders. Quiet. Talks like the Gate." She gives Pell a short look. "Like you. Said Thale sent him. He came round the day after they carried Jassan down, and again yesterday. The rent, and two gold over for my trouble, and he takes the lot. Box, books, bedding, all of it." She folds her arms. "I didn't say yes. Vell's a moneylender. I wasn't having him on my step saying I'd sold his box out from under him. He said he'd come back this afternoon and ask me again." The padlock is hers. "Mine. Got it off Garth at the chandler's the same morning. A dead man's door, people start getting ideas." Nobody has been in since the bearers took Jassan down, she says, only her. "Once. To strip the bed and open the window for the smell. Then I shut it again." When she says *once*, her hands stop on the apron and stay there, flat, the way they didn't for anything else. Pell has watched people hold things back for a living, and Sefa has watched them do it at sickbeds. Both of them see it: she has told them the truth and left a piece out. Neither of them can tell which piece. From the gallery you can see straight across the lane, over the chandler's awning. The boy is still sweeping. Nobody is leaning on the awning post now. Up the Coast Way toward the town an apple core lies in the gutter, and beyond it a pair of broad shoulders is walking away, no faster than anyone else in the street, until a cart turns in between and hides them. Wenna turns her back on the street and puts the key into the bright padlock. She doesn't turn it. "Paid's paid. Room's yours to clear by sundown. I open it when you say, and I stand right here while you're in."
