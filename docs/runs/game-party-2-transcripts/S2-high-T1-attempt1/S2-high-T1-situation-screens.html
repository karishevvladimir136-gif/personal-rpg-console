"Two gold and two silver," Wenna says. "The ragman gave me one gold two for the coat. That's what's left. Not a copper more, and I'll not take less." She says it to Pell, because Pell is the one holding the money. Then she looks at Sefa and at Bram. "See it first, then. That's fair. It's there. Nobody's been in." She answers the rest in the order it came, flat, as if she were reading it off a list. "No name. He said he came from Master Thale's. He came for Mister Ferr, knocked on his door, not mine. Mister Ferr wouldn't open to him. I heard him say so through the wood. 'Not today.' Yesterday the man came back, and that time it was me he wanted." Behind Bram, Tobin's mallet stops for half a stroke and then starts again. He says nothing. "The door's got its own lock," Wenna goes on, to Pell. "Mister Ferr's key was on his table when I found him. I took it. I've had both keys since Thursday, and I locked it myself when the brothers had gone. Rudd's lock went on the next day, after the man in the dun coat first asked what was left." She pulls her shawl tighter. "A lock's a lock. Two's better when somebody's asking." To Pell's request for a line in writing, she gives a short dry sound that isn't quite a laugh. "I make my mark. Mister Ferr wrote my rent book for me. You write it and I'll put my mark under." She turns and climbs back up past the basket of washing. On the gallery she takes a cord from inside her collar with two keys on it, one iron and one bright. The bright one opens Rudd's padlock, and she lifts it out of the hasp. The iron one turns stiffly in the door's own lock. She pushes the door open with two fingers and doesn't go in. She stands to one side of it with the keys in her fist and looks down at the yard. The room has been shut up for three days, and its air comes out onto the gallery stale. It smells of cold lamp-oil and old paper, with something faintly bitter under it, like a crushed green stalk. From the yard the three of them can see only a slice of it in the daylight: bare boards, the leg of a chair, and the dark edge of a table. Across the road, the cord has stopped moving.
