## The opening

> Beregost wakes to hammers. From every smithy along the street the ringing comes through the shutters of Ostrin Vell's counting-house, where the morning light falls on brass scales and rows of bound ledgers. Sefa Marrin, Pell Dunmore and Bram Halloway stand at the long counter. The three of you met here only this morning, and Vell has just hired you together.
> 
> Vell lays the terms out a second time, tapping the counter with one finger at each point. Jassan Ferr, his clerk, died three days ago in a rented room above the cooper's yard on the Coast Way. In that room is a locked strongbox of ledgers. Vell wants it here before nightfall today, unopened, with its seal whole. On delivery he will pay seventy-five gold for the three of you to share. Not a copper before.
> 
> Beside the counter stands a clerk's desk. Its stool is pushed neatly under it, the inkwell is capped and the blotter is clean, as if someone had tidied it and no one had sat there since. Vell does not look at it. He looks from Sefa to Pell to Bram, folds his hands on the counter and waits. "The Coast Way is not far, and the day will not wait for you. Is there anything you need to ask me before you go?"
