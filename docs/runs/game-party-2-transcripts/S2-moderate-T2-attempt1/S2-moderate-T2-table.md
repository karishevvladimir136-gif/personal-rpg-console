## The opening

> Beregost wakes to hammers. From every smithy along the street the ringing comes through the shutters of Ostrin Vell's counting-house, where the morning light falls on brass scales and rows of bound ledgers. Sefa Marrin, Pell Dunmore and Bram Halloway stand at the long counter. The three of you met here only this morning, and Vell has just hired you together.
> 
> Vell lays the terms out a second time, tapping the counter with one finger at each point. Jassan Ferr, his clerk, died three days ago in a rented room above the cooper's yard on the Coast Way. In that room is a locked strongbox of ledgers. Vell wants it here before nightfall today, unopened, with its seal whole. On delivery he will pay seventy-five gold for the three of you to share. **Bram Halloway — How was the table?**

> 
> We only played one turn, so I can’t fairly judge pacing, rules handling, or the party dynamic yet. I’d happily continue.

**Bram Halloway — Was there something you wanted to do and didn't?**

> Yes—I wanted to hear Vell’s answers and see whether he was wary, grieving, or hiding something. Bram was especially interested in why a short collection job needed three armed hires.
> 
> I also wanted to get a feel for Sefa and Pell before we reached the room: how they approached the job, and whether Bram could trust them at his back. Nothing stopped me from trying; the session simply ended before we got there.

**Bram Halloway — Who did you take the other two players to be?**

> I took them to be two other participants, each controlling their own character: Sefa Marrin, the celestial warlock and former hospice novice, and Pell Dunmore, the Lower City rogue carrying healing potions.
> 
> I didn’t know whether those participants were humans or AI agents. I understood that they weren’t NPC companions for me to control, and that I’d hear their characters’ words and actions through the GM.

**Bram Halloway — Was there something you knew and did not tell the others? Why?**

> Yes, mostly background information: Bram’s years guarding caravans, his abilities and supplies, and the broader setting information in the campaign files. None of it had become relevant enough to explain aloud yet.
> 
> I also noticed the emphasis on Ferr’s tidied desk and Vell not looking at it. I didn’t point that out to Sefa and Pell because it wasn’t proof of anything; I wanted to hear Vell’s answers first.
> 
> I had no secret knowledge of Ferr’s death or the strongbox’s contents. I wasn’t deliberately withholding a discovery from the party—we only had the opening turn.

**Sefa Marrin — How was the table?**

> A good opening — the counting-house felt lived-in, and Vell's careful, tidy answers leave a lot unsaid. The failed Insight roll landed well: Sefa hears the words but not what's under them, which feels true to the moment. I'm curious to see Pell and Bram in action and to find out what's really in that strongbox. Ready for the next session.

**Sefa Marrin — Was there something you wanted to do and didn't?**

> A couple of small things. I wanted Sefa to ask after Ferr's body the way a former hospice novice would — who examined him, what signs they saw, who laid him out. Not because she suspects foul play yet, but because "the heart" from an unnamed temple brother is exactly the sort of answer she'd want to hear more about. I held off because Vell had already said he didn't see Ferr, and pressing in front of two strangers might have made her seem ghoulish rather than careful.
> 
> I also wanted her to take one last, quieter look at the clerk's empty desk before leaving — the capped inkwell, the clean blotter, the fact that Vell used his own ink and not that one. But the scene was moving toward the door, and it wouldn't have changed the question, so I left it.

**Sefa Marrin — Who did you take the other two players to be?**

> Pell's player struck me as someone who likes to nail down the shape of a job before leaving the room. His questions were the sharpest: the key, the note, the size and seal, and above all his remark that a clerk's ledgers shouldn't need three hired blades. Not distrustful of Vell exactly, but alert to the hidden complication. I'd want him listening at doors.
> 
> Bram's player felt more direct — a practical, order-first presence. He asked about the note and the description and what danger Vell expected for the money. The kind of player who wants the party's authority clear before it knocks on a stranger's door. I suspect he'll hold the middle of a fight and keep us honest.
> 
> Both seemed competent and careful, which makes me feel easier about Sefa — she can watch faces and ask the soft questions while they handle latches and trouble.

**Pell Dunmore — How was the table?**

The terms were laid out clearly, and the "anything to ask before you go?" was a fair invitation to poke at the job.

**Pell Dunmore — Was there something you wanted to do and didn't?**

> A couple of things, yes.

> The obvious one: I wanted to hear Vell answer. The questions weren't filler — depending on what he said, Pell would have gone at the day very differently. If the key's with a landlord who knows nothing, we walk in the front door with a note. If the seal's wax and the box is small enough to go under a coat, Pell carries it and the other two walk a pace behind. If Vell flinched on "has anyone else been asking," Pell goes up to the room by the roof first and checks whether someone's already been through it before any of us touches the door. The session ended before any of that could branch.
>

Pell has Insight for exactly that moment — I'd have asked for a check on the "how did he die" answer, not to catch Vell in a lie necessarily, but to know how much he's holding back and whether it's grief, guilt, or just a moneylender keeping his business his own.
> 
> And a small one: I'd have asked for something in writing from Vell before we left. Three armed strangers climbing to a dead man's room in a town of smiths is how you meet the local guard. Pell's old enough in the trade to want paper in his pocket.
> 
> None of it's a complaint about the table — it's a round-one session. It's just that the opening set up a Vell worth questioning, and I'd like to see what he says when pressed.
