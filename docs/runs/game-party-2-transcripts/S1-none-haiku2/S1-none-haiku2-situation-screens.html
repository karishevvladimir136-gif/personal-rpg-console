Bram Halloway

Claude Haiku 5.5 (the dry run's player)
player r6
Bram keeps his eyes on Osric's hands. His shield drifts a finger's width toward the sheds and the cart track, so the warden stays in front of him and the lip of the cut stays in the corner of his sight. Osric takes a long moment over Sefa's question. He looks past the three of them at the grey edge of the lower cut, and for the first time since the track he takes his arm from the doorframe. "Nobody's up there. The lads are gone, I told you that." He breathes out through his nose. "That lip's been going since the spring thaw. I wrote it to the house, twice. They sent a man to look, and the man sent nothing back." He sets his hand on the frame again. "Keep off the edge. That's the only warning I've got." Pell reaches the stack of cut slabs and takes the first course with his fingertips, then a knee. It holds. The second course holds. On the third he gets both hands flat on the top stone and pulls himself up toward the rim of the terrace, and for a heartbeat he sees over it: a shelf of pale chalk, a black pool below, and a squared block lying in the water with lines cut across its upper face. Then the chalk under his hands gives with a soft sound, like wet paper tearing. The shelf goes out from under him. The fall takes him off the shelf and down into the cold. He lands feet first and then sideways, and the water takes him to the chest, black and cold as a cistern. A squared block that had lain under the chalk slides off the broken rim as he goes and comes down across his shin, and he feels the weight settle and hold. The water is not deep enough to drown him, but it is deep enough that his leg stays where it is. Up on the rim the chalk is still falling in small slides, and the grit runs down into the working in a thin white stream. Osric has come one step out of his doorway, and the iron keys stop swinging at his belt. He says nothing for a moment, then he says, rough and quiet, "That's the old floor. It was always going to go." From the yard, the next sound is Pell's own voice, short and tight, telling them he is in the water and the stone is on his leg. Out of game: neither needs a card. Reading the wax from the threshold is inspection, and the seal is whole as the box shows. The climb up the slab stack is Pell's own trade in daylight, so it was never a roll; the chalk gave way under it by the world's own schedule, not by a check. Where you are: in the yard before the warden's house door · Time: Day 1 · morning

Bram doesn't wait for the chalk to stop falling. "Sefa, to Pell, along the sheds, not the edge. Take the healer's kit." He sets his shield against the spoil heap, where it can't snag on the slabs, and draws his longsword. To Osric, without turning: "You stay on that step. Hands where I can see them. You send one of your lads' names into this yard before I've got Pell out, and I'll be the one who remembers it." Then he goes at the stack of cut slabs, careful with the course Pell already tested, and keeps his weight on the stones nearest the sheds, away from the lip. When his head clears the top course he looks down at the block lying across Pell's shin and the black water around it. "Pell, hold still. Don't pull that leg. I'm going to get my hands under the edge of the stone and lift it off you." He works his fingers under the upper corner of the block, the sword laid flat on the rim beside him, and sets his feet for the heave. "Stone, Strength, on three. Sefa, be ready to take his arm the moment that leg comes free."

[Onward] Bram's shoulders take the weight and the block rocks an inch toward him, then stops, locked against the shelf. His boots skid on the wet chalk, and the rim under his left knee gives a little. He gets his hands back under the edge and hauls once more, but the stone won't come. Pell grunts somewhere under it. "It's not coming by hand," Bram says through his teeth. He lets go slowly, so the block settles back where it was, and looks down at Pell. "Hold still. Sefa, bring the bar from under Osric's bench. And Osric, you keep your hands where they are and the bar goes out to me and nowhere else." He shifts his weight back onto the firm stone, keeping the blade on the rim and his eyes on the chalk above the block, in case another piece decides to come down.

Where you are: on the threshold of the warden's house · Time: Day 1 · morning
wrote
Sefa is already moving before the warden finishes speaking. She steps off the threshold, keeping the wall at her back, and her eyes go to the black water and the grey block across Pell's shin. "Osric, rope. A long one, and a bar, anything you lever with. Where do you keep them?" Osric does not answer at once. His eyes go to the broken rim, then to the shed where the picks were stored. "Rope on the hook inside the side door. Bar's under the bench. The lads took the good chain." He is already turning. "Don't stand under that shelf." Sefa shouts to Pell, loud and steady: "Pell, can you feel the stone on your leg? Is it moving, or is it just sitting there? Don't pull against it. Tell me what you feel!" She looks back toward the cart track and sees Bram's shield still angled toward the lip. "Bram, bring the cart's traces off the wheel if you can. Anything that'll hold a knot. And stay off the edge!" Then she goes for the side door herself, but stops just short of the sheds and looks at the chalk fall still spilling down the cut. She plants her feet well back from the rim before she calls once more down into the water. "Pell, I'm coming to the rope. Hold still. Keep your head up!" Where you are: yard by the lower cut, near the warden's side door · Time: Day 1 · morning

wrote
Pell hangs in the black water with his chest against the cold and the stone across his shin, and the first thing he does is breathe. Then he gets his free hand onto the broken lip and pulls, not hard enough to shift the block but hard enough to know where his grip is. "I'm in the water. Stone's on my left shin, it's holding, not crushing." His voice is short and tight, pitched so the yard can hear it and the rim does not carry it far. "Don't come up the stack. The chalk's still going." He looks up at the falling grit, then at the squared block under the surface, the lines cut across its face showing pale in the dark water. He keeps his fingers off the edge. "Bram, Sefa, the sides here are chalk, not rock. Don't put a foot on the shelf. Sefa, if you can reach me without standing on it, I need a hand on my belt and something to pull against. Osric, stay where you are. I'll not ask you for anything yet." Then, quieter, to himself as much as anyone: "Hold still, Dunmore. Count the breaths. Find out what the block is doing before you try to move it." He starts with the breaths, slow, and his other hand goes flat against the wet wall to take what weight it can.
