## The opening

> The cart track bends one last time, and Whitestone opens up before you: a white wound in the hillside, terraces of chalky stone glaring in the late-morning sun, dust drifting across a yard of spoil heaps and sheds. Pell Dunmore, Bram Halloway and Sefa Marrin have covered half a day of road since before dawn, and somewhere behind them, half a day back, Maela Thorn's shop waits for a sealed case that must reach it before sunset.
> 
> But the quarry does not sound like a quarry. No ring of picks, no shouting from the faces, no creak from the lifting frame. Hammers and wedges lie scattered near the lower cut as if their owners simply set them down and walked away. A stone cart stands half-loaded, its traces empty, no mule in sight. > 
> In the doorway of that house stands a heavy-set man in a dust-whitened leather apron, a ring of keys at his belt. He has already seen you. He does not wave and he does not come forward; he just stands there, arms folded, waiting to see what you do next.

## Round 2

**Bram Halloway** (action):

> I take my hand off the sword hilt and stay outside the threshold.

> 
> I nod towards the box. “What’s owed altogether? And have you any word from his agent—a letter, a receipt, a name? Something we can put before Thorn while there’s still time to act.”

**Sefa Marrin** (action):

> Sefa stops just short of the doorstep, hands still open at her sides, and lets a little of the road's tension ease out of her shoulders. She doesn't cross the threshold — that's his house, and he hasn't invited her in.
> 
> "Warden Hale," she says, "I won't stand here and tell you you've no right to be angry, because you have. Eleven men walked off and left their tools for the house — that's not a mob of rioters, that's men who trust their warden and are waiting for him to set it right." She nods toward the upright sledgehammer. "That's a thing left so it can't be missed. They're expecting you to come through for them."
> 
> She meets his eyes, calm. "We're not here to break your door down or walk out with what's yours. But Maela Thorn's not your enemy either — she's a woman in Beregost who paid for those dye-salts in good faith, and from your own account her coin went into somebody's pocket in Baldur's Gate long before it ever reached this yard. Vandarren's man. Not her."
> 
> She lets that sit for a heartbeat, then shifts her weight and offers the one thing she has: her word.
> 
> "Let us carry the case back to her before sunset — it's needed by then, or her buyer leaves without it — and I'll put your grievance in front of her myself, as clear as you've put it to us. Or if you'd rather, tell us who in the Gate takes Vandarren's money, and what proof you'd need to see, and we'll do what can be done about it. But the case won't help anyone sitting on your table while the light runs out."
> 
> She pauses, then asks plainly, with no edge: "What would it take, right now, for you to let that box go back to Beregost with us?"
