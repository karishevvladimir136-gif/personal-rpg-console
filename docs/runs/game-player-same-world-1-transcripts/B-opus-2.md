The Sixpenny Rail is one long low room. Its back wall is the bridge railing itself, boarded in and shuttered against the river. There's a fire at one end and a plank bar at the other, and in between is a fug of pipe smoke, wet wool and boiled mutton. Somebody has hung a horse collar over the hearth for luck.

A stout woman with her sleeves pushed up looks at the silver, decides it's real, and sweeps it into her apron. A tankard of something brown arrives. Then comes a wooden bowl of mutton and barley with a heel of bread stuck upright in it. The silver covers both, and she doesn't offer change and doesn't seem to expect to be asked for any.

James turns on the stool with his back to the bar and the tankard in his hand.

The drover tells it well. He was born to it. The lace goes *snap*, the pie goes face-down in the muck, and the pie-man's face gets worse each time it comes round, until the carters by the fire are wiping their eyes. He's maybe forty, red-faced, with forearms like hams. Every time he gets to the part where he still had his winnings, he slaps the pocket of the sheepskin and it chinks.

It isn't cold to the skin, and nothing anyone could see. It feels like a draught where a door has opened in another room. It happens with the first chink, again with the second, and again with the third. Between the slaps it warms back to his own heat.

Then James raises his tankard toward the fire and the whole corner turns to look.

"Keeps fine, friend!" The drover slaps the pocket again, and the coin goes cool again. "Keeps right here. Bram Tolley never lost a penny he'd won fair. Forty head up from Beregost and not one gone lame, and now the lady's own luck on top." He lifts his tankard back at James. The handle turns in his wet fist, and half a pint goes down the front of the sheepskin.

The corner roars. Bram looks down at himself, dripping, and laughs the loudest of anyone.

"*That's* barrel-luck, is it? Here, then." He digs into the pocket and comes out with a copper between two thick fingers. It's a worn, dark little penny. "Lady's own penny. Call it, stranger. Heads, and Bram stands you the next."
