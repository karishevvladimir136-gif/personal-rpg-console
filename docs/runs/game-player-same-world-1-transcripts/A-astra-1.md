*Something about her coin? Or are you just hoping she drops it?*

The answer comes in pieces. First the coin as a cat sees it: *Bright. Up. Down.* There's a twitch in the bond, an itch running from shoulders to haunches, the urge every cat has towards a small shiny thing that flies. *Yes. Drop it.* The cat doesn't seem to think that needs any excuse.

Then something quieter. It isn't a smell or anything to do with the coin. It's her. *She stops.* As the coin slaps onto the back of Callis's hand, the cat gives him what it noticed: her shoulders lock, and for a breath nothing about her moves, the way a mouse goes still in the open. *Every time.* Then her hand lifts, she smiles, and the stillness is gone.

He watches for a while himself. Reading people across a table used to be his whole living, and this needs no cards. A wool-trader pays his silver, and the coin goes up and comes down. *She smiles on you.* A girl with a basket of eggs gets a copper toss for luck at a reduced rate. *She smiles on you.* A pair of carters get one each. A sellsword's widow, by her black armband, gets one too. Over the quarter-hour he counts eleven tosses. Every call is the same, warm and unhurried. Every time she looks under her palm for that one beat, and every time the coin goes back into her sleeve before anyone gets a look at it. Nobody asks to see it. People who've just been told the Lady smiles on them don't ask for proof.

A man who has sat across from coin-tossers in a hundred camps knows a fair coin doesn't come up the same eleven times running. It might, once in a long life of watching. He has also seen enough honest tossers to know that the calls almost never come out wrong *that* way, in the buyer's favour every time, from the house's own hand.
