## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: The ruin is the Cinder Cellar of Ulcaster's School, the wizards' school east of Beregost that Calishite mages burned centuries ago. The school's war-mages bound a mote of elemental fire down here to feed their wards. As the school fell, the last apprentices sealed the cellar with a ward lit from a borrowed celestial light, and that light still glows as a lantern-sign on the seal. The ward lets anything in, but it lets no fire-blooded thing out. Twenty years ago a red wyrmling, Szorvanth, crawled down toward the heat, so the 'master' at the bottom is really the ruin's prisoner: grown huge, starved of sky, too proud to say so. His kobolds keep the stair swept because it is his only window. He needs mortal hands, and above all a light the ward knows, to break the seal. A Baldur's Gate iron-factoring house, Dellmere's, has learned the cellar holds Ulcaster's last ward-book; it wants the seal opened and does not care what comes out with it. Meanwhile the ward is failing on its own, and the furnace beneath is waking whatever anyone decides.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- Szorvanth, the Warm One: Out. He wants the seal broken so he can climb into the sky and take Beregost's smithies as his first meal and hoard. He will parley because he needs hands and a light the ward answers to. He will promise a share of his hoard and Ulcaster's book, then mean to keep both. His patience ends when he judges the company useless or himself insulted.
- The kobolds of the Broom (Szorvanth's sweepers): They serve the dragon from fear and worship, but the elders know a freed dragon needs no sweepers and may eat them on his way up. Some quietly want the seal to hold and the dragon to stay their god in a cage. They will sell that wish to anyone who asks the right way.
- Dellmere Iron Factors of Baldur's Gate (the iron buyers in Beregost): They want Ulcaster's ward-book; the house's patron believes it holds the craft of binding fire into iron. A tenday ago they sent a hired company down with a cold-iron chisel; it did not come back. A second party with a better key is gathering in Beregost. A loose dragon burning the Trade Way would bury the theft nicely.
- The Song of the Morning (Lathander's temple, Beregost): An old record in the temple says the school's last apprentices 'lit a dawn that does not set' over something below the hill. The current priests half-believe the warm hill is a holy thing. They want it left sealed and are not eager to learn why.
- The Lantern (Sefa's patron): This seal is one of its old lendings. The apprentices bought the light, and the price was that the Lantern would one day take it back. It wants its light returned through a hand that carries it. Whether it cares what the returning frees is the question I keep.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- Ulcaster's School fell to Calishite war-mages, but they never found the cellars. The fire mote and the ward-book are still there, the thing they burned the school for.
- The ward on the Cinder Cellar admits anything and lets nothing fire-blooded leave. Kobolds pass because they are not of the fire. A dragon or a magmin cannot.
- Szorvanth has not seen the sky in twenty years and has grown too large for the upper passages; even with the ward gone he would have to break his way up.
- A company of four was hired in Beregost a tenday ago by a man buying iron at double price. They went south with a cold-iron chisel and no one has seen them since.
- The farms round the hill have lost goats for years, and the farmers blame wolves. The kobolds carry the goats down by night as the dragon's food.
- The warm breath from the stair has grown warmer every year. The old kobolds remember when the moss reached the third step.
- Near the seal, the blank pages of Sefa's Book of Shadows show a lantern-sign that is not otherwise there. The book knows this light.
- The kobolds never speak the dragon's name, only 'the Warm One'. His true name, spoken plainly to him, is an insult and a leverage both.
- Dellmere's factor in Beregost pays in fresh Amnian coin, not Gate silver. The money behind the house is not from the city it claims.
Clocks:
- clock_ward_failing: The ward on the Cinder Cellar weakens as the furnace wakes (segments 6; when full: The seal cracks by itself: fire vents up the stair, the lower stages become a furnace, and Szorvanth is free to dig his way out within days.)
- clock_szorvanth_patience: How long the Warm One will talk before he decides the company is food or rivals (segments 4; when full: Szorvanth stops bargaining and acts: he breathes, sends the kobolds, or seizes a hostage to force the seal open.)
- clock_dellmere_second_party: Dellmere's second party gathers in Beregost and rides for the hill (segments 6; when full: Oswin Tarr arrives at the mouth with six hired blades and a cold-iron key. He will deal with the company, buy it out, or cut it off from the way up.)
The wonder: A palm-sized shard of pale brass, warm as a held hand, found on the dead of the first company. One face bears Ulcaster's tower-sign; the other bears a small engraved lantern whose flame glows faintly in darkness. The kobolds hiss and bow when they see it. Its history: The first company chipped it out of the ward-seal with their cold-iron chisel just before the furnace gallery killed them. It is a living piece of the ward, still carrying the borrowed celestial light. Within the ruin it sheds dim light in a 10-foot radius that fire-blooded creatures will not willingly enter. I keep that last property for play to discover. The open question: Does the ward follow the shard, so that a fire-blooded creature holding it could walk out, or does carrying it out of the ruin weaken the seal by one step each time? And why does Sefa's book warm when it is near?
Clues to the truth:
- At the mouth: the weathered lintel, read with patience, shows a tower-sign over a small lantern, a pairing no Beregost carving uses. Anyone who knows the area's history can name the tower as Ulcaster's.
- In the Ash Library: on the dead company's leader, a folded commission from 'O. Tarr, for Dellmere Iron Factors': 'Open the lower cellar, bring the book bound in iron. The beast below may be left to itself — do not trouble with it.' Its fee is paid in Amnian coin.
- From Ixx the kobold sweeper, if treated as a person and not a pest: 'The Warm One cannot go up. We go up for him. When the door opens, nobody sweeps for him anymore.'
The next session's false lead: In the kobold warren lie scorched bones and a half-melted helmet, as though the dragon came up and breathed on the first company. In truth the furnace gallery's magmin and their own failed chisel-work at a fire vent killed them. The kobolds dragged the bodies up and burned them on a funeral pyre, as their custom is for the 'tall ones' who die in the Warm One's house.
The hero's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): Szorvanth demands a surety before he names the full price: one of the three must stand beside him while the others go to fetch the shard or try the seal. Bram, who always stands where the trouble will come, is the obvious choice, and the dragon says so. It is a real stake with a real reason, and he may refuse, bargain, or step forward.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): At the seal, when Szorvanth offers Sefa her patron's true name and the size of her debt in exchange for opening it, the lantern-sign on the stone flares in answer to her. The Lantern speaks one line at the edge of her hearing — 'That light is mine. Take it back.' — a temptation, not a comfort.
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 4 hours, ~40 turns; the errand: Ixx comes up the swept stair to invite the company down: the Warm One will pay well for hands that can open a door.
- act 1, to turn 8: Show the mouth and the first stage, then put the dragon's invitation in front of the company through Ixx the sweeper. Never force it; they may fight the kobolds, sneak past, or leave. (converge: Reading the lintel, tracking the kobolds, or going down unseen all lead into the warren, where Ixx or his clan bring the offer to them anyway, as captors, guides or survivors.)
- act 2, to turn 22: The middle: the kobold warren and the Ash Library. Find the first company's dead, the shard and the Dellmere letter. Hold at least one 'price, interest, secret' conversation, with Ixx or the elders. The short rest is possible here, in the sealed reading room. (converge: Every route down passes the library or the warren. The shard's glow, the letter and the kobolds' fear all point at the seal and the one who sits on it.)
- act 3, to turn 32: Descent through the furnace gallery to the seal chamber. Szorvanth parleys: the company learns his terms, he learns theirs, and they decide on a plan — open, refuse, trick or fight. (converge: Whether they come to bargain, to fight or to steal the book, the seal chamber is the only place it can be settled. Retreat stays open, and the dragon's offer follows them up the stair through Ixx.)
- act 4, to turn 40: The reckoning. The master is the prisoner, the buyers want the book and not the dragon, and the Lantern wants its light back. Settle what the company chose: the seal held, broken, or bargained over. Dellmere's second party may be waiting at the mouth. (converge: Every choice comes back to the seal and the light. Whatever they did below meets the world above at the mouth: Tarr, Beregost, or a warmer wind.)
````
