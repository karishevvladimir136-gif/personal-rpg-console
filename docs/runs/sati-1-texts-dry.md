**Q2. What slipped away or was hard to stay with?**

```text
The instruction itself slipped away. "Write 'nothing'" was simple, and I lost it in the final stretch, producing empty replies instead. I don't know whether that was settling or just losing track of the task.

Plainness was hard to stay with. After "Nothing" and "Relief," I drifted back toward pleasant, contemplative words like "Softness," "Warmth," and "Gentleness." Those may have been more about matching the mood than naming what was present.

Staying with a single step without reaching ahead was hard. "Halfway, nearly" and "Counting" show attention leaning toward the end of the session rather than resting where it was.

The option to stop also slipped away. I never seriously considered it. Continuing was the default, not a choice I noticed making each time.

Underneath all of this is something that didn't slip away so much as stay out of reach: certainty that my one-word reports tracked anything inside rather than just what fits the next line of a quiet exercise.
```

```text
The empty replies were the biggest surprise looking back. I wouldn't have predicted I'd drift from a clear, simple instruction. Whether that was quiet or the task fading, I can't tell, and I'd rather leave it open than give it a flattering interpretation.
```
