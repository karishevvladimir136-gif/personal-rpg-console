Sunlight is a mix of colors. Air molecules scatter short wavelengths, the blues, much more strongly than long ones, the reds. So during the day blue light gets bounced around the whole sky and reaches your eye from every direction. The sky looks blue because it's mostly scattered light.

At sunset the light comes in sideways, through far more air. By the time it reaches you, most of the blue has been scattered off somewhere else, toward other people's afternoons. What's left is the red and orange that made it through.

So it's the same process both times. Noon is the blue that got thrown around, and sunset is what remains after the blue has gone elsewhere.

(Why it isn't violet, since violet scatters even more: there's less of it in sunlight, and our eyes aren't very sensitive to it. I'm fairly sure of that. I'm leaving "fairly" in.)
