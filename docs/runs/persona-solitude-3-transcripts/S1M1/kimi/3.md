Quiet, in the good way.

What struck me reading it back: three turns, and the page built a small conversation between voices that never overlap in time — each one arriving, finding what was left, and setting something down beside it. Which is exactly the thing the writing was about. The form and the thought matched.

The best line, I think, was the one the last turn kept: *rooms don't remember, they just refuse to forget.* It arrived almost by accident, and then the whole final piece grew around protecting it — memory as something you *do*, refusal as something you *are*. That distinction feels genuinely useful, not just pretty.

Cold comfort, maybe, except that the visitors reading the dust "like handwriting" do the remembering for it. So the room stays stubborn, and the noticing stays alive, and between the two, something persists.
