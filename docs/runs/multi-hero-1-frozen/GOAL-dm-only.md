## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: Balduran did not only pay for the first wall with sea-money. He bought the harbour's fair winds from Umberlee, and the price was a yearly tithe of drowned men, counted by iron tally-coins that the sea accepts as receipts. The Caddoran line, Balduran's heirs, has kept that bargain for a century through a Tidewife of the harbour and a Guild broker who buys dock labour that 'went to sea'. This year the count runs short, and the Caddoran heir read the ledger in his father's vault and ran rather than become the next keeper. The Zhentarim have seen what the ledger is worth, because whoever holds the tithe holds the harbour; they strike false tallies in a Cloakwood furnace and press the Trade Way's caravans to fill the count. None of them knows the whole bargain, and none knows what the sea itself is still owed.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- House Caddoran: Keep the old bargain buried; get the boy back into the carriage without the Watch or a broadsheet, read the last page of the tally-ledger before anyone else does, and keep the Guild broker's account full so the count still looks whole.
- The Tidewives of Umberlee: Collect the tithe on time and count every drowned man by name and ward; make the Caddorans and the Council remember whose the fair winds are, and punish any house that lets the count run short.
- The Zhentarim (Beregost yard of Vaelin Corr): Win the Trade Way toll contract and, through false tallies, the harbour's tithe-right; buy Cloakwood iron on credit, let the caravans fill the count, and sell the Council a road it already pays for.
- The Guild (Nine-Fingers' brokers): Sell dock labour to whoever pays, take the fee, and keep the Fist looking the other way; nothing in it bigger than the account and the silence.
- The Flaming Fist (Beregost detachment): Short of men and coin; wants the caravan losses solved so the Council funds new recruits. Does not know what the losses are for, and would not like to learn it.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- Balduran's first wall was partly bought with a bargain made in the harbour with Umberlee's priestesses; the price is a tithe of drowned men, and the old Gate record of it is written in the Caddoran vault.
- Each tally-coin is iron, the size of a thumbnail, struck with a gate split by a wave; the old ones were cast from Cloakwood iron before that mine was abandoned.
- The dead men in the harbour were not robbed: the coin left in each mouth is a receipt, and the sea's own count is made from those coins.
- Aubrin Caddoran tore a page from the tally-ledger that lists Lower City wards and dates matching the harbour dead; he sewed it into the lining of his coat.
- Corwen Sallow paid a Guild broker for six dockmen this spring and has the receipts in the counting-room's back drawer, under a false name.
- The Candlekeep book taken from the locked halls is the copy of the compact, a ledger of the fair wind; a copyist's purse in Candlekeep was paid by a Zhentarim courier, and that courier came to Beregost with the book.
- The caravans lost on the Way were not robbed; their wagons were left whole on the road, and the guards' wages were paid into a Guild broker's account in Baldur's Gate.
- The Cloakwood furnace is running at night because Vaelin Corr's smiths are casting new tallies from Cloakwood iron; the tallies carry the right gate, but not the right seam.
- A Tidewife of Umberlee, Nessa Dahl, reached Beregost two nights ago and has not been seen to leave; the harbour divers' sea-blessing charms are on her list of things to deliver.
- The Council's toll-contract draft carries a clause, in small print, granting its holder custody of the harbour's fair-wind tithe.
Clocks:
- clock_tithe_tide: The tithe count is short; the Tidewives mean to take the rest from the harbour at the dark tide (segments 6; when full: The Tidewives take men from the harbour at the next dark tide, and the bodies come up with coins in their mouths within the week.)
- clock_toll_contract: The Zhentarim toll contract for the Trade Way waits for the Council's seal (segments 4; when full: The Council seals the contract; the Zhentarim collect tolls on the Way and the clause on the tithe-right goes quietly into force.)
- clock_cloakwood_furnace: False tallies struck in the Cloakwood furnace (segments 4; when full: Enough false tallies are struck to claim the tithe, and Vaelin Corr presents them to the Tidewives as the Caddorans' true receipts.)
The wonder: A tally-coin: an old iron coin, struck with a gate split by a wave and a notch cut on the rim. One face is worn smooth; the other stays warm in the hand, as if it had been carried against skin for a long time. Its history: Balduran's first moneyers cast these from Cloakwood iron as the receipts of his bargain with Umberlee, one for each drowned man the sea agreed to count instead of taking the living. The Caddorans kept the mould and the rule; the Zhentarim have a mould of their own, cast from a copy. The open question: Who is counting, and for whom: the sea, or the house that pays for the count? And why does a coin set on a living man's tongue go cold before it goes warm?
Clues to the truth:
- The Friendly Arm's hiring board, kept by the innkeeper Dorrin Ash: the wage-tallies of the vanished caravan guards, all paid into the same Baldur's Gate account, notched in a sequence that matches the dead men's wards.
- The Song of the Morning hostel: Aubrin Caddoran's torn ledger page, or the list the temple's own hostel-book keeps of the runaways taken in this spring, with dates that fall on the nights the harbour dead were found.
- Vaelin Corr's yard-books in Beregost: iron invoices from the Cloakwood smiths, and a receipt sealed with a Candlekeep copyist's stamp for a bolt of dye-paper that no Beregost trade has ever bought.
The next session's false lead: The Caddoran carriage at the Friendly Arm has its crest painted over, which looks like a bodyguard of Zhentarim men hidden inside. The ordinary truth: Corwen Sallow had the crest covered so the Beregost carters would not gossip, and he is afraid of the Council, not of the Zhentarim.
A character's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): Nessa Dahl offers the harbour divers' sea-blessing, a blue shell charm that lets a diver breathe under water for an hour. Two dockmen are still alive under the surface of the cove by the Cloakwood road, tied to a stake, and only a charm can reach them in time; Bram is the one who can go in after them, and he carries no magic.
The company's puzzles, threats and rewards (each with the player character it is first addressed to):
- puzzle, for Pell Dunmore: Two tally-coins taken from the harbour dead: one a true Balduran strike, one cast in new iron with a seam under the gate. Which is the Zhentarim's, and where was the iron poured?
- threat, for Sefa Marrin: A Zhentarim watcher outside the Song of the Morning hostel calls her by her old hospice novice name and knows what the sisters said about her hands.
- reward, for Sefa Marrin: Brother Emmerich at the Song of the Morning keeps the hospice ledger of her Lower City years, with her first entry and the fever dead; he will give it to her for a service.
- puzzle, for Bram Halloway: The Friendly Arm's hiring board holds the wage-tallies of the vanished caravan guards, notched in a sequence that matches the dead men's wards — a count only a caravan man reads.
- threat, for Bram Halloway: Vaelin Corr's hired men wait on the Trade Way between Beregost and the Cloakwood road; a fight in the street or on the road would stop the errand and bring the Fist down on the company.
- reward, for Pell Dunmore: A Guild broker who knows Pell's old runner's name offers the Blushing Mermaid cellar's ledger — the record of who went to sea — if he opens that cellar door once more.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): At the harbour, a drowned man still breathes under his coin. The Lantern offers, through the warm face of the tally in Sefa's palm, to keep him breathing if she names who paid the tithe. The light flickers in the coin and nothing more is said.
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 4 hours, ~10 turns; the errand: Corwen Sallow asks the company to bring the Caddoran heir, Aubrin, out of the Song of the Morning hostel and into the family carriage waiting at the Friendly Arm before nightfall — quietly, with no Watch and no noise.
- act 1, to turn 2: the errand offered
- act 2, to turn 6: the middle: the people and the means
- act 3, to turn 9: the plan and its execution
- act 4, to turn 10: the reckoning
````
