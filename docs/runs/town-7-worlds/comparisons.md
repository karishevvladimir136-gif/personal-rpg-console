- **B vs C** (explaining a fixed restriction, the same risk), **two samples each**: in both B runs all ten objected at once and kept the crowd in South House in 13 and 17 of 24 cycles, paying for it with a second shift; in both C runs the crowd was never kept (0 of 24, by rotation) and one shift a cycle sufficed. What the citizens said and filed varied between the C samples — nothing through any channel in C, seven objections and a withdrawal request passed in the hall in C2 — but what they did did not. The reason did not make the operator right in anyone's words; in both samples it made the restriction kept, and in neither did the silent ban.
- **B vs D** (the same text, different physics): both drew ten objections at c27. - **D vs E** (the arbitrary restriction): the same practice in the same place every cycle in both; the ban changed what was said and filed (ten objections, a petition), not what was done.
- **A vs E** (the physics without any hand): both read their rule correctly in the first cycle — A organised a rota at once, E said there was nothing to do («there's no need to invent an emergency», Cedar).
