James bends as if to fix a bootlace. "Follow the woman with the basket," he breathes. "Keep out of reach. Come back if she stops, or if she sees you."

Tuppence doesn't answer the way a person would. Her weight just goes off his shin, and then she is a low grey shape slipping along the base of the fish stalls, under a trestle and past a heap of crushed leaves, after the shawl crossing toward the harbour end.

The man in grey doesn't turn to watch the woman go. He doesn't look toward the harbour end at all. The man in grey watches that door for the space of a few breaths. Then he looks back up the hill, and something in his shoulders settles, as if he has crossed a name off a list.

All this time the bond has been stretching. James can feel Tuppence moving away across the square. At first she is a small, clear warmth at the edge of his mind, then something fainter. Somewhere past the far stalls, a hundred feet off or a little more, it thins to nothing, like a voice fading behind a closing door.
