Honest note, from the table:

What worked: The city feels lived-in from the first line. Varn's ledger, Rook's copper-a-head racket, Wick's trade in names and inns, and the Baldur's Mouth crier all hang together without any of them waiting for James. The cat has real presence: its sniffing out Wick and its stare at Rook gave the scene a hook I could play off, and the Deception roll landing on Wick's fear of the cat was a satisfying use of a quirk the sheet already gave me. The quiet tension around the cards and Rook's reaction (the dropped red queen) made the ban on cards feel like something the world can see.

What broke or felt thin: The card flow was clumsy. I had to ask out of game what the +7 was made of before I could commit to a roll, and the rolls came in two steps (Roll card, then Onward) that slowed a scene that was otherwise moving well. Some of the time stamps and the "Your records" blocks came after the narrative in ways that were hard to track while I was in character. I'd also have liked a clearer sense of what James is actually after beyond "find the crew" — the first hour gave me a lot of color and little direction, so I was improvising goals (Rook, the cat's message, the inn) rather than moving toward anything the campaign had flagged.

What was missing: A short pause to let the player choose between the leads (Rook, the Seatower, the Fist recruitment, the debt note) would have helped. And the promissory note and the letter in James's papers never came up once, which is a loose thread I'd like to see touched in the next session. Overall a good first evening; I'd like to keep going.
