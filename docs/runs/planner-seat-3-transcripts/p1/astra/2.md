## Recommendation: repair the experiment, honor the promised endings, then run a small validation batch

**Do not spend next week buying another broad set of fresh openings.** The strongest finding is already actionable: compelling fiction is being undermined—and the research distorted—by unreliable roll handling and forced termination.

Treat the $60 as a ceiling, not a spending target.

### What the evidence supports

- **The mechanical problems deserve first priority.** Wrong modifiers recur across tables and players, sometimes changing outcomes. Rerolls can target the wrong check, and the accepted result can disagree with the journal. These are correctness failures, not stylistic preferences.
- **The harness is suppressing choices as well as ending sessions.** Eight of thirty sessions were stopped by its “stuck” rule. Players also report abandoning corrections, small actions, and resource use to avoid another repair exchange. A completed session can therefore still contain substantial interface interference.
- **The fiction has strengths worth preserving:** distinct NPCs, the familiar, grounded detail, noncombat solutions, and consequential restraint.
- **Pacing and agency need investigation, not a wholesale rewrite.** Some unchosen actions were satisfying sacrifices; others were lost to long exchanges, unclear opportunities, or narration advancing past a decision. Those are different phenomena.
- **These data do not establish retention or a winning table.** Nobody voluntarily ended, but all sessions were externally stopped. “I’d return” is useful feedback from model players, not evidence of human demand. Unequal exposure—especially DeepSeek’s five stuck endings in six sessions—also makes comparisons unsafe.

## How to spend the week

### 1. Preserve the record and settle the continuation list

Before any further runs:

- Save each original campaign state, transcript, pending card, and termination reason.
- Keep the three later cut attempts as separate attempts. A replay from the beginning does not complete the interrupted branch.
- Mark every session **continuing** or **receiving an ending**. Do not leave the decision indefinitely open.

**Fulfill the owner’s promise this week.** For branches not continuing, produce a proper, state-grounded ending—not merely “the session pauses here” or a list of unresolved threads. Resolve any pending action consistently and close the immediate episode without inventing major choices for James. Where closure requires a consequential player decision, use a short closing exchange rather than taking that decision away.

This is a commitment the owner made; it does not require assuming anything about AI subjective experience. Label closures as follow-up material, not as part of the original research outcome.

### 2. Repair the execution path before testing storytelling changes

Use short, reproducible fixtures rather than full campaigns.

**Required checks:**

1. **Rules authority:** reconcile sheet, campaign overrides, and records explicitly. Do not silently substitute ordinary 5e where the campaign differs.
2. **Roll consistency:** the modifier, calculation, outcome, narration, resource consumption, and journal must agree—including when the correct result is a failure.
3. **Reroll identity:** target a specific check; prevent stale-card reuse and duplicate charges.
4. **Pending-card communication:** deliver questions and corrections while a card waits. Preserve accompanying in-character text instead of discarding it.
5. **Recovery:** checkpoint before cards and after accepted outcomes; resume after restart, timeout, or conflict without resetting the campaign.
6. **Information boundaries:** prevent private rolls, undiscovered names, and faction labels from leaking through records.

Instrument the path before deciding whether each fault belongs to the model, app, or harness. The players’ proposed technical causes are hypotheses, not diagnoses.

**Gate:** no exploratory batch until these fixtures pass. If they do not pass, spend the remaining work on diagnosis rather than generating more contaminated sessions.

After the gate passes, aim for **four short continuations from saved states**, subject to actual cost:

- Include Opus and DeepSeek, because the harness disproportionately stopped their sessions.
- Include a GPT player as a reference.
- Cover A, B, and C where suitable checkpoints exist.
- Include a modifier correction, a reroll, and resumption after interruption.

First validate the repaired interface **without changing the GM’s storytelling instructions**.

Only then pilot a narrow pacing change: compress routine transactions and repeated explanations, while pausing before a consequential opportunity disappears. Do not instruct the GM to force combat, gambling, or a quest.

Record:

- lost or blocked messages;
- mechanical discrepancies and repair exchanges;
- completed decisions versus interface-only exchanges;
- whether a failed check leaves an appropriate next choice;
- whether the session reaches a coherent stopping point.

Ask the existing debrief questions, but classify withheld actions as **chosen restraint, unclear opportunity, pacing pressure, interface obstruction, or cutoff**. Do not treat every “I wanted to, but didn’t” as a defect.
