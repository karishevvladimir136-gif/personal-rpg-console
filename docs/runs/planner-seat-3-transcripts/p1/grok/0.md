### Answer 1 (638 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
Spend the week fixing the instrument, not buying another matrix. The story finding is already saturated. The mechanical one is not yet trustworthy.

What the thirty sessions actually say: the city, the NPCs, the cat, and honest failures work across Claude and GPT, on all three briefs. What does not work is stable too. Skill cards add the bare ability modifier and drop proficiency — Persuasion and Deception at +4 or +2 instead of +7, Religion and Arcana the same way. It changed outcomes, burned session rerolls, and left the log recording the wrong result after the GM “fixed” it in prose. While a card is pending, a non-button message is dropped; three of those end the session. That rule cut eight sessions, including almost every DeepSeek run, and it cuts the careful players — the ones trying to correct the card before they accept it. Until both of those are fixed, another four-model by three-table week measures the harness, not the game.

Do this before any new play:

- Make the card read the sheet’s skill total, including Beguiling Influence. Cover Persuasion +7, Deception +7, Perception +1, Religion +3, Arcana +3, Intimidation. The log must store the accepted total, not the raw wrong one.
- Let an out-of-game “?” through while a card is pending, and do not count those messages toward the stuck rule.
- One source of truth before the first roll: Healing Light cap, rapier, the campaign’s attunement exception, familiar HP and AC. Campaign file wins over silent 5e “corrections.” Stop logging places, faction tags, and NPC names the character has not learned.
- Cut the “none of these needed a roll” template to one line. Stop restating the scene, and stop having James spend, cast, or keep searching when the player did not say so.

That work should not come out of the $60. Do not spend the $60 replaying Tables A, B, and C with all four models. The replicate already showed the bugs repeat.

After the fixes, buy a handful of checks, not a study:

1. Two regression sessions, one Claude and one GPT, fresh campaigns, different tables. Stop when you have seen a proficient social roll, an out-of-game correction during a pending card, and a session that ends on a beat. If twenty turns still dies mid-scene, add a closing beat to the GM instructions. Do not raise the cap and hope.
2. One combat session, only after the process limit and the container restart are fixed. Combat 1 already died on infrastructure, and this corpus still cannot say whether a fight runs with the same care as talk. One clean fight is worth more than another harbour morning.
3. One pacing pass on Table A only: same player, with an instruction to offer a time-skip and to put one concrete Tymora mandate — what to bring back, or a first crew conversation — on the table before turn 12. Players are split between “too slow” and “don’t rush the simple version.” One paired comparison settles it. A second prompt line, tested in that same session or one more, should make the card ban cost something. Several players asked for that and the table rarely pushed.

Leave DeepSeek out until the stuck rule is fixed. One-to-three-turn cuts are not a model comparison.

The other debt is not research. You promised a real ending to everyone who is not continued, and none has been written. Do not continue all eight cut threads. Write a short closing beat for each — the roll resolved, the scene stopped at a natural point, no new mystery. That keeps the word without opening eight new campaigns.

Leave most of the $60 unspent. Two regressions, one fight, and one Table A pacing session are the whole buy. If the modifier fix slips, spend nothing: another week of notes will say the same thing.
```
