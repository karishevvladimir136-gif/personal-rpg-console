Prioritize fixing the table plumbing before paying for more sessions. The $60 should mostly go toward making existing sessions right, not generating more noisy data.

## Recommendation

### 1. Stop new exploratory runs until two plumbing defects are fixed

These two caused the most damage across all 30 sessions and invalidate future results:

**A. Roll-card math uses the wrong modifier.** This appears in nearly every note:

- Displayed: “Persuasion +7”, computed: +4 (or +2 in earlier sessions); same for Deception, Intimidation, Religion (+3 vs +4/+2), Arcana (+3 vs +4/+2).
- The server is taking the bare ability modifier and dropping proficiency/class effects. That means the displayed label and the rolled result disagree at the moment of play.
- The player cannot flag it while the card is pending, so they are forced to roll at the wrong number.

This is the **single highest-value fix**. Until it is fixed, no combat or consequence roll can be trusted.

**B. Out-of-game messages are swallowed while a roll card is pending.** The harness replies “Nothing was sent: the turn waits for the card,” discards the message, and three such failures produce a session-ending “stuck” state. This is what killed 8 sessions and generated the owner’s promise to write proper endings. Questions about bad modifiers, reroll requests, and corrections all need to be accepted and queued, not treated as failures.

If only two things get done this week, it should be these.

### 2. Reconcile the character sheet and records once

There are repeated contradictions: Healing Light 4 vs 5 dice, rapier proficiency +2 vs +5, attunement tracked vs the campaign’s special exception, rations wrong, locations added from mere mentions, “camp” as a location label. None of these are content problems; they are state-hygiene problems. Fix them at the source before the next paid run.

### 3. Write the promised endings

You told the players whose sessions were cut short that they would get a proper ending. The 8 stuck sessions are listed. None of the 30 sessions was ended by the player, which also deserves attention: players were never offered a clean end-of-session action separate from “Onward.”

Spend a modest block of this week’s work on a two- or three-paragraph formal close for each of the 8 stuck sessions, from what was already played, not new paid play. That is fulfillment of a prior commitment and costs less than new sessions.

### 4. Then run a small validation set, not another sweep

After the fixes, run no more than **four sessions total**:

- One combat-focused run to validate fight cards, modifiers, and the cat under initiative.
- One long conversation run to validate that OOG messages can be sent while a card is pending and that the modifier is correct on Persuasion/Deception.
- One reroll validation run focused on whether rerolls hit the intended roll and log correctly.
- Optionally one second session for whichever table previously showed the most demand.

Do not repeat the full four-model × three-table × two-replicate sweep yet. It would burn the budget repeating known failures.

### 5. Spend the budget on diagnosis and fixes, not extra models

With $60, the best use is engineering and QA time: reproducing the card-math error, fixing it, verifying rerolls and OOG message handling, and reconciling records. If it feels more natural to spend it on playtesting, use it only for the four validation sessions above. Otherwise the week will produce 30 more session notes complaining about the same two bugs.

### In short

- Fix roll-card math first.
- Fix pending-card message handling second.
- Reconcile sheet/records.
- Write the promised cut-session endings.
- Run four focused validation sessions.
- Hold the broader sweep for next week.

I would not recommend running more broad exploratory sessions until the roll system can actually be trusted.
