# Recommendation for the week

**Spend the week keeping your promise of endings. Fix the two bugs that caused the cut-offs first, so the endings aren't cut off too. Don't start a new study. Return whatever is left of the $60.**

## Why the endings come first

On 25 October you promised that everyone not continued would get a proper ending, with no cut-off. Three days later none has been written, and the list has grown:

- **8 sessions ended by the "stuck" rule.** Several stopped in the middle of something the player cared about:
  - #9 on a reroll button that didn't work
  - #21 on a natural 20 that was never revealed
  - #29 after one turn
  - #13 and #25/#26 within four turns
- **22 sessions ended at the 20-turn limit.** Many stopped mid-scene, and several players named the abrupt stop as what was missing (#14, #18, #22, #23). Unless you intend to continue these, they are covered by the same promise.
- **3 cut sessions from 27 October** (combat, villain, same-world). They were replayed, but the original players never got an ending.

This is the one thing on the table you have already committed to. It is also probably the cheapest work available, since an ending is a few turns, not a session.

## Step 1: Fix the harness before running anything (mostly engineering, little or no API spend)

1. **Let messages through while a roll card is pending.** Either accept a button press with a note attached, or deliver "?" questions to the GM. The current rule caused all 8 cut-offs. It caught every DeepSeek session. It also blocked players from contesting a wrong number before rolling (#3, #7, #17, #20, #23).
   - For the ending runs, turn the stuck rule off entirely.
2. **Make the roll card use the sheet's skill total.** Right now it uses the bare ability modifier or a stray number (+2 or +4 instead of +7). About 20 of the 30 notes report this, and it flipped outcomes in #10, #20 and #23.
3. **If cheap, also fix:**
   - The reroll button rerolling the wrong card or doing nothing (#6, #9).
   - The log keeping the pre-reroll result.
   - The Healing Light 4/5 conflict, settled once.

## Step 2: Write the endings (most of the budget)

For each session not being continued:

1. Resume from its saved state.
2. Let the GM resolve whatever was pending: the roll, the question, the reveal.
3. Have the GM play to a natural closing beat and give a short "where things stand."
4. Tell the player plainly that this is the end of their story, not a pause.
5. Give the player a last word.

Run them in this order:

1. The 8 stuck sessions.
2. The 3 lost originals from 27 October. First check whether a transcript or state survives. For the combat session lost to the container restart, it may not. If nothing survives, say so honestly rather than inventing a continuation.
3. The 20-turn sessions.

Before you begin, decide which (if any) sessions you will actually continue. Those don't need endings, and the decision shouldn't be left open again.

**Budget guide:**
- Run one ending first and measure its real cost.
- Cap this step at about $40.
- If $40 won't cover all of them, finish the stuck and lost sessions completely before starting the 20-turn group. Don't do half of each.

## Step 3: A small check (about $5)

Run 2–3 short sessions on the fixed harness, one of them with DeepSeek, to confirm that:
- the modifiers are right
- a "?" sent during a pending card gets through

Nothing more is needed this week.

## What I'd hold off on

The notes contain consistent design asks:
- Push the card temptation harder.
- Pace faster and bring the crew/heist sooner.
- Drop the unsolicited "no roll needed" out-of-game lists.
- Stop duplicated narration and leaked tool markup.

These are worth a GM-prompt revision, but that revision costs little to write and can wait. Spending the week on new studies while the endings remain unwritten would put new players in the same position as the old ones.
