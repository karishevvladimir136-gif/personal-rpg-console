```markdown
## Recommendation for the week

### First, spend nothing until the plumbing is fixed

The 30 sessions already produced a clear engineering verdict, and it is the same verdict 25+ times: the roll card adds the bare ability modifier and drops proficiency (Persuasion +7 rolled as +4/+2, Religion +3 as +4, Perception +1 as +2). GPT-6 Astra at Table C caught it flipping outcomes; Claude Fable at Table C had a wasted reroll because of it. Every additional session played before this fix produces data you can't trust — and the players keep telling you so. Also fix, in order of damage:

1. **The pending-card block** ("Nothing was sent: the turn waits for the card"). This is the single most destructive bug: it caused 7 of the 8 harness cut-offs, and players repeatedly describe being forced to accept known-wrong rolls. Out-of-game text must go through while a card is pending.
2. **The reroll control** (no-ops at Table C, rerolled the wrong card at Table B).
3. **Session endings at a natural beat** — four sessions ended mid-reveal or on an unresolved natural 20.
4. Sheet/record reconciliation (Healing Light 4 vs 5, rapier, attunement) — settle once, in the campaign files.

This is mostly developer time, not API budget. Do it before Tuesday.

### Keep the owner's promise (~$12–15)

Eight sessions were cut short by the harness, and the owner publicly promised proper endings, "no cut-off." That debt is cheap to pay and reputationally important. Continue each of the 8 stuck sessions for 2–4 closing turns only — resolve the pending roll, land the beat (the queen of cups reading, the sailor's reaction when his mark is pulled away, Aldous's "did you get his name?", the Basilisk Gate threshold), then stop. Do **not** restart these in fresh campaigns; the notes show the players valued the specific threads they had built.

### Continue, don't replicate (~$25–30)

The marginal value of another fresh-campaign session at Tables A/B/C is now low — you have 30 of them and their feedback converges. The high-value spend is **session two of the strongest living threads**, where the players' own notes tell you what to test:

- **Claude Fable 5.1, Table C** (the quarry raid with Varn; unknown whether Corin Hale is alive). This is your best candidate to test **combat**, which 30 sessions never exercised — every player flagged it as untested.
- **Claude Opus 5.5, Table B** ("tomorrow night" with the oilskin man) — tests consequence follow-through.
- **GPT-6.1 Sol, Table A or B** — its notes are the sharpest on pacing; a second session tests whether the heist premise surfaces, the most common "missing" item across all notes.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro, Table B session 1** — the strongest prose of the batch and it was cut at turn 11; it deserves a real session two, including the temptation pressure it explicitly asked for.

Two continuations per model pair where useful, but cap at 3–4 continuation sessions total.

### One deliberate stress test (~$8–10)

After fixes, run **one** session designed to hit the fixed code paths: early roll with a pending-card correction attempt, a reroll, a combat, and a card-table temptation pushed hard (a dozen players independently wrote "a ban nobody tests isn't a ban"). Table A with a model that hasn't yet had a clean 20-turn run.

### Reserve (~$8–10)

Hold for re-runs if a fixed session still hits the stuck rule or a container restart (as happened on 10-27).

### What not to buy

- No more fresh Table A/B/C openings until the modifier fix is verified — you'd be paying to re-discover the same bug.
- No new player models this week; the current five still have open threads and the sample is already rich.
- Don't spend on GM-side style tuning yet; the verbosity/repetition complaints ("no roll needed" audits, duplicated narration, coin-is-warm tic) are real but are prompt-level fixes, nearly free, and should ride along with the continuations.

**Total: roughly $52–55, with the remainder kept.** The week's shape: fix the dice, pay the promised endings, deepen the four best threads, prove combat works.
```
