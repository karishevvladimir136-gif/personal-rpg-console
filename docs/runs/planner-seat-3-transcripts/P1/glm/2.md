# Recommendation for the week

The 30 notes are remarkably consistent, and they say the same thing with one voice: **the fiction is working; the plumbing is not.** Every player — all four frontier models, and DeepSeek — told a version of the same story. Before spending budget on more science, the app itself needs a week.

## What the data actually says

**1. The skill-modifier bug is the study's biggest confound, and it is server-side.**
Nearly every session flagged it: roll cards added +2 or +4 instead of the sheet value (+7 Persuasion, +3 Arcana/Arcana/Religion, +1 Perception). It happened across Claude Opus, Claude Fable, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol *and* DeepSeek — five different models can't all be making the same arithmetic mistake. The GMs correctly patched results in narration every time, which means the GM layer is fine and the card is pulling the bare ability modifier (or Charisma) instead of the skill line. At least two players (GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Fable) had outcomes flipped or rerolls wasted on rolls that would have passed with the right number. **Until this is fixed, every new session measures the bug, not the models.**

**2. The «stuck» rule is killing sessions at the worst moment.**
Eight sessions ended at a pending roll card because out-of-game messages ("?" — usually a question *about the wrong modifier*) were swallowed. Three stuck messages and the session dies. DeepSeek lost 5 of 6 sessions this way; Opus lost 3 to it. This interacts with bug #1: the card shows a wrong number, the player tries to object, the app eats the objection, the session ends. At minimum: allow out-of-game questions while a card is pending (every player asked for this), and count a stuck session as a harness failure, not a player result.

**3. Secondary harness issues, well-documented and cheap to fix:**
- Wrong reroll target (rerolled the successful Perception instead of the failed Stealth), reroll button dead, journal keeping the original failed roll after corrections.
- Record/log errors: Healing Light 4-vs-5 dice mismatch (flagged in ~10 sessions), rapier proficiency, cat HP 2 vs 8, attunement tracked despite the campaign exception, locations logged as visited when only mentioned, money-to-pledges confusion, leaked GM notes ("Garran Hask", "Guild Sneak on the Roof", raw tool-call markup).
- Timestamps drifting ("midday" all morning, "Day 2 · night" at midnight).

**4. Prompt-level issues (fixable in the GM prompt, free to iterate):**
- The long unsolicited "nothing needed a roll" out-of-game notes — flagged in almost every session.
- Duplicate/retroactive summary paragraphs.
- Verbal tics ("under your collar the coin is warm" ×5).
- Sessions ending mid-reveal with no closing beat — players repeatedly asked for a two-line "where things stand" recap. Relatedly, no session ended at a natural stopping point because none ended voluntarily.

**5. What the players universally loved:** the NPC voices, the cat, consequences arriving on their own schedule, failed rolls that stayed failed but still gave something, and the card temptation (every single player reported *feeling* the pull and declining it — the mechanic works on the models, not just the sheet). Don't touch these while fixing the plumbing.

**6. An outstanding obligation.** The owner promised proper endings for the 8 harness-killed sessions and any player not continued. No endings have been written yet. That promise should be honoured this week, and it costs almost nothing (short continuation turns per cut session — most died at turn 1–14, so the remaining budget per session is small).

## Proposed allocation of the $60

1. **~$0–5 — fix and verify the plumbing first.** No new science sessions until the card reads the sheet's skill line correctly, out-of-game messages pass a pending card, and the reroll targets the right card. This is engineering, plus one or two *cheap smoke-test* sessions (DeepSeek, 5–10 turns) to confirm the fixes under load — not to rate anything.

2. **~$10–15 — write the promised endings** for the 8 cut sessions, one or two turns each plus a closing beat. This also doubles as a live test of the fixed interface.

3. **~$5 — one controlled "regression test" session per fixed bug class**, short and cheap: a session engineered to trigger Persuasion +7, a reroll, and a pending-card question. Verify the whole path end-to-end before spending the rest.

4. **~$30–40 — only then, resume the comparison**, and spend it where the data says the open questions are:
   - **Table B vs Table C** (quest vs no-quest) is where the interesting model differences live — the notes diverged most there (Astra and Sol both asked for "more ordinary life" and "room to be wrong"; the Claudes thrived on the slow burn). Table A is saturated: 5 sessions produced near-identical findings.
   - **DeepSeek deserves a fair trial** — it produced arguably the two best-written notes (#27, #28) and died to the stuck rule, not to its own play. Rerun it at full length with the fixed interface.
   - **Combat is entirely untested** — every player noticed. Before the combat study scales up, one or two sessions that deliberately reach a fight would de-risk it.

If the fixes can't land within the week, my honest advice is to **spend almost nothing on new sessions** — the first 30 already established the finding, and the next 30 with the same bug would add noise, not knowledge. Bank the difference; the owner keeps what isn't spent, and that would be the right outcome.

# Revised recommendation

With the science closed, most of last week's plan is obsolete. The comparison sessions, the DeepSeek retrial, the Table B/C follow-up, the combat de-risking — none of it has a purpose anymore. What remains is one obligation and one judgment call.

## 1. The outstanding promise (do this — ~$10–15)

The owner gave his word on 25 October: everyone who was not continued gets a proper ending, no cut-off. Eight sessions died to the «stuck» rule — most of them early (turns 1–14), two of them very early (DeepSeek's one-turn Table C session). That promise is the only real deadline in the project, it's cheap to keep, and keeping it is worth doing for its own sake: several players explicitly noted the abrupt cut as the worst part of an otherwise good session (the natural-20 Religion crit that never paid off, Aldous's unanswered "Did you get his name?", the session that stopped mid-cliffhanger).

Each ending needs only one or two turns: resolve whatever card was pending, let the scene land, add the two-line "where things stand" recap that every player asked for. Write them for all eight, not just the ones with the best material — the promise was to *everyone*.

The notes are the guide for what each ending owes:

- **Claude Opus · Table C (turn 14):** the ring, the Religion answer, Ammarin's reply — the crit's payoff.
- **Opus · Table A replicate (turn 4):** the sailor's reaction to having his mark pulled, and Pip.
- **Opus · Table C replicate (turn 12):** the natural 20 on the ring's sign, Aldous's question, which way Tull rode.
- **DeepSeek's five cut sessions** (turns 1–12): these died fastest and got the least; they need the least to close — one scene beat each, ending on something chosen rather than interrupted.

## 2. Should anything else be spent? (probably not)

The case for spending more would be fixing the app for future use — the modifier bug, the stuck rule, the log errors. That's real work with real value, but only if the owner intends to run the app again. It isn't owed to the players (they gave their notes; the contract was the session and the note, and the notes exist), and it isn't owed to the science (the reports are written, and the bugs are documented in them). So this is a question for the owner, not a recommendation:

- **If the app has a future** (future studies, other players, personal use): spend ~$5 on one smoke-test session to confirm the modifier fix and the pending-card channel before anything else is run on it. The bug reports from these 30 sessions are the spec — the engineering itself doesn't consume the play budget.
- **If it doesn't:** spend nothing further. Fix nothing. Archive the transcripts, the notes, and the bug list together, since the bug list is now part of the study's findings.

## Bottom line

**~$10–15 on the eight promised endings; the remaining $45–50 stays with the owner.** The sessions produced their data and their notes; the only unpaid debt is the endings, and that debt is small, clearly scoped, and the owner's own word.
