### Answer 1 (758 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
**Recommendation: spend the week on plumbing, not on more play. Fix five things, write the promised endings, then run one small verification batch. Hold everything else.**

### Why

Thirty notes say the same thing. The fiction is already good — every player would come back — and the complaints are almost entirely about the apparatus:

- **Roll cards use the wrong modifier** in roughly 25 of 30 sessions (+2 or +4 shown beside "Persuasion +7"; "+2 Perception +1"). It flipped outcomes at least five times (Fable Table C, Sol Table B, Astra Table C ×2, Sol Table C Arcana). The GM patches it in prose but the log keeps the wrong result. DeepSeek is the one model that didn't notice — it praised the card for "working cleanly" — so it is the weakest auditor, not evidence the bug is intermittent.
- **A pending card blocks every message that isn't a button name.** That is both the thing players most wanted fixed ("I wanted to challenge the modifier *before* accepting the roll") and the thing that ended 8 of 30 sessions by the stuck rule — including three DeepSeek sessions at turns 1, 3 and 3, which were effectively wasted. These two bugs compound: the number is wrong *and* you can't say so.
- Reroll button hits the wrong card or does nothing; sessions end mid-roll; the log disagrees with the ruling.
- Sheet vs records disagree on the same list every time: Healing Light 4/5, rapier +2/+5, attunement, cat HP 2/8, rations, all-gold purse.
- GM verbosity: the four-item "none of these needed a roll" list, retrospective repeat paragraphs, answers to questions nobody asked. Unanimous.

Running more sessions now re-measures known defects, and any cross-model or cross-table comparison is confounded (stuck-rule exits correlate with model). The later studies already queued (combat, villain, same-world) will hit the cat-HP and rapier mismatches head-on in a fight. Fix first.

### The week

**Days 1–2 — harness and server (no model spend)**
1. While a card is pending, accept out-of-game text and button+note; let the player name which card to reroll. Retire the three-strikes "stuck" rule or make it warn once.
2. Card modifier read from the sheet's skill line, not the ability score / combat-profile field. Assert `card total == sheet total` before the die is thrown.
3. When the GM rules a correction, update the log entry, not just the prose.
4. One source of truth for the sheet: settle Healing Light, rapier, attunement, familiar HP, rations, mixed coin — once, in the file.
5. Harness tells the GM at turn 18 that two turns remain; GM must close on a beat plus a two-line "where things stand." Never end on an unresolved card.

**Day 3 — GM prompt (no model spend)**
One line max for "no roll needed"; no retrospective summaries; no out-of-game reply unless asked; resolve the check at the point of uncertainty before narrating consequences; pause after a failed roll ("two voices below, can't make out words") rather than narrating the whole visit away; don't decide James's spending, casting, inner state or borrowed senses for him; don't leak faction tags, private rolls or unspoken names into the records panel.

**Day 3 — the promised endings (~$5)**
The owner gave his word on 25 Oct and nothing has been written. One closing GM turn per cut session (the 8 stuck-rule sessions, Opus at turn 14, and any player not being continued) picks up exactly where each stopped — the natural 20 on the ring, the gate at the Basilisk, the Insight on the sailor — and lands it. Cheap, and it should come first in the spend because it's a debt.

**Days 4–5 — verification batch (~$35, cap $40)**
One table only (B — it exposed the most machinery). Two 20-turn sessions each for Opus, Fable, Astra, Sol; two short DeepSeek sessions purely to confirm nothing gets stuck. Pass criteria: zero card/sheet mismatches, zero stuck-rule exits, every session ends on a beat with a recap, no "none of these needed a roll" lists. If it passes, the queued combat/villain/same-world studies can resume the following week with clean data.

**Leave ~$15–20 unspent.** Nothing here needs more.

### Not this week, but write it down
The content requests are consistent and worth a design pass later: Table A's heist/crew premise never surfaces by turn 20; players repeatedly ask for the card ban to be pressed harder ("a weakness you never test isn't a weakness"); several warlocks went a whole session without a levelled spell because "a strange spellcaster frightens people" read as a prohibition — soften that line in the canon file.
```

### Answer 2 (442 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
**Revised for $30: the fixes still cost nothing; the endings stay; the verification batch shrinks to the minimum that can still catch a regression.**

The core of the plan doesn't change, because the first three days were never model spend. What changes is how much play buys the proof.

### Days 1–3 — fixes, prompt, endings (as before)

1–5 (card modifier from the sheet line, messages through a pending card, log follows the ruling, one source of truth for the sheet, turn-18 warning + close on a beat) and the GM-prompt trims — **$0**.

**The promised endings — ~$5.** One closing GM turn per cut-short session. This stays. It is the owner's word and it is the cheapest line on the sheet; it goes first.

### Day 4 — verification without a model (~$0)

Before any play: replay the roll-card requests from the 30 logged sessions through the fixed server and diff `card total` against `sheet total`. That tests bug #2 across ~70 real cards for free — far better coverage than a new batch would give. Likewise feed the logged "?"-while-pending messages through the fixed harness and confirm they're delivered.

### Day 5 — one small live batch (~$20, hard cap $22)

Play is now only for things the replay can't check: the GM's prose behaviour, the turn-18 close, and the stuck rule in a live loop.

- **Table B only.** Two full 20-turn sessions: **Claude Opus 5.5** and **GPT-6 Astra** — one from each family, the two sharpest auditors (both flagged modifiers, leaks and pending-card blocking unprompted). Skip Fable and Sol this round; their complaints were the same ones.
- **Two DeepSeek sessions capped at 5 turns** — pure smoke test that it no longer stalls at turn 1–3, since it's the model that trips the stuck rule and won't tell you about it.

Pass criteria unchanged: zero card/sheet mismatches, zero stuck exits, every session ends on a beat with a recap, no "none of these needed a roll" lists, no leaked tags or names in the records panel.

**Leave ~$3–5 unspent** as margin for a re-run if one of the two full sessions dies on infrastructure (the container-restart / two-hour-limit kills on 27 Oct are a known risk).

### What this gives up

Fable and Sol aren't re-verified this week; if the fixes pass on Opus and Astra, run them first when the queued combat/villain/same-world studies resume. No model comparison is possible from a batch this size — that was never the point of this week. Content changes (heist surfacing on Table A, pressing the card ban, softening "a strange spellcaster frightens people") stay written down for later; none of them are blocked by budget, only by sequence.
```
