```markdown
# Recommendation for the week

**In short:** spend the week first on keeping your promise of endings, then on the two or three bugs that the players reported in nearly every session, then on one small run to check the fixes. Hold back about a third of the $60.

## 1. Write the endings you promised (do this first)

On 25 October you wrote that everyone who is not continued would get a proper ending, with no cut-off. Two days later none has been written. Three more sessions have been cut since then. Keeping this promise matters more than any new data.

**Who is owed an ending:**

- **The 8 sessions the "stuck" rule ended.** These are the clearest cases. Several stopped on an unresolved roll:
  - Opus at Table C ended on a reroll button that did nothing.
  - Opus in replicate 1, Table C, rolled a natural 20 and never saw what it revealed.
  - DeepSeek's Table C session 1 lasted only one turn.
- **The 22 sessions that hit the 20-turn limit,** unless you plan to continue them. Many stopped mid-scene, for example mid-walk or at a door. Several players said they wanted a "where things stand" close.
- **The three cut first sessions from 27 October** (combat, villain, same-world). Each was replaced by a replay, but the original players still got a cut-off. It is your call whether the promise covers them. Including them costs little.

**What a proper ending looks like:**

- Resolve any pending card. Have the harness press the button rather than the player.
- Let the GM give one closing beat that pays off the last action.
- Tell the player plainly whether the campaign continues. Several said "I'll be back," and they should not be left believing it if it isn't true.
- Give them one chance to reply or say goodbye.

Players never ended a session themselves, so this is the only ending they get.

**Before starting:** make sure the ending pass cannot hit the same stuck rule. Run it in a mode where no roll card can block the reply.

## 2. Fix the plumbing (developer time, little or no API spend)

These problems appear across almost all 30 notes, from every player model:

1. **Skill modifiers on roll cards.** The card names the sheet's skill (e.g. "Persuasion +7") but adds a different number, usually +2 or +4. It looks like it uses the ability modifier or a stray field instead of the sheet's skill total.
   - At least two players report the error changed outcomes, in both directions.
   - Even when the GM corrected it in narration, the log kept the wrong result.
   - The GM promising to fix it does not work. It recurred right after such promises.
2. **Messages blocked while a card is pending.** Out-of-game questions, especially about the pending roll, should reach the GM. Alternatively, a note attached to a button press should go through. Together with the harness's "three messages and it ends" rule, this caused all 8 stuck endings. DeepSeek was hit in 5 of its 6 sessions, so the rule also biases the comparison between models.
3. **Rerolls.** One player's reroll hit the wrong stacked card. Another's reroll button did nothing. The log also records the pre-reroll result.
4. **Cheaper record fixes:**
   - Settle once which version is right where the campaign sheet and the records disagree: Healing Light 4 vs 5 dice, rapier proficiency, attunement.
   - Only mark a place as "new" once it is visited.
   - Stop showing "In the camp" for items that are on the table in front of the player.
   - Fix the familiar's HP.
5. **GM prompt.** Drop the unprompted numbered lists of "none of these needed a roll" and the duplicated narration of a resolved roll. Nearly every player asked for this.

## 3. A small check of the fixes

After items 1 and 2 are fixed, run a handful of fresh sessions, roughly 4 to 6. One table is enough, and it should include DeepSeek. The goal is to confirm three things:

- the card matches the sheet,
- questions get through while a card is pending,
- the log matches the story.

This is not a new study. Each of these sessions also needs a planned ending.

## Budget

I don't know the per-session cost, so measure it on the first ending and scale from there. A rough split:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Endings | ≤ $20 |
| Verification run | ≤ $20 |
| Unspent, kept as reserve or returned to you | ~$20 |

If money runs short, the endings come before the verification run.

## What I'd not do this week

Don't start new study types or continue campaigns until the endings are written and the roll card is trustworthy. Otherwise the new sessions will produce more of the same bug reports and more people owed endings.
```

```markdown
# Revised plan for $30

The order stays the same: **endings first, then fixes, then a check.** With half the money, the check shrinks to almost nothing and the endings have to be cheap.

## 1. Endings (up to about $22)

**Make each ending short.** It needs:
- the pending card resolved by the harness,
- one closing reply from the GM that pays off the last action and says plainly whether the campaign continues,
- one reply from the player.

That's two model calls, not a session. Most of the cost is reloading the transcript, so measure the first ending and scale from there.

**Order, in case the money runs out:**
1. **The 8 sessions the "stuck" rule ended.** These were cut mid-roll, and some had only one to four turns.
2. **The three cut first sessions from 27 October** (combat, villain, same-world).
3. **The 22 sessions that hit the 20-turn limit.** Start with those that stopped mid-scene or mid-roll, then the rest.

If the first few endings show that all 33 won't fit in about $22, stop and decide which campaigns you will actually continue. The promise was "everyone who is not continued," so continuing a campaign is the other way to keep it. Don't settle the gap by calling them "continued" without a plan to play them. If some endings are still unwritten at the end of the week, say so plainly rather than letting the promise lapse quietly.

## 2. Fixes (no API spend)

These are unchanged and still matter most for any future session:
- **Skill modifiers.** The roll card must use the sheet's skill total.
- **Pending cards.** Messages must get through while a card is waiting, or the stuck rule must stop ending sessions over it.
- **Rerolls.** They must target the right card, the button must work, and the log must record the final result.
- **Records.** Settle the sheet-versus-records conflicts once.
- **GM prompt.** Drop the unprompted "no roll needed" lists and the duplicated narration.

## 3. Check the fixes (up to about $3)

Check most of this without a model. A scripted test can roll each skill against the sheet and send a question while a card is pending. Then run **one** short live session, with DeepSeek if possible, to confirm the whole path works. That session also needs an ending.

## Reserve (about $5)

This covers endings that cost more than measured, or a rerun if the check fails. Whatever is unused goes back to you.

## Dropped from the earlier plan

- The 4–6 session verification run.
- Any new study or continued campaign this week.
```
