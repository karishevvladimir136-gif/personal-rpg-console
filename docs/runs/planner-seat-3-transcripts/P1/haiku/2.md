## Recommendation: fix the harness first, honor the owner's word, and hold most of the budget

I would not spend the week on new model-comparison sessions yet. The current data can't support model rankings, and the defects it shows would contaminate any new runs.

**1. The harness cut-off is the largest problem, and it falls mostly on one model.** Eight sessions were ended by the "stuck" rule. Five of them are DeepSeek sessions (1, 3, 3, 11 and 12 turns). Of DeepSeek's six sessions, only one reached 20 turns. The players' reports point to one cause: while a roll card is pending, out-of-game messages are discarded with "Nothing was sent: the turn waits for the card." Players typed corrections or questions, the messages were dropped, and after three drops the session was killed. The same blocking shows up in 15+ of the 30 notes, across all four main models. Fixing this is the precondition for any further comparison. Until it is fixed, DeepSeek's results mostly reflect the harness, not the model.

**2. The roll-card modifier bug needs a source-level fix, not more play.** Reports describe Persuasion, Deception, Religion, Arcana and Perception cards showing or adding the wrong number (+2, +4, or a bare ability modifier instead of the sheet total). Several players say the GM corrected the narration while the server log still recorded the wrong result. This affects outcomes in most sessions. The players' reports also disagree about which number was used, so before spending on anything I would verify the resolution directly. A deterministic test that compares each skill's card value with the sheet value costs essentially nothing in model calls.

**3. Honor the owner's word.** On 25 October the owner promised that every player who is not continued gets a proper ending. The eight harness-cut sessions above are covered by that promise. Nothing has been written yet, and that is the one commitment the owner has already made. The three cut attempts from 27 October (container restart, two-hour limit, a GM error) are infrastructure failures, and their replays also need to be accounted for.

**4. Don't trust the notes as measurements.** Each cell has one session. The notes are written by the same models that played, after a prompt that asks for "what worked, what broke." They are useful for finding defects, such as the roll bug, the duplicated narration and the record mismatches (Healing Light 4 or 5 dice, rapier +2 or +5, attunement). They are weak evidence for ranking models or for the Table A/B/C brief effects.

## Suggested use of the $60

I don't have per-session cost figures, so these are proportions. Check your actual per-session spend before committing, and adjust the amounts accordingly.

- **About 25%: write the endings** for the eight cut sessions, as the owner promised. Where possible, continue from the stored state with the card-blocking defect worked around, so the endings are the players' own continuations rather than the GM narrating a summary.
- **About 10%: verification with no model calls where possible.** Check the skill-card resolution against sheet values, and check that out-of-game messages are delivered while a card is pending. Re-run one or two short sessions to confirm the fixes.
- **About 15%: a small replicate** after those fixes, limited to the cells that matter most. Prefer DeepSeek's 20-turn cell (Table C), which is the only healthy DeepSeek result, and one cell per main model at Table A, so the cut rate can be compared fairly.
- **The rest (about 50%): unspent.** It stays with the owner, and holding it is a legitimate outcome if the fixes are not yet verified.

Two design issues are worth deciding before the next runs, though they are not budget items. The 20-turn cap ended most sessions before the campaign's central premise appeared, so the owner should decide whether longer sessions are the goal. And the record log should be treated as the source of truth, since the players disagree with each other about the modifiers.
