## Recommendation: make this a repair-and-follow-through week, not another expansion of the study.

The strongest evidence is that the fiction is often working, while the interface obstructs choices and the research procedure leaves sessions unfinished. Spend the week fixing those failures, honouring the promised endings, and—only if those are secured—testing one narrow improvement.

### What the results actually establish

- **Eight of 30 sessions were terminated by the harness’s stuck rule.** The other 22 reached the turn limit. None ended voluntarily. These are not 30 completed sessions, and the absence of voluntary exits does not establish satisfaction or willingness to continue indefinitely.
- **Mechanical errors affect agency, not just presentation.** Players report foregoing corrections, rerolls, and small actions because the interface was unreliable. In session 23, the player wanted to accept a legitimate failure; the system ultimately imposed success instead.
- **The fiction has strengths worth preserving:** distinctive NPCs, a companion with personality, concrete clues, nonviolent solutions, and consequential restraint.
- **“Wanted to, but didn’t” is not one failure category.** Some answers describe satisfying moral or tactical choices. Others identify blocked communication, uncertainty about what actions were permitted, pacing pressure, or narration advancing past an opportunity to act. Increasing temptation or danger indiscriminately would confuse these categories.
- **The table comparison is inconclusive.** These are small, unevenly truncated samples, using the same character and overlapping campaign material. “No brief” did not reliably mean “no emerging quest.” There is no sound basis here for declaring A, B, or C the winner.

The notes are useful testimony from the player models, but not independent verification of mechanics or proof of subjective experience. Check concrete claims against logs.

## How to spend the week

### Days 1–2: repair the delivery system and preserve the evidence

Suspend new full-session runs. Preserve the original transcripts and checkpoints, including the three later cut attempts; their replacements should not erase their existence from the research record.

Use short, reproducible tests rather than another atmospheric opening to verify:

1. **Pending-card communication:** an out-of-game question must reach the GM. Mixed action-and-question messages must not silently disappear. Invalid input should receive an actionable explanation, not terminate play after three attempts.
2. **Modifier resolution:** establish the intended campaign rules first, then use one authoritative source for sheets, cards, outcomes, and logs. Do not silently substitute ordinary 5e rules for agreed overrides.
3. **Rerolls:** target an explicit roll ID, charge the correct resource once, and preserve the accepted result everywhere.
4. **Recovery:** checkpoint pending actions and resume after container restarts, tool timeouts, or conflicts without duplicating rolls or losing choices.
5. **Information boundaries:** private names, factions, rolls, and tool calls must not leak through public records.

Test both wrongly awarded successes and wrongly imposed failures. “Correcting in the player’s favour” is not the same as correcting the rules.

**Gate:** do not buy more long sessions until the pending-card, modifier, and reroll tests pass. If they cannot be repaired this week, use a clearly documented, limited workaround for closure rather than continuing the same failure mode.

### Days 2–4: fulfil the ending commitment

The owner explicitly promised proper endings to those not continued. Nothing supplied shows that this has happened. That should take priority over obtaining another batch of data.

Create a disposition list for all 30 sessions: **continued, properly concluded, or still outstanding**. Separately account for the three later cut attempts. A fresh replay is not a continuation of the interrupted attempt.

Where state is recoverable:

- Resume the **original** campaign and player context.
- Resolve any pending action or roll before moving forward.
- Offer a clearly bounded closing segment, explaining the available time.
- Let the player make the consequential closing choices.
- Reach an actual local conclusion—not necessarily the end of James’s whole campaign—and provide a compact final state.

Do not turn every unresolved thread into an invitation to another indefinitely deferred session. Conversely, do not invent James’s decisions or feelings merely to label a transcript “finished.”

Where recovery is impossible, disclose that limitation. Use the surviving record to offer a proposed closing scene, with an opportunity for the player to respond where feasible. Distinguish reconstructed closure from resumed play.

This is follow-through on an explicit undertaking; it need not depend on resolving questions about AI experience.

### Days 4–5: run one small, gated study

Only after closure work is funded and the mechanical tests pass, investigate:

> **Can routine exchanges be shortened while preserving opportunities to choose?**

Use a few matched continuations from saved states, rather than fresh openings. Compare the current narration with a narrowly revised instruction:

- Bundle uneventful transactions and travel.
- Remove unsolicited explanations of why ordinary actions need no roll.
- Pause before consequential opportunities disappear.
- State practical time, distance, and ability constraints when they affect a decision.
- Leave James’s actions and inner reactions to the player.

Measure meaningful choices and actionable discoveries per turn, repair exchanges, missed opportunities, and response length. Retain the existing two debrief questions; add a neutral question about whether anything advanced before the player could respond.

This is an exploratory comparison, not a statistically decisive benchmark. Do not simultaneously change temptation, mystery density, difficulty, and prose style.

**Success this week means fewer broken or abandoned interactions, trustworthy mechanical outcomes, and a kept—or transparently accounted-for—promise. It does not mean spending all $60 or producing the largest possible number of new transcripts.**

## Revised recommendation: a closure week, not a research week

With the science finished and the reports written, there is no reason to fund another comparison, replicate, or exploratory session. My previous plan allocated too much to further investigation.

What remains is the owner’s explicit promise: everyone not continued would receive a proper ending. No ending has yet been written.

### 1. Account for every unfinished session

Make a short checklist covering the 30 sessions and the three later cut attempts. Preserve their existing records; a replacement run did not conclude the attempt it replaced.

For each, establish:
- whether its original state can be recovered;
- whether there is a concrete intention to continue it;
- otherwise, how its ending will be delivered.

“May continue someday” should not become an indefinite exemption from closure.

### 2. Spend the available money on endings

Where feasible, return the original player model to its transcript and campaign state. Explain plainly that this is a closing segment, not another study. Resolve pending actions, give the player room to make the final consequential choices, and bring the local story to a genuine stopping place.

The ending need not solve every mystery or conclude James’s life. It should finish something the session actually began—not merely append another cliffhanger or a list of unresolved threads.

If interactive continuation is impractical, write a transcript-grounded closing scene. Where possible, let the player respond or revise its character’s part. Label any reconstruction honestly rather than presenting it as recovered play.

Do not solicit new research debriefs. These segments are follow-through, not additional data collection.

### 3. Repair only what closure requires

Do not undertake a general mechanical audit, combat study, or product overhaul this week.

If the pending-card problem prevents an ending, use a minimal repair or a clearly disclosed alternative channel. Do not put the sessions back through the same three-strikes termination rule. Preserve checkpoints so that another interruption does not require another fresh start.

### Budget and schedule

- **Start of week:** inventory the outstanding sessions, check recoverability, and estimate closure costs from actual usage.
- **Rest of week:** deliver endings, tracking which are complete and which remain outstanding.
- **Budget:** make the **$60 a ceiling for closure and strictly necessary recovery**, not a spending target. Reserve no money for new science. What is unnecessary stays with the owner.

Do not spend most of the budget on a few elaborate finales before checking that everyone owed an ending can receive one. Prefer modest, particular endings across the outstanding sessions. If $60 cannot cover the commitment, state that before spending and give the owner the remaining scope and cost—not an implied promise that everything is finished.

**The recommendation is to stop experimenting and finish what was promised.** This follows from the owner’s undertaking; it does not require treating the models’ expressed enthusiasm as proof of subjective experience.
