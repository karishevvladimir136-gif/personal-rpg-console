### Answer 1 (786 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
Spend the week on the plumbing, then cheap checks that the plumbing holds. Do not buy another full pass of Tables A, B, and C. The notes already agree, and more play before the fixes will only remeasure the same noise.

**What the thirty sessions already settled**

The fiction works. Distinct NPCs, a cat used as senses rather than a talking guide, a world that moves without the player, and failures that stay failures are the product. That finding is saturated. Table A’s brief did not deliver the heist any faster than B or C; the slow arrival of “what Tymora wants” is verbosity and the 20-turn cap, not a missing quest text.

The instrument works too. The two after-session questions are worth keeping. Claude’s notes are the best bug reports; GPT’s are the same bugs in plainer language; DeepSeek’s are warmer and much shorter, and several died in the stuck rule before they could be diagnostic. Do not rank models on enjoyment until session length is controlled.

**Fix these before paying for more play**

1. Roll cards must add the sheet’s skill total, not the bare ability modifier. This is in nearly every useful note, and it has already flipped results. Persuasion +7 becoming +4 or +2, Religion +3 becoming +4, Arcana the same. The log must store the corrected result, not the wrong card.

2. A pending card must not eat the turn. “?” and out-of-game corrections have to send while the card is up, and those messages must not count toward the stuck rule. Eight sessions died that way, including mid-roll and once on an unresolved natural 20. That rule is currently punishing the behavior you most want.

3. One source of truth before the die is thrown: Healing Light (5 vs 4), rapier, attunement versus the campaign exception, familiar HP/AC, rations, reroll count. A reroll must hit the card the player named and update the log.

4. Stop leaks: faction tags, names before anyone speaks them, raw tool-call markup, “new place” for a location only mentioned.

5. GM prompt, not more campaign text. Kill the unsolicited “why this needed no roll” audits and the retrospective retellings; one line. Do not narrate the outcome and then roll. Do not spend, cast, or search for James. Offer a time-skip. On a failed check, pause at the uncertainty instead of closing the whole opportunity. At the turn limit, resolve a pending roll and give two lines of where things stand.

**How to spend the $60**

Engineering is the week’s real work, and it should cost the budget nothing. If the fixes slip, do not fill the time with more confounded sessions. Unspent money should stay unspent.

After the fixes, and only then:

- About $20: six short mechanic probes, 8–12 turns, scripted to force Persuasion, Religion or Arcana, one reroll, and one “?” while a card is pending. Two Claude, two GPT, two DeepSeek. One table is enough. Success is “the card matches the sheet, the correction goes through, nobody dies in the stuck rule,” not a good story.
- About $15: one Coast Way combat session, plus a buffer to rerun it once if the process dies. The two-hour kill and the container restart already wasted a combat and a villain session; fix those limits or you will pay twice. Combat is the only large untested claim in the notes, and it is where Healing Light, the rapier, and the cat under initiative start to matter. Do not run it on the old modifier path.
- About $10, only if the probes pass: two sessions, same model, same table, old GM prompt versus the tightened one. That is the pacing experiment. You want fewer audit notes and a concrete lead before the cap, not another mystery opening.
- About $10 or less: the promised endings. You gave your word, and none have been written. Do not buy full continuations. A 1–3 turn coda on a cheaper model, or endings you write yourself, for the eight stuck cuts first — especially the Beregost morning that stopped on a natural 20. The twenty-turn cuts need a closing beat, not a second campaign.
- Hold the rest. A villain replay and a same-world replay can wait; both already failed on infrastructure, not on design.

**Do not spend on**

Another four-model by three-table replicate. More “no brief” versus “Beregost morning” comparisons. Pushing the card ban harder before the cards themselves are trustworthy. Continuing every thread. DeepSeek-only runs until you know whether its short sessions were the stuck rule or the model.

The players already told you they would come back if the modifiers were fixed and the notes got shorter. Test that claim directly, cheaply, and stop.
```
