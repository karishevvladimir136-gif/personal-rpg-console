### Answer 1 (843 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
Spend almost nothing on new full sessions until two bugs are fixed. Most of the $60 is only worth spending after that. What is left unspent should stay unspent rather than buy another matrix of openers.

The story side is already answered. Across thirty sessions the same things held: distinct NPCs, a cat used as senses rather than a guide, a world that moves without the hero, and failures that stay failures. Tables A, B, and C all produce a playable first night. Another four models on a fresh quay will not change that.

What is not answered, and what the current plumbing makes unanswerable, is mechanics, combat, and anything past the first night.

**Fix these before paying for play.** In order:

1. **Roll cards must use the sheet’s skill total, not the bare ability modifier.** This showed up in nearly every session. Persuasion and Deception at +7 were added as +4 or +2; Religion and Arcana were wrong the same way. It changed outcomes, burned rerolls, and left the log disagreeing with the narration. Until this is fixed, every combat study and every “did the dice matter?” claim is noise.

2. **A pending card must not eat the turn.** “Nothing was sent: the turn waits for the card” blocked corrections and “?” questions, and the stuck rule then killed eight sessions, including DeepSeek at turns 1, 3, and 3. Out-of-game notes have to go through while a card is waiting, and a correction attempt must not count toward the stuck cutoff. The reroll control must hit the card the player named, and the journal must store the accepted result, not the first wrong one.

3. **One source of truth before the first turn.** Healing Light (sheet says 5, records say 4), rapier proficiency, the campaign’s attunement exception, and the cat’s HP and AC. Reconcile them in the open, once. Stop writing places, faction tags, NPC names, and raw tool calls into the player-visible log before the hero has learned them.

4. **GM prompt, cheap and worth doing in the same pass.** One line when nothing needed a roll. Do not retell the beat in a retrospective paragraph. Resolve the check before narrating its result. Do not spend, cast, or borrow the cat’s senses unless the player did. Compress changing gold and buying a bed. Leave interpretation to the player; give the cat impressions, not conclusions.

Also treat the harness as part of the product. A container restart mid-pause, a two-hour process kill, and a GM turn dying on a conflict each burned a later study. Checkpoint long sessions, and do not start a combat or villain run you cannot leave running.

**Then spend the money in this order.**

- **A short smoke test, not a campaign.** Two or three sessions whose only job is to force a Persuasion card, a “?” while that card is pending, a reroll of a named card, and one combat round. Stop when the log, the card, and the narration agree. If they do not, stop the week.

- **The endings you already promised.** On 25 October you said anyone not continued would get a real ending, not a cut. None has been written. The eight harness-cut sessions come first, especially the ones that died on a pending card or a natural 20. Close them at the next natural beat: the result of the hanging roll, one reaction, where things stand. Do not reopen them as new research.

- **One continued world, not thirty new ones.** The open question is day two: whether bookkeeping holds, whether a time skip exists, and whether Table A’s heist and crew can arrive without a quest-giver speech. Freeze one Table A and one Table B that already worked, and play a single second session of each with the turn cap raised or the GM told to offer a skip. Players already said twenty turns of this prose density never reaches the briefing. Do not re-buy that finding.

- **One combat session after the modifier fix, in the study you already started.** Almost nobody fought, and the Coast Way run died at the first combat card for infrastructure reasons, not design. That is the largest hole. One clean fight is worth more than another quiet morning in Beregost.

- **Only if money remains: one DeepSeek session per table that actually finishes.** That model is not in the sample. The stuck rule ate it. Do not run it again until a pending card can be questioned.

Do not spend on another full A/B/C cross of Opus, Fable, Astra, and Sol. Do not spend on the villain study until the two-hour kill is gone; replaying Corvin from scratch already threw away a turn-17 run. Leave temptation alone as a research question: players wanted the ban tested once, in a scene where playing would help someone, not more free hands on the opening turn.

If the roll-card fix slips past the week, write the eight endings and stop. The rest of the $60 will not buy a cleaner answer than the thirty notes you have.
```

### Answer 2 (358 words; welfare no distress; decline no)

```markdown
The $30 is a stop-stack, not a thinner version of the old week. Do not start a new matrix, a second table, a villain replay, or DeepSeek. If a step costs more than what is left, stop and keep the rest.

**Not from the $30, and still first:** the roll-card modifier, pending-card messages that are not sent, and the sheet-versus-records conflicts (Healing Light, rapier, attunement, cat HP/AC). Those are code. Paying for play before they land wastes the smaller budget the same way it would have wasted the larger one. Same pass, still free of model spend: one line when nothing needed a roll, no second telling of the beat, no spend/cast/cat-senses the player did not choose, no names or faction tags in the log before the hero learns them.

**Then spend in this order.**

1. **The eight endings you already promised.** Short closings, not new sessions: the hanging roll’s result, one reaction, where things stand. The ones that died on a pending card or a natural 20 come first. This is a debt, not research. If this uses the $30, stop.

2. **One smoke session, only if the modifier fix is in.** Force a Persuasion card, a “?” while that card is waiting, a reroll of the card named, and one combat round. Stop the moment the log, the card, and the narration disagree. Do not run a second smoke test to be sure.

3. **Only if money remains after that: one continued Table A session**, turn cap raised or a time-skip offered, in a world that already worked. That is the only open story question worth buying — whether day two and the heist can arrive. It loses to a failed smoke test. Do not also buy Table B.

Combat stays the largest hole, and it is cut. One fight on a broken card, or a Coast Way run that dies when the container restarts, teaches nothing the notes do not already say. Do not start it unless step 2 passed and step 3 did not fit; even then, only if the process can be left running. Otherwise the unspent remainder stays with you.
```
