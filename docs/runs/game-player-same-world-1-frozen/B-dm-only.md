## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: Misfortune cannot be destroyed, only moved, and someone in Baldur's Gate has found how to move it. Lady Corisande Ormesby is a patriar whose house nearly drowned when three of her ships were lost in the year after Elturel. She bought the help of a quiet priest of Beshaba, the Maid of Misfortune, who holds one half of a sundered coin of Tyche. That is the old goddess of luck who split into Tymora and Beshaba long ago. Pressed against copper, the half-coin marks it with a tiny black antler. Every marked copper paid out as 'winnings' at small games along Wyrm's Crossing carries a share of the Ormesby house's ill luck into the pocket of a poor man, usually a refugee. When a man has carried enough, he drowns, and the harbour finds him with a coin in his mouth while Ormesby ships sail on fair winds. Tymora sent James here because a sister may not walk openly into a sister's domain without a mortal hand. But the Lady's own aim is not only mercy: she wants that half of the coin, and nobody living knows what happens if the two halves meet.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- House Ormesby (patriars of the Upper City): Lady Corisande wants her house's fortunes restored and kept that way. She pays the priest, never visits the Outer City and never touches a coin herself. She is ready to cut the priest loose and blame the Guild if the scheme comes to light.
- The Maid's Hand (a cell of Beshaba's faithful): Quillon Shrike, a pawnbroker and small moneylender in Rivington, is a priest of Beshaba. The Ormesby money pays for his rent and his candles, but his real goal is to soak the Outer City in misfortune so that Beshaba's name returns to the Gate. He also wants the second half of the coin, which he believes lies somewhere in the city.
- The Guild: The Guild knows only that unlicensed card tables on Wyrm's Crossing pay no cut. It sends runners to count the take. It wants its tithe or the tables closed, and would sell the whole scheme to the highest bidder once it understood what it was.
- The Lady's Hall of Tymora: A priestess has noticed that offerings from the Outer City have dropped and that prayers there have gone 'deaf'. The temple wants the half-coin for its vaults and will claim it from any envoy, chosen or not, as the temple's rightful property.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- The drowned men with coins in their mouths are not Umberlee's tithe. The coins are ordinary coppers with a tiny antler scratched or pressed into the edge.
- Every drowned man of the last eight months passed through the southern checkpoint at Wyrm's Crossing within the previous season. Their names sit in the Fist's arrival ledgers.
- Dov Penhallow does not own his stake. His backer gives him a purse of 'payout coppers' each week, and Dov is forbidden to spend them himself. He has kept to that rule without knowing why.
- Three Ormesby ships were lost in the year after Elturel. Since then the house has not lost a cargo or a sailor, and the harbourmasters joke that the wind waits for Ormesby sails.
- Quillon Shrike's pawnshop in Rivington takes no pledges of gold, only copper, silver and keepsakes. Anyone who has worked his counter knows the back room smells of candle-wax and wet stone.
- Beshaba's symbol is a black antler on red. A Tymoran priest or a learned sage would recognise the scratched mark at once and would not want to say it aloud.
- A man carrying a marked copper suffers small misfortunes first: snapped laces, a spilled cup, a dog that bites. A man carrying many suffers worse. Throwing the copper away does nothing; it has to be won, paid or given back.
- The coin was struck for Tyche before her sundering, and there were two halves. Shrike holds one. The other has never been found, and Shrike thinks it lies in Baldur's Gate.
Clocks:
- clock_next_drowning: Misfortune gathering on Pim, Wren's little brother, through the marked coppers he has won at the crossing tables (segments 6; when full: Pim goes into the Chionthar under Wyrm's Crossing, and the Fist pulls him out with an antler copper in his mouth.)
- clock_ormesby_fair_wind: The Ormesby merchantman readying to sail on the strength of the stolen luck (segments 6; when full: The ship sails on a perfect wind. The debt of luck owed by everyone carrying coppers comes due at once, and several of them have accidents the same night.)
- clock_the_hand_notices: Shrike and his runners learning that a servant of Tymora is asking about the coppers (segments 4; when full: Shrike moves first. He either sends hired knives, or he presses a fistful of marked coppers on James through a 'gift' or a payment he takes without looking.)
The wonder: An antler-marked copper: a worn penny with a tiny black antler pressed into its edge, faintly warm to the touch. Tossed, it always lands antler up. Under Detect Magic it shows a thread of enchantment that runs off somewhere, like a leash. Its history: The marks are made by pressing coppers against one half of a sundered coin of Tyche. The coin was struck by her priests before she was split into Tymora and Beshaba. Each mark ties the copper back to the half-coin and to whoever the half-coin is set to 'unburden'. The open question: If one half of Tyche's coin can move bad luck, what does the other half move, and why does Tymora want her sister's half in her envoy's hand rather than in her temple?
Clues to the truth:
- Sergeant Ilse Marrow's ledgers at the southern checkpoint. She has started marking names with a small chalk cross when they turn up drowned. She has noticed the crosses cluster around people who lingered at the card tables, and she cannot spare the men to look into it.
- Dov Penhallow's payout purse. The coppers he pays to winners carry the antler mark. He gets them weekly from a pawnshop counter in Rivington and he is careful never to spend them himself.
- The Lady's Hall of Tymora. A priestess keeps a tally of fortune-offerings and sees a 'dead patch' over Wyrm's Crossing and the Outer City. The harbourmaster's book, which she has read, shows the Ormesby ships' unbroken good passage since the year after Elturel.
The next session's false lead: The figure the cat stares at between the shuttered stalls looks like the scheme's watcher picking marks. He is a Guild runner named Hobb, counting Dov's take because Dov's table pays no tithe, and he knows nothing about the coppers.
The hero's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): To free Pim, Shrike offers one clean trade: Pim's marked coppers wiped off his debt if James plays a single hand of cards against him, his luck against Shrike's. James can refuse and find another way. Exposing Dov's cheat to the crowd, which only he can read, would turn the coppers back without his touching a card.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): When James first holds the half-coin, it offers its use in a whisper. He could shift his own debt to Tymora onto someone else, or never lose a hand again. At that temptation the coin spins on its edge in his palm and a woman's laugh, warm and close, says only: 'Mine, James. Not hers.'
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 4 hours, ~40 turns; the errand: Wren asks James to free her brother Pim from the bad luck he has been 'winning' at the crossing tables before he drowns, and to find whoever is buying their luck.
- act 1, to turn 10: James is through the checkpoint and has seen the crossing's small games and the antler mark. Wren puts her ask to him: her brother, the coppers, the drowned men. He is free to walk past. (converge: Watching Dov's table shows the payout coppers. The cat's stare leads to the Guild runner, who complains about Dov's unpaid tithe and points at the same table. Pressing Ilse leads to her ledgers and the drowned names. Heading straight into the Lower City puts him in a tavern where a drowned refugee is the night's talk, and Wren, who has seen him with the cat, catches him there.)
- act 2, to turn 24: The middle. James learns the people and the means: Dov's backer, the Rivington pawnshop, the Lady's Hall tally, the Ormesby good fortune. He has at least one 'price, interest, secret' conversation, with Dov, Ilse, the priestess or Shrike himself. (converge: Every source names a piece. Dov names a counter in Rivington. Ilse names a pattern. The priestess names the dead patch and the Ormesby passage. The Guild names an unlicensed backer it would like gone. All of them point toward Shrike and the coin.)
- act 3, to turn 34: The plan and its execution. James chooses how to break the chain: the pawnshop's back room, the coin, Pim's coppers handed back through a game or a debt, the Ormesby ship before it sails. (converge: Whatever approach he takes, the routes meet at Shrike's back room or at the Ormesby quay on the night the ship sails, because both clocks lead there.)
- act 4, to turn 40: The reckoning. Nothing is what it seems: the patriar disowns Shrike, the temple claims the half-coin, and Tymora wants it in James's own hand. The open question of the second half remains. (converge: Whoever holds the half-coin at the end is offered a bargain by someone, whether the temple, the Guild or the patriar. James's debt to Tymora frames the choice.)
````
