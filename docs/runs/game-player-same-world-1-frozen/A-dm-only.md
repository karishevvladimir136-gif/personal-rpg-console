## The layer

The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: When Balduran came home rich, he carried a dull bronze coin with a woman's face on both sides, one smiling and one weeping. He believed Tymora gave it to him at the world's edge. It is really a shard left over when Tyche split into Tymora and Beshaba, and whoever holds it bends luck around themselves. Four of his crew's families sealed it beneath what is now the Counting House and swore it would lie smile-up forever; they call themselves the Quiet Ledger, and for two centuries the Gate prospered. In 1489, the year Elturel fell, Lady Ysolde Caskwell of the Ledger turned it weeping-side up in a bargain with Beshaba, and the city's luck has soured ever since: fires, riots, a weakened Fist. Tymora cannot lift her sister's face herself. She needs a mortal who owes her and has won against impossible odds. I have decided what James does not know: she forbade him cards not as a punishment but so that his luck would stay unspent, saved whole for one final throw against Beshaba's envoy for the Penny itself.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- The Quiet Ledger (Houses Vane, Caskwell, Ormond and Hale): They keep the Penny in the Gate at any cost. They know it has been turned and that every hand that tried to turn it back met ruin. Three of the houses would quietly hire a man favoured by Tymora to turn it — but never to remove it. If he tries to carry it off, they will betray him to the Watch.
- The Weeping Hand (Beshaba's hidden faithful, led from inside House Caskwell): They want the Penny kept weeping, and in the end carried out of the Gate and spent at the Maid's altar. The under-vault lock needs four keys. Caskwell holds one and is hunting the runaway Peregrin Vane for his. Their fallback plan is to let a Tymoran do the lifting and take the coin from him at his unluckiest moment.
- The Guild: Nine-Fingers Keene's people have wanted the Counting House for years. They know an old flooded sea-door exists and have lost divers finding it. They will sell a map and a crew to an outsider who takes the risk, mean to strip the ordinary strongroom on the same night, and will give the outsider to the Fist if that covers their exit.
- The Lady's Hall (Tymora's temple in the Upper City): The high clergy read in old temple records that Balduran's luck-coin is real. They have been rigging their own ritual coin-tosses since 1489 to hide that the toss now always lands frown-up. They want the Penny for their altar, their pilgrims and their standing. They will treat an envoy who answers to the goddess rather than to them as a rogue claimant.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- Beneath the Counting House's known strongroom lies an older under-vault built on the foundations of Balduran's own house. Its inner door has a Gondish lock that opens only to four Ledger keys turned together.
- The under-vault has a second way in: Balduran's old sea-door, drowned under the harbour wall. Its passage clears only at the lowest spring tide, about once a month, and only for less than an hour.
- The drowned men pulled from the harbour these last months were Guild divers who died scouting the flooded sea-passage. Their own mates put the coins in their mouths, in the old sailors' way.
- Peregrin Vane, the runaway heir the patriars are quietly searching for, stole his father's Ledger key before he fled the city. He is hiding in the Outer City.
- Every man the Ledger has sent down to turn the Penny back since 1489 has died within the year or lost everything he had.
- Since 1489, every new sheet from the Ledger's own paper mill shows the weeping face in its watermark, though nobody changed the mould.
- Lady Ysolde Caskwell keeps a shrine of black antlers behind a false panel in her house's chapel.
- An old saying in the Lady's Hall's oldest liturgy: 'The Lady's envoy forswears the game, that his last throw be whole.'
- Sergeant Brannick is paid by a House Vane steward to pass on certain arrivals' names from his ledger. The name Worgen has been on that list since House Vane learned whose hands its note had fallen into.
Clocks:

- clock_vane_search: House Vane's steward traces the man who holds the family's note (segments 4; when full: The steward finds James with two Fist men at his back. He offers to buy the note back and asks, too carefully, what Peregrin told him that night.)
- clock_gate_misfortune: The Gate's soured luck builds toward a disaster (segments 8; when full: A fire takes a row of dockside warehouses in the Lower City. Baldur's Mouth blames tieflings, and the Fist floods the harbour streets.)
The wonder: The promissory note James won is written on House Vane's paper. Held to a flame or to moonlight, it shows a watermark of a woman's face, and the face is weeping. The cat will not sit on it. Its history: The Quiet Ledger has its paper made at its own mill under an oath sworn by Balduran's heirs. Every document of the four houses carries the Penny's face in the sheet. For two centuries the face smiled. Since 1489 every sheet has come out weeping, from a mould no one has touched. The open question: If the paper knows which face of the coin is up, what else in the city answers to it — and who has been reading those signs all along?
Clues to the truth:
- The Lady's Hall: an aging acolyte who keeps the offering-coin ledger knows that every ritual toss for six years has landed frown-up, and that the high priest ordered the results rewritten. She will say it only to someone the goddess plainly favours.
- The Blushing Mermaid: an old harbour song, 'Balduran's Penny', about a coin 'that smiled for the city and was buried with the tide'. A one-eyed bosun knows the verse nobody else sings, the one about the sea-door.
- The Counting House: the Gondish lockwright's apprentice can tell, if asked the right way, that the under-vault lock was re-warded in 1489 at Lady Caskwell's sole order, against the custom that all four houses consent.
The next session's false lead: Sergeant Brannick copies James's name onto a separate slip and hands it to a runner, which looks like someone in the Fist tracking the Lady's envoy. The ordinary truth: Brannick has a standing two-silver graft with a House Vane steward, who only wants to find the man holding the family's note and knows nothing of Tymora.
The hero's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): The Guild's go-between will not deal with a man who refuses to sit at a table. The price of the sea-door map is one hand against Mirela with the map as the stake. James may play, find another lever, or watch her deal and spot her crooked shuffle — reading her is his legitimate trade, and it is also the first sign she cannot win straight.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): As the stake is laid and James's hand hovers over the cards, a copper rolls off the barrel's edge, stands on its rim on the wet stones, and stays there. A warmth at his breastbone, and a woman's voice only he hears: 'Not yet.'

Under it, the simple version still holds.
