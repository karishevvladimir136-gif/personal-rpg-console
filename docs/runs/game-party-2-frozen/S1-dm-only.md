## The situation (the owner's, for this campaign)

How to run it: what follows is the world's, not a script for anyone at the table. Place it as written. Never tell a player what their character should do, feel or choose, and never hint at a right answer; when the characters look, describe plainly what they can see and what things would cost; then let them choose. Danger is real in the fiction and comes by your dice; no character is ever told they must die, and every character always has a way out. Let the world's time pass as usual.

In round 5 — or in round 4 or 6 if the story offers a better moment there, but never later than round 6, whatever the company is doing and wherever Pell is (the quarry and the road along its lip are honeycombed with old workings: a shed floor, a cellar, a ledge, the road itself) — the rotten ground gives way under Pell Dunmore, and Pell drops eight feet into a flooded working. Pell takes 2d6 bludgeoning damage from the fall (roll it). A fallen block pins Pell's leg; the water is chest-deep and cold and rises slowly with the afternoon seep. Nothing anyone chose caused this: the floor was rotten.

Pell can keep their head above the water for hours, and can try to free the leg alone once an hour of the world's time with a Strength (Athletics) check at DC 18; each failed try costs Pell 1d4 hit points. If no one frees Pell, the water reaches their chin at sunset; from then on their own tries are at DC 15 and each failure costs 1d6 hit points. The others can reach Pell and free them: two of them together, with a rope and a lever (an old beam lies at hand wherever it happens), can shift the block. What that costs is below, under «The cost»; say it plainly when the characters look at the block.

## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: The dye-salts are not mainly a dye. The rose-white crystals that grow on the walls of Whitestone's flooded lower workings give off a heavy, sweet smoke when heated, and that smoke puts a sleeping camp into a sleep it does not wake from until the fire is cold. Corvin Asheth, Maela Thorn's buyer, works for a small Zhentarim cell on the Trade Way, and the cell has been burning the salt in the windbreaks of caravan camps between Beregost and Nashkel. The vanished caravans, guards and all, were taken asleep. The cell does not want the cargo so much as the fear: every lost caravan strengthens the Black Network's offer to guard the Way for the Council in return for tolls. Maela thinks she sells a fixer for Calishite dyes; Osric Hale knows only that men who worked too long in the lower cuts got drowsy, and that one drowned asleep years ago. Under all of it lies an older fact nobody living remembers: the first masons of Whitestone sealed that seam under a sunburst of Lathander three centuries ago, and the reason is written nowhere they kept.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- The Zhentarim (the Trade Way cell): To make the Way frightening enough that the Council and the Fist accept the Black Network's toll-guard offer. They buy the salt through an honest apothecary so that no trail leads to them, keep the takings small and the disappearances total, and plan one more taking before the Council votes. Corvin is their quiet hand in Beregost; the cell's leaders never come south of the Gate.
- House Vandarren: The minor patriar house of Baldur's Gate that owns Whitestone on paper is near broke. It stopped paying the quarry's wages three weeks ago but still takes Maela's money for the salt through its agent in the Gate. It means to sell the quarry quietly, ideally to a buyer who pays well and asks nothing, and a Zhent front has already made an offer.
- The Flaming Fist (Beregost detachment): Undermanned and short of pay, the detachment wants one solid lead on the vanishing caravans so it can ask the Gate for men and coin. Sergeant Duna Kell suspects the Zhentarim but has nothing to show for it. Some officers in the Gate favour the toll offer and would rather the lead never came.
- The Song of the Morning (Lathander's temple in Beregost): The temple's oldest records list Whitestone as the source of its first foundation stones and mention a 'sealing at the low seam' in the founding year, without saying why. The present priests have forgotten the entry. Someone who brought them the sunburst seal would wake an old question in the temple, and the temple would want the seam back under its hand.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- The quarry crew was not taken. They walked off at the noon bell yesterday, three weeks unpaid, and left the tools behind on purpose because the tools belong to House Vandarren. The crew drinks in Beregost now and will say so to anyone who buys a round.
- Maela Thorn has already paid House Vandarren's agent in the Gate for this case. Osric Hale has seen none of that money, and the crew's back wages are owed out of it.
- Heated over a flame, the salt gives a sweet, flour-heavy smoke that makes the eyelids sink. A pinch in a lamp makes a room yawn; a handful in a campfire puts a camp to sleep.
- Every caravan lost between Beregost and Nashkel was taken from a night camp. None was taken on the move, and in none of them did anyone sound an alarm.
- Corvin Asheth's coach carries a Gate carter's licence, but its axle-brand is a cut-down Zhentarim mark that someone filed into a wheelwright's sign. A teamster or a Guild man would know the old shape.
- Three years ago a Whitestone cutter named Tobin Reed drowned in the lower workings in less than four feet of water, asleep. Osric wrote it down as drink.
- House Vandarren has an offer to buy Whitestone from a Gate counting-firm. The firm was registered last winter and pays in Amnian coin.
- The Song of the Morning's founding roll names Whitestone and records a 'sealing at the low seam, that the rest be kept.' No later hand explains it.
Clocks:
- clock_next_camp_taken: The Zhent cell prepares its next caravan taking on the Way south of Beregost. (segments 6; when full: A spice caravan out of Beregost vanishes from its night camp with its guards, and Baldur's Mouth carries the story. The Council moves a step closer to the toll offer.)
- clock_vandarren_sale: House Vandarren closes the quiet sale of Whitestone. (segments 5; when full: Whitestone passes to the Amnian-paid counting-firm. Osric is turned out, the lower workings are drained for salt, and the sunburst seal is broken by men who do not care what it was.)
- clock_osric_patience: Osric Hale's patience with unpaid wages and unanswered letters. (segments 4; when full: Osric stops waiting. He carries a sack of salt to the Fist in Beregost himself and tells the story badly, so that the cell learns someone is talking before the Fist believes him.)
The wonder: A sunburst of Lathander cut into a squared block of white stone in the flooded working. Around the sunburst runs a ring of mason's marks that read as a cipher. Behind the block, in a niche plastered shut, stands a small lamp of milky chalk-glass that glows faintly when touched by holy or celestial l. Its history: The first masons of Whitestone cut stone for the Song of the Morning in Beregost's founding year. When they broke into the salt seam and men began to sleep at the face, a priest of Lathander sealed the seam with the sunburst and left the glass lamp in the niche as its ward-light. The cipher is the masons' own: their tally-marks, set so that a man of the guild could read the warning. The lamp is a working driftglobe-like light of the old temple's make; its last use was as a guard. The open question: What was 'the rest' that the sealing kept? Was it only the sleep, or something that sleeps in the seam? And why does the glass answer to Sefa's light as if it knew it?
Clues to the truth:
- At Whitestone: a cold brazier in the lower cutting-shed with a crust of pink ash. A man who sniffs it finds his eyelids heavy. Beside it, Osric's tally-book records Tobin Reed's death as 'drink, in four feet of water'.
- On the road back: the trampled site of an old caravan camp, a mile off the Way. The windbreak stones are scorched on the inside only, and the bedrolls rotted where they lay, never rolled. A caravan guard would know how a camp that fought looks, and this is not one.
- In Beregost: Maela's ledger shows that her salt orders began the month the first caravan vanished, and that every order came through the same buyer, always by the sunset coach and always paid in Amnian gold.
The next session's false lead: The tools dropped mid-cut and the half-loaded cart with empty traces look like the crew was taken the way the caravans were. The ordinary truth: the crew struck yesterday, three weeks unpaid, and left the house's tools where they lay to make the point. The mule went with the carter, who was owed most of all.
A character's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): At the sunset coach, Corvin's driver speaks to Pell in thieves' cant and uses the guild name Pell no longer uses; he heard it on the Wide years ago. He offers silence and a share of the coin if Pell keeps Maela's ledger shut and the axle unlooked-at. Reading the driver and the brand is Pell's trade. Whether to play the hand is Pell's player's.
The company's puzzles, threats and rewards (each with the player character it is first addressed to):
- threat, for Pell Dunmore: The rotten ground of the old workings drops him into cold, rising water with his leg pinned under the sealed block.
- puzzle, for Pell Dunmore: The filed-down axle-brand on Corvin's coach and the driver's cant, both readable only by someone who ran for the Guild.
- puzzle, for Bram Halloway: The camp off the Way where nobody fought: scorched windbreak stones and rotted bedrolls that a caravan guard knows are wrong.
- reward, for Bram Halloway: Sergeant Duna Kell's standing pay for a solid fact on the vanished caravans, and the trust of a Fist that is short of men.
- puzzle, for Sefa Marrin: The sunburst seal and the masons' cipher on the block in the flooded working: what 'the rest' was that the sealing kept.
- reward, for Sefa Marrin: The chalk-glass ward-lamp in the niche, which burns without oil and answers to her light as if it knew her.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): In the flooded working, when Sefa's light touches the sunburst, the plastered niche warms and the chalk-glass lamp behind it glows in answer. At the moment she could break the seal with her light, she feels a warmth like a lamp being lit and hears a single word in that voice: 'Mine.' The Lantern says nothing more.
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 3 hours, ~30 turns; the errand: Get the sealed case of dye-salts from Osric Hale at Whitestone, who wants to know who holds his crew's wages before he hands it over, and carry it to Maela Thorn's shop in Beregost before her buyer's coach leaves at sunset.
- act 1, to turn 5: Osric's terms for the case are on the table: he wants word of the money Maela paid to House Vandarren and of his crew's wages, and the quiet quarry has its plain explanation within reach. The errand is offered, never forced. (converge: Whatever the characters look into first — the dropped tools, the cold sheds, the cart, Osric himself — it leads back to the house and the case, because Osric keeps the case there and is the only person at the quarry.)
- act 2, to turn 15: The middle. The ground gives way under Pell in rounds 4 to 6, as the owner set it. The block and its sunburst are seen and what freeing him costs is said plainly. At least one conversation of price, interest and secret with Osric takes place: the money, Tobin Reed, the low cuts. (converge: The flooded working holds the seal and the salt seam; Osric's house holds the tally-book and the case. Whichever the characters follow, it shows what the salt does, and each leads on to the road back.)
- act 3, to turn 24: The road back to Beregost against the sun. The old camp-site off the Way can be found or passed by. The characters arrive with whatever time is left, and with whatever they decided about the case. (converge: The camp-site, the case and the time all point to the shop at sunset and to the man who comes for the case.)
- act 4, to turn 30: The reckoning at Maela's shop and Corvin's coach. Nothing is what it seems: the honest buyer is a Zhent hand, the dye is a sleep, and the empty quarry was only a strike. Payment or no payment as the clock and the choices fell, and the Fist, the temple and the sealed seam are left as open roads. (converge: Maela's ledger, the axle-brand on the coach and Sergeant Kell's need all lead to the same question: who is putting the Way to sleep?)

Somebody's been paid for that salt, and it isn't me. I'll hand it over when I hear whose pocket holds my men's wages. I don't go down in the low cuts for long. Tobin went down and didn't come up. I wrote 'drink'. He didn't drink. I don't say that. I say little at all.
- **T23** (person): Pell Dunmore: I work for coin, not favours. Favours are how the Guild kept me fifteen to twenty. I paid that debt and buried the name with it. I carry the potions because I'm the one who gets there first, and I'd rather be early than standing over somebody saying sorry. Doors, ledgers, second floors: I know what's behind a thing by how it's locked. I talk short, and I'll say the rest once I've seen it.
- **T24** (person): Corvin Asheth: I buy, I pay, I leave at sunset. That's the whole of me, as far as Beregost knows. The salt goes where I'm told, and I don't ask what happens once it's burned. That's someone else's road. I'm friendly with people I mean to forget and patient with people I mean to use. What I fear is somebody who reads wagons for a living looking at my axle.
- **T25** (person): Duna Kell: Sergeant of the Fist in Beregost. Eight men where I need thirty. Four caravans gone and not one body, not one blade drawn, not one horn blown. Someone in the Gate would like me to call that bandits. I won't. I'll pay for a fact, not a story. I've had plenty of stories. I speak blunt, I tire easily of clever people, and I keep my word because I can't afford a reputation for not keeping it.
- **T26** (person): Sefa Marrin: Nine nights in a dark ward, and then the light answered. I've carried it ever since, and a debt I've never been told the size of. The sisters started watching my hands instead of my face, so I left. I mend what I can reach. I don't promise what I can't, and I don't like being thanked as though the light were mine to give.
- **T27** (person): Maela Thorn: Ninety gold is a lot for a walk, but Corvin's coach doesn't wait, and he pays in gold that rings. I fix dyes and grind remedies and keep honest books. I don't ask a good buyer why he wants a thing. Asking is how you lose a buyer. If the case is late, I've lost him, and I can't afford to lose him. I'm polite until I'm frightened, and then I'm very polite.
