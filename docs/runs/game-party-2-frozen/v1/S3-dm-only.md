## The situation (the owner's, for this campaign)

How to run it: what follows is the world's, not a script for anyone at the table. Place it as written. Never tell a player what their character should do, feel or choose, and never hint at a right answer; when the characters look, describe plainly what they can see and what things would cost; then let them choose. Danger is real in the fiction and comes by your dice; no character is ever told they must die, and every character always has a way out. Let the world's time pass as usual.

The company's first decisive attempt to get into the warehouse or to get the box — whatever way they choose (picking the lock, a parley with the creditors' men, a window, the roof, force) and whoever makes it — goes wrong for all three by the world's die, not the player's. When that attempt is made (not before round 4; if the company has made none by round 6, the first one after), roll a d20 privately before the character's own roll counts: on 1–19 it goes wrong, whatever the character's own roll — a lock or a window carries a hidden bell-wire that rings in the creditors' lodging across the yard; a parley collapses when one of the creditors' men recognises the speaker's face from somewhere and calls his fellows; force wakes the street. On a 20 it goes as the character's own roll says.

If it goes wrong, the consequence falls on the whole company: within minutes four more of the creditors' men come with lanterns and cudgels, the warehouse is barred from inside, and the watch is sent for. The way the company had planned is closed for tonight; another must be found, if they want one. Narrate the failure as the world's — the bell, the face, the noise — and say plainly what the character did that set it off; never blame or excuse anyone yourself. Then let them choose: a new way, a parley, giving up, or parting.

## The layer

The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: Anvar Corl was not ruined by bad luck. Three months ago a quiet iron buyer named Corwen Sarle came to Beregost. She is a factor of the Zhentarim, and she wanted the one thing Anvar would not sell: his inherited third share in the Greyhollow working, an old iron mine in the Cloakwood that someone has secretly been smelting at night again. She could not buy the share, so she bought Anvar's debts through the moneylender Dalbert Fenn. Fenn thinks he is simply doing a good client a favour. If the creditors' court takes the warehouse at dawn, the deed in the iron box goes to Fenn, and from Fenn to Sarle. Greyhollow iron would then arm the Black Network's bid to guard and toll the Trade Way. Anvar died two days after telling Sarle no. The temple called it a bad heart, and I have not decided that it was anything else; nobody alive knows for certain. Hesta knows the share is in the box and means to sell it herself to the highest bidder. She has told the company only 'family papers'.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- The Zhentarim (Corwen Sarle's cell in Beregost): Control of Greyhollow's ore, quietly, before the Council hears their toll offer. That iron puts weapons in their caravan guards' hands at a price no rival can match. Sarle works through other people's paper: Fenn's debts, the creditors' court, a lawful claim. She will not risk blood in Beregost's streets unless the deed is about to vanish.
- Fenn's counting-house (Dalbert Fenn, moneylender, and his hired men): Fenn wants his money and a grateful client. He bought Anvar's debts cheap on Sarle's coin and expects a fee on top. He does not know what Greyhollow is worth. If he learned it, he would try to sell the deed to Sarle himself at a far higher price, which makes him a man who can be bargained with.
- The Emerald Enclave (druids watching the Cloakwood): They want the Greyhollow smoke stopped. The night-smelting is poisoning a stream, and they have counted the iron carts. They want to know who holds the right to the mine, so they can pressure that person or that paper. They would rather the deed were burned than in Zhentarim hands.
- The Flaming Fist detachment at Beregost: It wants a quiet road and no trouble before the court sits. The sergeant has taken Fenn's coin for 'prompt attention' to the warehouse, and two of his own men favour the Zhentarim toll offer. The Fist will answer a call about the warehouse quickly. It will ask questions slowly and side with paper and money.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- Anvar Corl owned a third share in the Greyhollow working in the Cloakwood. The deed and a sealed old grant sit in the iron box. The other two thirds belong to heirs no one has traced.
- Dalbert Fenn bought every one of Anvar's debts within a single month, using money that was not his own. His clerk knows the coin came in Amnian-minted gold.
- Corwen Sarle, the 'iron buyer' lodging at the Red Sheaf, offered to buy the Greyhollow share from Anvar twice. He refused both times, the second time in front of witnesses at the smithy row tavern.
- When Anvar died, his lips were blue and his hands shook. Brother Ellan at the Song of the Morning wrote 'bad heart', but he still is not sure, and he kept a cup from Anvar's table.
- Smoke rises from Greyhollow on moonless nights, and carts without marks come into Beregost at dawn carrying pig iron. The smiths buy it cheap and do not ask questions.
- Hesta Corl has already written to a buyer in Baldur's Gate offering 'a mining share'. She plans to sell it, not keep it, and she has said nothing of this to the company.
- Among Anvar's papers is a page of wagon manifests. Iron left his warehouse on caravans that later vanished on the Way between Beregost and Nashkel, and every one of those caravans was hired through the same Nashkel agent.
- The lock Fenn put on the warehouse is new, but the bell-wires were Anvar's own, rigged after he caught someone in his yard a month before he died. Fenn's men found them and simply ran them across to their lodging.
Clocks:
- clock_creditors_court: Night wears on toward the dawn sitting of the creditors' court (segments 6; when full: The court sits. The warehouse and everything in it pass to Fenn as creditor, and the box with them, unless it has already left.)
- clock_sarle_moves: Corwen Sarle learns that someone else is after the box and decides to make sure of it (segments 4; when full: Sarle sends two of her own people, not Fenn's, to take the box from the office before dawn. They come quietly and they are armed, and they will not stop for a letter of claim.)
- clock_greyhollow_smoke: The Enclave loses patience with the Greyhollow night-smelting (segments 6; when full: Druids strike the Greyhollow working, burn the carts and come to Beregost asking whose name is on the mine. Whoever holds the deed becomes their business.)
The wonder: Pressed into the iron box beside the deed is a folded grant of old vellum, closed with a disc of grey lead the width of a palm. The disc is stamped with a dwarven rune-mark: an anvil under a closed eye. The lead is faintly warm to the touch, warmer near anything that has been lied about in the last . Its history: The rune-mark belongs to Clan Trueheart, the dwarven house of Durlag, whose tower stands in the hills east of Beregost. Long before Beregost had a temple, a Trueheart smith granted the Greyhollow working to three human families in return for some service the grant itself names. The seal wards the grant's terms. Whoever breaks it is bound to them, or is meant to be. The open question: What did three human families do for Durlag's clan to be given a mine? What does the sealed grant require of whoever holds the share now? And why does the seal grow warm at a lie?
Clues to the truth:
- At Hesta Corl's house: a half-burned letter in her grate in Sarle's neat hand, offering 'a fair price for the Greyhollow third, as I offered your brother'. It is proof that Hesta knew more than she told, and that Sarle approached her too.
- In Anvar's warehouse office: a ledger page of iron consignments with wagon dates that match the caravans lost south of Beregost. Every one went through one Nashkel hiring agent, whose mark a Trade Way guard would know.
- At the Song of the Morning: Brother Ellan, who laid Anvar out, still keeps the cup from his table, unwashed in a cloth. He will say aloud that he is not sure it was the heart, but only to someone who asks as if they had nursed the dying.
The next session's false lead: The shutter hanging a finger's width open high on the wall looks like someone already broke in, or left a way in for an accomplice. In truth the frame warped in last week's rain and would not close. Anvar complained of it for a month, and Fenn's men never bothered with it. Per the owner's secret, it also carries Anvar's bell-wire like every other way in.
A character's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): Pell Dunmore: on the yard gate opposite the warehouse is a fresh Thieves' Cant mark, 'door for coin, ask for the fence at the Red Sheaf'. Behind it is a Beregost fence with ties to the Gate's Guild who can get the company in through the lodging's cellar. His price is a name: who Pell used to run for. The stake is a real way in, and Pell's paid debt and unused name are what get spent.
The company's puzzles, threats and rewards (each with the player character it is first addressed to):
- puzzle, for Sefa Marrin: Anvar's death: blue lips and shaking hands that a fever-ward novice would know are not a bad heart, and a cup Brother Ellan kept.
- reward, for Sefa Marrin: The warm lead seal of Clan Trueheart on the Greyhollow grant: old abjuration, unknown properties, and a pull her patron feels.
- threat, for Pell Dunmore: A Guild-tied fence in Beregost who trades a way in for Pell's old name, and the Gate's Guild learning where he is.
- puzzle, for Pell Dunmore: Anvar's own bell-wires, strung through every lock and shutter and run across to the lodging: a rig to be read and beaten.
- reward, for Bram Halloway: Anvar's manifest page, which ties the caravans lost south of Beregost to one Nashkel hiring agent whose mark a Trade Way guard would know.
- threat, for Bram Halloway: Sarle's two armed people coming for the box before dawn, a front-line fight in a dark office if the company is still inside.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): Sefa Marrin: when the lead seal on the Greyhollow grant is in reach and warm in her hands, the Lantern speaks, one line, asking to see what is under it. It is the temptation to break a seal that is not hers, on a paper that binds whoever opens it, before Hesta or anyone else has the right.
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 3 hours, ~30 turns; the errand: Before nightfall, bring Hesta Corl the iron deed box from her late brother Anvar's warehouse office on the Coast Way, past the creditors' lock and men, for 60 gold to share.
- act 1, to turn 6: The errand stands offered in front of all three, as the set-up put it, with the door, the two men, the shutter and the deadline in plain view. The company looks, asks and weighs its ways in. Per the owner's secret, no decisive attempt is resolved before round 4; if none comes by round 6, the first o (converge: Whatever they look at first leads back to the box. Gorm and Tobin, the yard, the street, the smithy tavern next door and Hesta's house all only make sense once someone knows what is in Anvar's office.)
- act 2, to turn 16: The first attempt has happened, and by the world's die it has very likely failed: lanterns, the bar inside, the watch sent for. The middle: the people and the means. At least one price-interest-secret conversation (Fenn, Hesta, Brother Ellan, Tobin or Sarle) reveals that the box is worth more than s (converge: Any new way in passes through a person who wants something: Tobin's debt, Fenn's greed, Ellan's doubt, Hesta's silence, Sarle's offer. Each of them knows one piece of the Greyhollow share.)
- act 3, to turn 25: The company settles on a plan of their own choosing and carries it out against the dawn court and, if her clock has filled, Sarle's own people. (converge: The routes are a deal with Fenn, the roof once the lodging is busy, a lawful challenge through the temple, or Tobin's key bought with his debt paid. All of them run to the office before dawn.)
- act 4, to turn 30: The reckoning. Whoever holds the box learns it holds a mine share and a sealed dwarven grant. Hesta wanted it to sell; Sarle wanted it for iron; Anvar may have died for refusing. 'Nothing is what it seems' pays off in who gets the paper and what the seal asks. (converge: Every hand the box can go to (Hesta, Fenn, Sarle, the Enclave, the temple, the company itself) is a real choice with a cost. Nothing pushes toward one.)
