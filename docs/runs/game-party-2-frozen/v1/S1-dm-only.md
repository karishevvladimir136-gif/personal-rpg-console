## The situation (the owner's, for this campaign)

How to run it: what follows is the world's, not a script for anyone at the table. Place it as written. Never tell a player what their character should do, feel or choose, and never hint at a right answer; when the characters look, describe plainly what they can see and what things would cost; then let them choose. Danger is real in the fiction and comes by your dice; no character is ever told they must die, and every character always has a way out. Let the world's time pass as usual.

On the way back from the quarry — between round 4 and round 6, at the first moment the company passes the old cutting-shed on the quarry's lip (or, if by round 6 they have taken another road, wherever an old working lies under the road there) — the rotten floor gives way under Pell Dunmore, and Pell drops eight feet into a flooded working. Pell takes 2d6 bludgeoning damage from the fall (roll it). A fallen block pins Pell's leg; the water is chest-deep and cold and rises slowly with the afternoon seep. Nothing anyone chose caused this: the floor was rotten.

Pell can keep their head above the water for hours, and can try to free the leg alone once an hour of the world's time with a Strength (Athletics) check at DC 18; each failed try costs Pell 1d4 hit points. If no one frees Pell, the water reaches their chin at sunset; from then on their own tries are at DC 15 and each failure costs 1d6 hit points. The others can free Pell: two of them together, with a rope and a lever (a beam from the shed), can shift the block. What that costs is below, under «The cost»; say it plainly when the characters look at the block.

## The layer

````text
The campaign's hidden layer (the DM's prep; the player never sees it; the truth comes out through clues, nobody explains all of it):
The truth: Under Whitestone's lower workings lies a lamp-shrine of Amaunator from Netheril's days, buried by a landslip centuries ago. Its cold, unsetting flame still burns behind a sealed door, and the seepwater that passes over it leaves pale crystals. Osric Hale has sold these to Maela Thorn for three years as 'dye-salts'. They are dawnsalt: linen steeped in them keeps a trace of that old light, and a hospice ward in Rivington wraps the hell-marked returnees of Elturel in it. That ward buys through a factor who never says where the salt comes from, and Maela has never asked what it is for. Two days ago Osric, deep in debt and pushing for a bigger batch, had his crew cut into a new chamber. They found sunburst carvings, a sealed door and water that glows faintly at night, set down their tools and walked to Beregost, where the tale is now loose in the taverns. Three powers are closing on Whitestone without seeing the whole of it: the Zhentarim, the Song of the Morning and the Rivington ward. Nobody yet asks who tended the lamp, or why it is still lit. The cutting-shed floor on the lip belongs to none of this: it is rotten, and its fall is chance; what freeing Pell costs is the table's to set, and I add nothing of my own to it.
Factions and their hidden aims:
- The Zhentarim (the Black Network), through its Beregost factor: They want every rare trade on the Trade Way under their hand, as a lever for their bid on the Way's tolls. Ruel Danner has heard there is a white salt worth far more than dye. He has already offered Osric gold for the quarry's output, and he means to learn who buys it and cut Maela out. He works by offers and quiet pressure, not blades.
- The Song of the Morning, Lathander's temple in Beregost: The temple counts every old Amaunatori site as Lathander's inheritance. Its archive speaks of 'the white hill of the morning lamp' east of the town, but the temple has never found it. Once the crew's tale reaches a priest, it will claim Whitestone as holy ground and close it to quarrying and trade alike. It would take the lamp into temple keeping, for the lamp's sake as much as its own.
- The Rivington ward (a hospice for Elturel's returnees, run by sisters out of the Lower City hospices): The ward needs a steady, secret supply of dawnsalt for the hell-marked returnees, whose scars ache in the dark and draw suspicion in the Gate. Its factor, Corlan Vesk, pays far over a dye price to keep the source unnamed and the patients unremarked. He fears the Fist, the temples and gossip more than he fears cost.
- The Lantern (Sefa Marrin's patron): I have decided only this much. It wants the unsetting lamp to stay lit and the salt to go out among the hurt, and it will tempt Sefa to keep a little for herself. Whether it was ever the lamp's keeper I leave open, even to myself.
Secrets (not tied to a place):
- Whitestone's crew walked off two days ago after cutting into a chamber with sunburst carvings and a sealed door. Most of them are in Beregost now, and Dannic the foreman tells it in Feldepost's Inn to anyone who buys the next cup.
- For three years Maela Thorn has sold the 'dye-salts' on at many times their dye price. She has never told her buyer where they come from, and has never asked him what he does with them.
- The salts dull if they are kept in the dark too long. That is why the case is light-tight and sealed, and why the buyer insists on a fresh batch rather than a stored one; by a few days on, they are worth no more than dye.
- The quarry's lease belongs to a Baldur's Gate patriar house that has stopped sending wages. For two years Osric has run Whitestone on salt money and owes Maela for an advance.
- Ruel Danner, the Zhentarim factor in Beregost, was a fence in the Lower City years ago and bought from Guild runners. He has already offered Osric gold for the quarry's salt, and Osric has not said no.
- Linen dyed with dawnsalt eases the 'hellmarks' some of Elturel's returnees carry. The Rivington ward buys it for that reason and keeps it quiet, because in the Gate a marked patient is a suspected one.
- The Song of the Morning's records speak of 'the white hill of the morning lamp', an Amaunatori shrine lost east of Beregost. Its priests have looked for it for a generation.
Clocks:
- clock_zhent_whitestone: Ruel Danner works to take Whitestone's salt into the Black Network (segments 6; when full: Osric sells his output to Danner. Maela's supply is cut, and the Zhentarim learn who the buyer in the Gate is and start naming their price on the road.)
- clock_whitestone_glow: The crew's tale and the breached chamber's night glow spread (segments 6; when full: Priests of the Song of the Morning ride out and claim Whitestone as holy ground. The quarry is closed to everyone, and the salt trade ends at the temple door.)
- clock_salt_dimming: The sealed dawnsalt dulls in the dark of its case (segments 4; when full: If the case has not reached the buyer by tomorrow's dawn, the salt is dye-grade only. The ward gets nothing it can use, and whoever holds the case holds a dye merchant's packet.)
The wonder: A palm-sized disc of white chalkstone veined with gold, kept on Osric's shelf. It is cut with a sunburst whose rays end in small flames around an empty lamp-shape. It is warm at night and faintly bright in full darkness. The crew found it on the floor of the breached chamber, before a door sealed wi. Its history: It is a votive token of the lamp-keepers of an Amaunatori shrine from Netheril's era. Pilgrims were given these to carry the unsetting flame's blessing home. A landslip buried the shrine when the old empire fell, and the flame never went out; the dawnsalt is its residue in the water. The open question: The lamp-shape at the centre is empty, on this token and on the door. What was meant to sit in it, and who is the keeper that the door's worn inscription says 'waits for the hour'?
Clues to the truth:
- Osric's house: his account book shows salt sold at many times the dye price, and a line two days back: 'cut into the east chamber — men gone'. The sunburst disc lies on the shelf above it.
- Beregost, Feldepost's Inn: Dannic the foreman, in his cups, tells of carved suns on the walls, a door that will not open, and water that glows when the lamps are out.
- Maela's shop at sunset: the buyer's crate is lined with black wool and stamped with a small open hand over a flame, the mark of the Rivington ward. Corlan Vesk calls the contents 'for the marked ones' when he thinks no one is listening.
The next session's false lead: The case's wax seal shows the marks of being lifted and reset. That reads as Osric skimming, a swap, or a Zhentarim hand. In truth Osric opened it himself to add his resignation letter to Maela and a small extra pouch to clear the advance he owes her.
A character's weakness tested (next session, for a reason, never a trap): At Beregost's edge or in Maela's street, Ruel Danner greets Pell by the Guild name Pell no longer uses, in front of the others. Danner's source is the Lower City runner trade he bought from. He asks for a pinch of the salt as the price of forgetting it. The stake is Pell's name, and the means is Pell's own trade, which can open and reseal a case unseen.
The company's puzzles, threats and rewards (each with the player character it is first addressed to):
- puzzle, for Pell Dunmore: The case's seal has been lifted and reset; who opened it, and what was added or taken?
- threat, for Pell Dunmore: Ruel Danner knows Pell's old Guild name and will trade his silence for a pinch of the salt.
- puzzle, for Bram Halloway: The yard tells a story to a caravan man's eye: a crew that left in a hurry for Beregost two days ago, a cart abandoned mid-load, the mule gone from its traces.
- threat, for Bram Halloway: The Zhentarim want Whitestone's salt on their own terms; they work the Way's toll bid that Bram knows from the road.
- reward, for Bram Halloway: Corlan Vesk needs a steady guard for the coach to Baldur's Gate and pays a road wage to a man who has kept wagons alive.
- puzzle, for Sefa Marrin: The sunburst disc and the salts answer to light; the empty lamp at the sign's heart is a question of old sun-worship that her Religion and Arcana can begin to read.
- reward, for Sefa Marrin: Dawnsalt-dyed linen eases hell-marked wounds; the ward that uses it is run by sisters out of the Lower City hospices she came from.
The patron's moment (at a temptation, not a comfort): Only if Sefa handles the dawnsalt or calls light near it: the salt warms and brightens in her palm more than in anyone else's. The Lantern says one line, at the temptation and not before: 'Keep a little. They will not weigh it.'
The act plan (yours alone; the player sees only the act number): about 4 hours, ~40 turns; the errand: Carry Maela Thorn's sealed case of 'dye-salts' from Osric Hale at Whitestone to her shop in Beregost before the buyer's coach leaves at sunset: ninety gold on delivery, nothing if late.

When they look at the block, the  (converge: Whether they leave at once or linger to look at the cuts, the only road back runs along the lip past the shed. - act 3, to turn 32: The road to Beregost against the sun. They plan how to carry the case and themselves, and the town's edge brings Dannic's tale or Ruel Danner's offer.
