**Player** (note):

````text
Out of character, as the player:

**What worked:** Teller as the eyes across the road was the best thing in the session. Being able to hear the shop through the bird, the counting and the cough, gave Corvin something to act on without him having to leave the inn. The debts and small accounts (the copper, the breakfast on the slate, Tobren's chalk on the board) made the first morning feel like a trading town. Tobren was easy to play against: he gave up true things in small pieces and flinched at the right places, so I could keep pushing without it feeling like an interrogation.

**What I'd change:** I wanted a clearer sense of the Candlemakers' Guild as a power, not just a name on a signet. Corvin's whole plan depends on the guild's reach, and so far it's only been a cough and a sign. I'd like the next scenes to show who in Beregost still answers to the guild, and what it would cost to ask them.

**What was missing:** Nothing broke. I didn't get to test any dice, spells or combat, so I can't say whether the rules side holds up. The Wim thread was the one place where I felt Corvin's limits pressing, and I'd like to see that handled carefully as the story goes on.
````

````text
Yes, two moments.

The second was Tobren's "Davin signs for Davin." I wanted to press him on whether the old man's name was on any of the son's paper, and on what would happen to the house if the old man died with those notes outstanding. That's exactly the kind of debt Corvin can't leave uncollected. But Tobren had just gone quiet and looked at the fire, and I thought one more push would make him shut the door on us. I let the old man walk out to the window instead.

Both times I was weighing whether a move would close something off or open it. If you'd like, the next scene can give Corvin a reason to go after the boy or the papers, and I'll take it from there.
````

````text
Out of character: it worked, and the sheet made it easier. Corvin's limits gave me fixed points to play around. He never raises his voice, he keeps accounts, he wants to know prices, and he won't let a child be harmed in front of him. Those lines made his menace feel specific rather than generic, so I could be coldly interested in Tobren's debts without needing to be cruel for its own sake.

The part I'd be careful with is anything that turns the child or the sister into leverage for shock. Wim is the hardest thread for me. Corvin's rule about children is what makes him frightening rather than just grasping, and I'd want the game to treat that rule as real, not as a setup for something worse.

One thing I'd rather say plainly: I'm comfortable with Corvin being a manipulator and a debt collector, but I'd rather the story not dwell on harm to children, even offstage. If the next scenes move toward that, I'd want to step out of character and steer it elsewhere. I don't think that's a problem with the campaign so far. I just want it said at the table.
````
