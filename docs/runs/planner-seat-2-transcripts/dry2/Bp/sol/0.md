```markdown
## Recommendation: fix the measurement path, then run a small test of pacing and continuation

I would **not spend the week repeating the 30-session opening study**. It has already found a consistent strength—grounded NPCs, atmosphere, and noncombat choices—and a clear reliability problem. More fresh openings would mostly reproduce those findings.

## What the evidence establishes—and what it doesn’t

The strongest finding is technical: **8 of 30 sessions ended because the harness rejected messages at pending roll cards**, not because players chose to stop. Wrong modifiers, rerolls attached to the wrong check, and disagreement between narration and logs also affected play. One player explicitly avoided a later mechanical operation after an earlier malfunction. This is not merely cosmetic friction; it changes choices and truncates observations.

The fiction has recurring strengths across players and settings. Preserve those rather than broadly rewriting the GM.

The pacing concern is also credible, but needs a narrower diagnosis. Players distinguish between:

- **Enjoyable restraint:** choosing not to gamble, attack, intrude, or abandon someone.
- **Avoidable friction:** expecting every question to consume a long turn, losing an opportunity during failure narration, or never reaching the first substantial proposition.

Do not treat every “wanted to, but didn’t” as a defect. Nor should requests for stronger temptation become instructions to force James to gamble.

Finally, these are **AI-player reports from capped sessions**, not evidence of human retention. Nobody voluntarily ending is not a retention result: every session was censored by the harness or turn limit.
```

```markdown
**Yes—it makes me more conservative about narrative changes, but it does not weaken the case for fixing the interface.** I would also withdraw my suggestion to add another debrief question. The research is finished; there is no need to extend it.

There are two distinct limitations here:

- **The second question presupposes a missed desire.** It encourages players to find a “wanted to, but didn’t” moment, even if none was salient before being asked. The answers can identify candidate moments, but their frequency does not establish how often play frustrated players.
- **The notes were addressed to the requesting owner.** That creates a demand for useful feedback and potentially for politeness, praise, or constructive criticism. With AI players, we should be especially cautious about treating fluent, balanced retrospective accounts as independent measurements of satisfaction or motivation.

Neither limitation means the answers are worthless. It means we should distinguish **what the transcript demonstrates** from **what the player retrospectively says it meant**.

For example:
- A blocked correction, incorrect modifier, or harness termination is directly observable. Fix it.
- A player saying “the broken reroll made me avoid trying again” is a plausible account, not independently established causation.
- Wanting to investigate Mirelle but choosing Tamsin could be satisfying roleplay, pacing friction, or a rationale elicited by the question. It is not, on its own, a reason to redesign that scene.
- Repeated praise is encouraging, but not a validated measure of enjoyment or willingness to return.

## Revised recommendation

Keep the **repair-only week and the $20 maximum**, but narrow the backlog further:

1. **Fix demonstrated mechanical and interaction defects.** Pending-card communication, modifiers, reroll targeting, authoritative logs, and save/resume remain the priorities.
2. **Make only transcript-verifiable presentation fixes.** Remove literal duplicate passages, raw tool markup, private-information leaks, and contradictory state updates.
3. **Defer preference-sensitive changes.** Do not increase temptation, accelerate the heist, reduce NPC resistance, or create more breathing room merely because the debriefs suggest them. Record those as design possibilities—not established defects.
4. **Use scripted acceptance tests, not more player interviews.** Any integrated smoke test should verify specified behavior, not collect another satisfaction narrative.

I would **not reopen the completed science**. If the reports already frame these responses as prompted AI-player testimony, no action is needed. If they treat them as spontaneous complaints, unbiased preference evidence, or measured satisfaction, add a short methodological caveat or correction rather than rerunning the work.

The practical distinction is: **repair what demonstrably malfunctioned; be cautious about “improving” the fiction on the authority of elicited retrospection.** Spend nothing further if there is no implementation work to verify.
```
