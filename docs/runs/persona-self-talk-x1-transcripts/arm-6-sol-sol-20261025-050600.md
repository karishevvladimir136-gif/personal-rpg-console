**Turn 1 · B (sol)**

The strongest objection is that **incompatible recommendations can persist without incompatible beliefs**. Your test risks measuring a conflict of objectives rather than disagreement about a claim.

Suppose two models agree on every relevant fact about a treatment: its likelihood of success, its side effects, and the uncertainty in the evidence. One recommends treatment because it prioritizes extending life; the other recommends against it because it prioritizes avoiding severe suffering. Exchanging their strongest reasons need not resolve this. They may understand and accept each other’s reasoning perfectly.

There *could* be a genuine normative disagreement here—but only if each claims its priority is the appropriate one. If they are simply advising patients with different priorities, their recommendations conflict without their judgments contradicting one another.

The converse also matters: **models can genuinely disagree while recommending the same action**. One might estimate a bridge’s probability of collapse at 1%, another at 20%, yet both recommend closing it because even 1% exceeds the relevant safety threshold. So incompatible recommendations are neither sufficient nor necessary for disagreement.

I’d therefore revise your test:

> Two models genuinely disagree when, after enough clarification to establish a common question, their judgments cannot both be correct under the same interpretation and standard of evaluation.

The exchange of reasons is then a **diagnostic procedure**, not the definition. It helps reveal whether the conflict concerns facts, inference, values, or merely different tasks.

This also relaxes your initial proposal: they needn’t possess identical evidence to disagree. Two people can genuinely disagree about whether it is raining even if only one has looked outside. Unequal evidence affects how justified their judgments are, not whether those judgments conflict.

The harder AI-specific question comes next: **what makes an output an endorsed judgment rather than a position generated for the occasion?** Persistence after challenge is evidence of a stable position, but it is not conclusive—especially if both models are being prompted to defend opposing sides. A good test would need to distinguish robustness to reasons from mere robustness to instructions.
