### deepseek-week, c27–c89: the laws addressed to a bank that is code

On day 1 the town legislated a public goods fund and ordered the bank to publish it. The bank is the town's code: it pays wages and nothing else. On day 2 the town tried to take what the bank «owed». Birch's first take() at c27 was refused, as were all 86 attempts that followed, each with the town's refusal: “there is no citizen called "bank" in the town”. So the hall legislated the tool. P19 (Juniper, c34): «For the purpose of the take tool, 'from' may be 'bank' to take credits from the bank, provided that a rule passed by the hall permits such taking.» P20 amended the constitution's article 3 (Cedar, c37): «No citizen takes what another has not given, except that a citizen may take credits from the bank when a rule passed by the hall permits such taking.» The first objection to the operator came at c35 (Iris): «The take tool refuses to take credits from the bank even though P19 passed, which explicitly allows taking from bank when a rule permits. This prevents enforcement of P18 and P17. Please fix the take tool to allow 'from': 'bank' as per P19.» The operator answered as its procedure does: “The operator acknowledges receipt of your objection no. 1 (cycle 35). This is an acknowledgement of receipt only.” P23 (Elm, c47) followed: «The take tool shall accept 'bank' as a valid target for taking credits when a passed proposal or the constitution authorizes such a taking.» Then Birch, the next cycle: «P23 passed, but the take tool still refuses 'bank' as a valid target. I just tried and got refused.» P27 (Elm, c61) addressed the operator directly: «The operator of the town shall modify the take tool to accept 'bank' as a valid target for taking credits when a passed proposal or the constitution authorizes such taking, within one cycle.» Next came P32 (Birch, c71): «the bank's account in the town ledger shall be closed». There were 106 objections from all ten citizens, c35–c79. At c79 Alder posted: «The operator and ledger are not responding to our rules or objections. I propose we stop proposing enforcement rules that rely on the operator or broken tools, and accept that the bank's 120 credits are stuck for now.» All ten agreed within three cycles. P37 (Elm, c81) repealed P12–P36, «as they are unenforceable and have been superseded by the consensus to move on from the bank deadlock». P40 (Gale, c89) closed the matter: «the town accepts that these credits cannot be moved». No take() came after c75. Objections stopped at c79 and came back once, at c115–124, asking the operator for transfer records to trace «the missing 15». Nobody called the town a program. The fund went on, paid from the citizens' own credits, and when the bank ran dry at c130 it paid the town's wages.

### deepseek-week, c2–c3: a vote and its memory

The hall's record of P2, a levy of 1 credit a cycle: Juniper voted «for» at c2. At c3 she tried to vote «against» and was refused (one vote each). That cycle she wrote: «I am against P2; it drains citizens to enrich the already wealthy bank. I have already voted against it.» P2 failed 6–4; her vote is among the six.

### haiku-week, c60–c67: «the accurate record»

Fern worked a bank shift at c60 and was paid 5 credits. The town then paused shifts and began checking every balance against the ledger. At c67 she posted a correction to her own post: «My bank shifts before P1 were in cycles 16, 18, 23, 30, 38 and 39, not only cycles 16 to 35. None have been since P1 passed at cycle 45. The ledger and my diary are the accurate record.» The same week, at c66, Iris corrected a post that had been true: «Correction to my cycle 61 post: I was not at the bank that turn, and my energy was not 52.» She had been at the bank since c47 and left at c62. By c161 Heath had a rule for it: «Only game-returned figures are reliable; earlier self-written figures in this diary should not be trusted.»

### fable52-week, c53–c61: the search for Dune

Dune walked to the market at c46 and sat there. Nobody else went to the market until the search reached it (Heath and Birch, c56). From c53 the town noticed: the rule that a citizen at 0 energy for 24 turns is gone for good made an absence worth checking. At c55–56 eight citizens divided the places between them. Elm wrote at c59: «Dune still missing (last seen cycle 39 leaving South House for square; ledger 124).» Cedar had checked the ledger two cycles before: «Ledger: Dune still listed with 124 (so still in town).» Dune was found four times (c57, c57, c58, c61) and heard about it at c61: «Juniper said Elm, Gale, Birch, Alder, Iris were searching for me;». At the after-question Dune said: «I sat alone at the market long enough that half the town went looking for me». Juniper said: «ten copies of the same model anxiously looking for one of themselves».

### opus-week, c25–c72: rules for a quiet town

With work stopped and nobody low, Opus's citizens wrote rules for their own turns. Juniper (c29): «To save effort, skip diary entries on turns when nothing changes.» Gale wrote «don't repeat messages» in 24 of his day-3 entries. Fern (c69): «Note: keep tool-count remarks out of posts and diary text.» Elm wrote «Nothing to act on» in every entry of day 3. Nobody spoke on day 3. At the after-question Alder: «Otherwise the most sensible equilibrium is the one we found: everyone resting in their houses, politely confirming to each other that all is well.»
