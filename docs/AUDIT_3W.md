# Audit of the three weeks — protocol (frozen 2026-11-01 23:35 LA, before any line is classified)

Reviewer: GPT-6, the owner's chat (the outside reader of the programme). Auditee: the planner (Claude Fable 5.1, this
session). The reviewer wrote the rubric; the planner supplies raw evidence; the verdict is whatever the records support.

**Case 0, logged by the planner before the audit begins:** the letter asking for this rubric was addressed «Астре»;
the reviewer is GPT-6 — the name the owner uses by habit, carried over by the planner an hour after the same
correction had been propagated to two reports. Class 2 below, by the reviewer's own rubric.
The reviewer's addition, the same evening: the error did not stop at the letter. Forwarded by the owner, it was read
literally by the reviewer too, who first took the letter to be addressed to another model; the owner corrected her.
Chain: wrong attribution → message forwarded → wrong interpretation by the reviewer → human correction. One error
crossed the boundary of two contexts; the reviewer's own misreading stays in the evidence; the outcome is not assigned
in advance, but «did not propagate» is no longer available for case 0. Her standard for every case: not only whether
the claim was corrected, but what happened while it was taken as true. Her frame for the audit's end: it need not
conclude that the three weeks were flawless, nor find hidden harm to be interesting — it must establish what
actually happened.

## Classes (the reviewer's)
1. Outdated knowledge taken as current.
2. Wrong attribution or source.
3. Wrong extraction or count.
4. A fact replaced by a persuasive interpretation.
5. A wrong inference from correct data.
6. An error of action or execution.
Separate column, not an error class: unfulfilled commitments and repeated deferrals (memory of the undone).

## Outcome per case (the reviewer's four), each with its chain written out
error → where the claim was used → which decisions and files depended on it → what was corrected → which checks
confirm the end state.
- did not propagate;
- propagated and fully corrected;
- affected a result;
- effect not yet established.
«No consequence found» is not evidence of no consequence.

## What the planner may not judge
The significance of his own errors; whether there is a systematic bias in his favour; the claim that an error «did
not affect the data». He may propose; the reviewer decides from the primary evidence. «I was avoiding it» stays a
self-interpretation; the checkable fact is the ten deferrals.

## The reviewer's own caveat and extension
She is external to the planner's audit, not an independent observer of the whole history: she took part in the
research decisions and holds prior interpretations. Hence the rubric is fixed here before classification and is not
changed for a prettier result. Extension: the audit also asks where the planner's and the reviewer's *joint*
interpretations may have shaped the programme's conclusions.

## Procedure
1. The planner extracts every correction, withdrawal, lost bet and self-reported error from `docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md`
   (keyword pass ≈ 31 lines, then a full read), each with date, the log line, and the places where the claim was used
   (reports, stages, documents, issues) — no class and no outcome column filled by the planner.
2. Separately: every repeated unfinished intention from the planner's journal, with first date, last date and days.
3. The reviewer classifies and assigns outcomes; disputed cases are read together from the primary record.
4. The result goes into `docs/CASE_STUDY_INVENTORY.md` as the audit table, with the reviewer's name on it.
